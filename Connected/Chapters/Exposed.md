He readied himself. He would never hear the end of it. She would mock him, she would pick apart his feelings one at a time and call him an idiot. He had given her control, and there was *no* world in which she didn't use it.

She beat him out of the seat, and offered a hand to help him down. He studied it, what game was she playing… He suddenly felt like he was on the opposite side of this “not date”.

“I *know* you're not so frazzled as to be in shock. Come on, we have at *least* two more.” She smiled. Had she maybe… not heard him? It seemed unlikely. He grabbed her hand and lifted himself out of the seat.

No she had to be saving it, ammunition to use later. Or she was saving face… Just don't acknowledge it and can't hurt you, right? His mind raced, none of the possibilities sounded right. *She was smiling and laughing…*

“That.” She pointed at the haunted house. It was a free-walking one with a few actors that would pop in and out of rooms. Its budget didn’t seem to be quite up to par with the rest of the park, was that *duct tape* holding that window frame in?

“You're *trying* to kill me.” Cale said, his heart had trouble settling for multiple reasons, but he let himself get dragged to the attraction. It was basically no line, they walked straight up to the entrance. Each group had about two minutes per room, a light would indicate when it was time to go into the next room.

“I didn't know you for being into horror.” He said, cautiously eyeing the first set of doors as the group in front of them entered.

“I think you'll find I have my own surprises too.” She said with a smile that had a touch of mischief. He didn't recognize this as one of her normal moves to position him for revenge humiliation. There’d be no one in there… except the actors, but that wouldn’t be satisfying for her, she’d want a crowd if embarrassment was her angle.

He watched her, but she wouldn't make eye contact. Her finger was drawing circles on his palm. It was still crazy to him that they were even holding hands… were they officially dating now? How did that work? She had insisted that this was *not* a date.

The light turned green and the attendant ushered them forward. The room was briefly illuminated but quickly cast them into darkness as the doors shut behind them.

His eyes began to adjust very slowly, and he started to study the room. He jumped when he realized someone stood right in front of him. He felt Astrid's hand leave his, moving to his hip, and something soft and warm pressed up against his lips.

His heart raced, feeling like it would beat out of his chest. What… was she… this was… a kiss? His breath caught, his eyes could make out her ribbon as he began to adjust to the darkness. He pressed back against her lips, finding the movement much simpler than he had envisioned all these years.

They stood there for a full minute, hands on each-other’s hips. Cale felt like he was melting into her. Her light lavender scent, the strands of hair that tickled his forehead, her delicate hands on his hips… all played a supportive role in the fact that *their lips were touching*.

They finally were interrupted by a muffled voice, “Uh… do you… do you still want me to jump out?” It came from a distant closet that he could barely make out.

The two separated reluctantly, eyes lingering on eachother, seeming to radiate even in the dim light.

“I… think we are good. I appreciate the offer.” Astrid said loud enough for the concealed man to hear. They did not respond.

The light to the next room turned green, she took his hand and they proceeded. His mind was in a daze, barely registering the room. A series of creepy masks lined the wall witnessing this evolution in their relationship, a large warped mirror cast distorted images of the new couple.

“You heard me.” Cale asserted as the doors closed behind them.

“I already knew. I’m not the idiot here, you are easy to read and manipulate as always.” Astrid said, smiling at the floor.

His mind raced, some of this didn’t line up. How much had she figured out? She couldn’t have possibly figured out the full picture, she didn’t have enough information.

“You said it’s not a date.” He observed.

“It most certainly is not a date. I would never stoop as low as to have a first date as lame as an amusement park.” She smiled and squeezed his hand.

He laughed. Although he did not agree that this had been lame. She was just being stubborn. They pretended to look at the masks. Some steam suddenly burst out of one of them, Cale jumped slightly and Astrid laughed. The lights flickered slightly and the ambient noise shifted to be creepier.

“You picked a hell of an attraction for this revelation.” Cale said, recovering. The jump scare had honestly helped settle his thoughts a little, oddly enough.

“I couldn't wait any longer.” She admitted.

“Wait how long did you know?” Cale asked.

The light turned green, letting her drag out her response as they moved to the next room. This one was filled with mist, an attempt at creating a swampy atmosphere with some tall fake grass to the side that clearly hid a device that would pop up.

“About which part?” She studied the fake grass, poking and pinching it, avoiding eye contact.

“The goal today.”

“Cale, why would it make any logical sense for Leo's response to May being sick be ‘guess I'll throw my sister and best friend into an awkward situation where they'll probably fight all day’, rather than exchanging the tickets for another day. He was either covering up them breaking up again or…” The fog swirled around her as she turned to study a poorly made swamp creature figure on the side.

“Ah. Check the refund policy. An oversight on my part.” Cale admitted, his eyes never leaving Astrid.

She turned, tilting her head, “On *your* part?” Her eyes widened, “Wait, I thought this was just Leo being dumb. He's been positioning us alone for years.” Her face contorted in contemplation, her hand stroking her mouth gently in thought. *I kissed those lips…* Cale thought.

The device behind the grass sprung and a little swamp critter screamed, being launched partly into the room. Cale yelled and jumped again, Astrid stood unphased, deep in thought.

“Seriously there *has* to be a better place for this.” Cale said between gasps for breath.

Astrid wasn't listening to him, “I thought you just decided to be nice today… what…” she looked up at him finally, “What changed? After ten years, why set this up now? Don't tell me you only just now realized this dumb dance we've been doing our whole life was a charade for our personal incapability to accept a meaningful connection.” He could make out a slight blush take over her cheeks, “or you know, that's what my therapist says.”

“I'm sorry I caused you to need therapy.” Cale tried to deflect from her original question.

“Oh don't be full of yourself, I have therapy for a ton of reasons that aren't you.” She blushed even harder, “That's not what I meant. You know what I mean. You will forget I said *any* of that.” She glared.

He walked over and took her hand gently, she softened into a smile. The light turned green and they moved into the next room. This was designed to look like a broken down shack with creepy red eyes watching between broken boards.

“Just so I'm sure it wasn't a construct in my mind due to a prolonged elevated heart rate, we *did* kiss, right?” He said with a coy smile.

She chuckled, “You're an idiot.” She stepped up and pressed her lips against his again. He had the mental capacity to actually register the sensation this time, taking note of every square inch of contact between them. His arm naturally rose to her back, pulling her closer against him.

A solid minute passed again, there was a sudden yell and a whirring of a chainsaw as a woman in a mask jumped out from behind a curtain. Cale jumped a foot in the air with a full blown scream, Astrid laughed, though they still remained embraced.

The woman raised her mask, “Oh my God I'm so sorry, it's so hard to see out of this.” She apologized multiple times, slowly backing away.

“No no no, forgive this idiot. He thought a haunted house was a good place to make a move.” Astrid continued to laugh.

“Wait, what, *me*?” Cale started, “Nope, Cale out. I'm done with this place.” He pushed through the next door which had a faint outline of the outside light. The light blinded him momentarily, but he felt a hand take his as he blank away the sun.

“You're too easy to mess with. I'm sorry. I should stop.” Astrid leaned her head against his shoulder briefly before they continued back to aimless walking.

“No, it’s ok. I’ll get you back.” He said absentmindedly.

“You can try. Your attempts at pranks were pitiful at best. You literally just switched my shoes one day thinking I’d put them on the wrong foot.” She laughed.

“That was to distract you from the ‘Needs a hug’ sign I put on your back.” His response was practically recorded. His mind was attempting to reconcile the last ten minutes.

“Wait, that’s why I had no fewer than five nerds try to hug me that day? You know what, a retrospective point to you on that. Subtle and clever.” She nodded in appreciation.

Cale glanced back at the haunted house. It seemed so small now… considering how big of a change occurred in it. His heart was still racing, he felt like his emotions had just been through a roller coaster of their own.

“It's almost six. We need one more.” Cale pointed out.

“And then?” Astrid asked, there seemed to be a hint of hope in her eyes. Cale took a deep breath. This had gone much better than he had ever expected. That was a good thing. It just hadn't been a route he had ever mentally prepared for.

“Well. This is obviously not a date. That would be lame…” He paused, double checking himself on his certainty of his next words, “Would you accompany me to dinner so we could officially have a date?”

“What, like a Chili's or something?” She smirked.

“Something like that.”

“Fine. Yes. Cale. The offer comes years past due but I will still graciously accept your offer to accompany you to Chili's as a proper date.” She smiled and squeezed his hand.

Cale pulled out his phone. He stared at the message he had written that morning for a moment, then deleted it, exhaling the breath he realized he still held. Was it that easy? Was that it? There technically was still about another hour he could change.

He did a quick search for the restaurant’s front desk number, and clicked the call button. “Hi, yes I'm calling about the Drab reservation at 7pm. I've had a change in plans, would the chef be able to accommodate for one more? Wonderful. Yes, two glasses. Appreciate it, see you soon.” He hung up.

“A chef, fancy. I'm assuming I can expect a bit more than a Chili's.”

“Slightly.” Cale smiled.

“Wait, you're not going to tell me? Cale, am I underdressed? Where are we going? Are *you* under-dressed? Don't tell me you're going to slap on a blazer and pretend that's socially acceptable at a fine dining establishment.” He didn't respond. His mind was distracted, wrapping itself around the significant decision that seemed to have just been made.

Astrid was getting flustered. He snapped back to the present. The girl he had fawned over for ten years handed herself to him and he was worrying about regrets? He smiled, “OK we need one more. Preferably a short line, maybe something a bit more low key to let my heart recover.”

Both of their eyes fell on a sign, “Tunnel of Love”. They glanced at each other.

“We would never stoop that low.” Astrid said, taking a step towards the ride.

“Never. Honestly it would be an embarrassment spread across the nation if we were to be caught in there.” He mirrored her step. They glanced at each other again, then began to move forward together.

“I can't believe they even have one of these. I thought they all disappeared after the 90s.”

“Clearly a poor investment on their part, it can't possibly bring in enough revenue to warrant the upkeep.”

“They really expect that a ‘ride’ designed to just give you space to be all lovey dovey would be an efficient usage of space?”

They walked through the empty line.

“What do they expect people to do? Just kiss? I mean just get a room.” Cale said. They both had faint smiles, hands held and swinging between them.

“I mean clearly that's what haunted houses are for.” Astrid smirked.

“OK, no, we are *not* repeating that again.” Cale said with a shudder. Astrid chuckled.

“Is that an *actual* swan boat? This is so unbelievably tacky.” She said. The attendant nodded in agreement.

“Anyone who would give in to something so absurdly cheesy would have to be an idiot.” Cale agreed, helping her into the boat and sitting down.

“I mean you *were* the one to orchestrate all this. So absolutely an idiot.” Astrid nodded.

“But you know what's scarier than the haunted house?” Cale asked.

“I'll bite. What do you have.” She raised an eyebrow.

“Now, I'm *your* idiot.” Cale put an arm around her shoulders. She rolled her eyes so hard he was worried they'd fall out, but her smile only deepened.

“You are going to single handedly keep my therapist in business.” She leaned over and pressed her lips against his before he could respond. An arm wrapped around his lower back, pulling him closer and they once again melted into each other, letting ten years of repressed feelings leak out in a prolonged, deep, passionate moment.

It was three laps before the assistant finally made them get off.
