**Leo**

Leo sat on the edge of their... his bed.

Boxes lined the room; the walls were bare. The faint scent of dust left Leo feeling like an intruder in someone else's home.

It would only be a few days before May would return... or he would run into her at an arcade.

Well, that was at least the standard pattern.

But this was all drifting well outside the established cycle.

Leo stood up, phone in hand falling limply to his side, and paced down the silent hall, eyes lingering on Cale's room, which had been empty for years. Only a thin sliver of light through closed curtains lit the space.

There had been discussions on and off about using the room for storage or inviting a fourth person from the community to help with the mortgage. But none of them could see the room as anything but Cale's.

There were two boxes for Cale. Just old game consoles that Leo was sure Cale would never touch again.

He passed Astrid's room, right at the top of the steps.

Her line of various plants, which Leo had kept watered, still lined the wall. But her books, paintings, the record player, her small collection of classical orchestra records, clothes, and remnants of college all sat in boxes near the front door.

He didn't need to look in the room to feel the presence of emptiness.

As he reached the bottom of the stairs, he averted his gaze from May's boxes.

He had tormented himself enough by sitting in the armchair, contemplating unpacking them and refusing to let her leave. He had been there for almost every one of those trophies, every collected piece of game merch.

The boxes upstairs were the most recent addition to the community of brown containers he had built.

They were his own.

There was no way he could afford the house; they had been barely scraping by when covering Cale's contributions for two years. With May, they might have made it work, but he couldn't plan around her anymore.

The hum of the refrigerator broke the silence as he entered the kitchen.

His eyes drifted through the room, preparing a game plan and calculating the supplies he would need.

His focus was interrupted by the sound of the front door creaking open, causing his head to turn.

A cautious Astrid poked her head in through the door, carrying an unwelcome glow.

Leo met her eyes through the wide connecting archway, and her face collapsed, shocked by whatever she saw on his own face.

He turned away, leaning forward on the counter and holding his phone up, though he didn't even register what was on the screen.

"Where's Cale?" He asked.

"Leo—" Her voice approached from behind, so he left the counter and moved away from the reaching hand that would otherwise have found his shoulder.

"Where's Cale?" he repeated, turning and leaning against the refrigerator.

Astrid's hand was, in fact, hovering mid-air where he had stood. Her hand slowly fell while her lips parted.

"Working. He will be by on the weekend to help move the furniture. He thinks you should stay with us. I can—"

Leo interrupted, "No. I'll be fine at Mom's."

"They are traveling; who knows when they'll return. You shouldn't be alone," Astrid said.

"Then you shouldn't have left."

The words expanded to fill the entire room, enveloping them in layers of unfelt pain.

He regretted saying them the second they left his mouth.

He looked down and realized his own arms were crossed in a distinctly Astrid manner. What was he doing? He unfolded them and attempted to soften his face, looking back up to his sister.

The damage had been done, though. Astrid's averted eyes and half-open mouth told a story of hurt.

Leo took a step forward. "Astrid, I'm sorry, I—"

"No. I understand, trust me." Astrid took a deep breath and closed the distance between the two.

Leo froze as he found her arms wrapped around him. He could feel her head shake lightly against his shoulder, though she remained silent.

His mind stuttered with input lag.

"I don't suppose you want me to say the words 'It will all be OK', do you?" Astrid asked, taking several steps back.

Her warmth lingered, but much of it was sucked into the cold vacuum of the house.

Leo shook his head. "I don't know. I want to hear it if it's true."

Leo strode to the living room and took a seat on the couch, in Cale's old spot.

Astrid followed him, finding her own place next to him.

"It will all be OK."

Her words escorted a deep breath into and out of Leo's lungs, providing the barest sliver of warmth.

"Are *you* OK?" Leo asked.

"Yes, why?" Astrid asked, though her eyes fell to the floor.

"You just used Zoey's deflection," Leo said.

Astrid shook her head and sighed.

"We have a lot to work through. You know that, Leo. Ten years of... whatever we were doesn't just... resolve," she said.

"Do you need help? I-" Leo started.

"Leo, one of us would have likely ended up dead without the effort you put in to soften our edges. You did your part. Cale and I need to figure this out ourselves."

"And if you don't?" Leo asked.

Astrid didn't respond.

Leo suspected the idea of "not working out" horrified both Cale and Astrid. Maybe he had been too harsh with that question.

"Sorry. I didn't mean it that way... May is in my head. She says Cale objectifies you."

He glanced up, watching for any uncertainty.

Astrid's eyes narrowed, glaring at the darkened TV.

After several moments of silence, she pulled out her phone and tapped several times. Leo was sure she was double-checking the definition of "objectification" just like he had.

"He hasn't... pressured you on anything, right? Everything is... consensual?" Leo asked cautiously.

"We still haven't done anything. Cale... waits for *me* to push boundaries. Every time. He avoided *showering* while I was in the condo until I had done so myself... It's respectful... I suppose."

Astrid was concerned that Cale was respecting her boundaries *too* much?

Did... Did Leo cause that?

A flare of pressure shot through Leo's head, causing him to close his eyes for a moment.

"That's not how he plays video games, for what it's worth," Leo noted.

"What?" Leo felt Astrid's gaze turn to him.

"Cale doesn't hold back. He uses *every* tool at his disposal to get what he wants. You've seen May break controllers over him spamming or zoning in fighters. The unwritten social rules of games don't matter to him. Winning does," Leo said.

"That... would explain a lot," Astrid said with a sigh. "You know he's a Director now? From my Corporate Governance and Fiduciary Duties classes, it sounded like the people who get ahead tend to be the ones who understand the rules and then exploit them or rebuild them."

Leo blinked.

It took a few moments to sink in.

"He's a... what the *fuck*. I wondered how he was covering that champagne. Mom accused him of being a drug lord."

The two drifted into silence.

Astrid stood up and walked over to the window, looking out into the back yard, then leaned forward on crossed arms. Leo shifted sideways on the couch, lying back with his head on the arm.

For a brief moment, Leo's mind slipped back to May. The house. The move.

He squeezed his eyes shut, attempting to push the thoughts out.

He had a problem he could actually *help* with here.

"There are topics he just... steers away from. Deflects. He knows *everyone*, did you know that?"

Leo opened his eyes and sat up, finding Astrid stroking her lips, still staring into the backyard.

"Everyone?"

Astrid shook her head. "In his apartment complex. At the restaurants, at the bars. Leo, there was a *betting pool* at the steakhouse we went to last night on when he would finally ask me out. People *love* him."

Images flashed through Leo's mind of high school, to a Cale who once took a full minute to prove to a bully that he knew how to tie his own shoes. A Cale that had caused the entire school to erupt into rumors and pointed fingers when he had joked about there being drugs in the teacher's lounge.

It had been coffee.

"What the fuck. *Cale*?"

Astrid nodded. "He goes quiet or deflects when I try to dig. It's the one subject that seems off limits... His success. His people skills. I don't know if he's trying to avoid arrogance or if he's hiding something."

"Maybe Zoey's right. Super spy."

Leo laughed.

Astrid didn't. She simply kept stroking her lips.

Cale was most definitely hiding something.

But Leo knew the impact that that reveal would have. Just the thought of it caused his heart to race with anxiety.

So he said nothing.

With an exhaled breath, Astrid turned back into the room. She looked around at the bare walls, the missing furniture, the imprints left in the carpeted floor.

"I'm sorry," she said. The floor creaked slightly as she began to pace the room slowly.

Leo tilted his head back against the couch, staring at the ceiling again. "Sorry? Why? For making a positive life change?"

"We left you alone, Leo. I know you-"

"I'm fine."

"Leo, you-"

"I'm fine. I have no option other than being fine." Leo rolled his head so his cheek rested against the leather, staring out the window that Astrid had just left.

Astrid was going to mention May now. Leo was sure May was pissed that he didn't talk to her first. And he was sure Astrid had been hearing about it every day at work.

Leo felt the couch sink as Astrid lowered herself next to him again. "May's pissed."

Yup.

Leo had this one ready.

"Does she want to find new housemates to split costs with?" Leo asked.

"No. She-"

"Are you or Cale going to move back in?" Leo asked.

"No. Leo, she-"

"Could we afford the house on our own?"

"Leo, stop," Astrid said.

Leo closed his eyes.

He was being an asshole, and he knew it. May just wanted him to talk to her first.

Astrid continued, "She just wanted you to talk to her first."

"I know."

He could never get it right. He was *always* wrong. The thought sent another surge of pressure through his head.

The sound of static from the TV and the whirring of a game console fan caused Leo's eyes to open. He found Astrid holding a controller, holding one out for him to take.

He accepted it and let out a sound that couldn't quite decide if it was a sigh or a laugh. "You hate video games."

The sound of hauntingly epic orchestral music filled the room, cutting through days of still air and stale silence. The controller felt somehow heavier, almost unfamiliar.

"Just because I don't cuss out underage brats online doesn't mean I didn't enjoy the time playing." Astrid flipped the cursor to a co-op mode and hit start.

Astrid's phone buzzed right before they began.

After a quick read, she added, "Cale's coming over. He took the rest of the day off to help."

"He doesn't—" Leo started.

Astrid looked over sharply. "Let him help, Leo. He and I are working things out, but he's still Cale. I spent a decade pushing away the very person who was positioned to make my life better. Don't pick that mantle up."

Leo sighed and started up the co-op campaign; it was an older shooter. He and Astrid had played it through when they were much younger.

Energetic rock music and fantastical alien blasting to fight off a less rational reality.

"Does May *want* to talk yet?"

"I caught her lost in her head while staring into a Frappuccino in the blender at work today. She then demanded to help move anything remaining into Mom's."

How many times could they do this?

Would things be different without Astrid around?

It felt like they were slowly running out of lives, and the idea of a game-over screen horrified him.

The empty echoes of cackling from May calling someone online a "bottle-drinking ass-wipe" seemed to accompany the sound of an exploding grenade.

"I miss her. If she asks," Leo said.

"I'm sure she will."

Leo squeezed his eyes shut, trying - and failing - to clear his head. It pulsed.

Astrid leaned her head against his shoulder.

He opened his eyes again, and fingers found the familiar rhythm. The sounds of plasma and explosions never quite reached his heart.

Astrid was right, though. He and May would be OK. They always were.

But... moving back in with his parents. While they were out traveling.

Leo would be alone.

Fuck.

Something dripped off Leo's chin and onto his arm. He looked down at the point of impact, but found nothing. He squeezed his eyes shut but felt no moisture.

His face remained dry.

What would tears solve, anyway?
