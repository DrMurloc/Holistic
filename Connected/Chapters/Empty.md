**Leo**

Leo sat on the edge of their... his bed.

Boxes lined the room; the walls were bare. The faint scent of dust left Leo feeling like an intruder in someone else's home.

Leo had already begun packing Astrid's belongings in the first half of the week; it had kept him busy.

It would only be a few days before May would return... or he would run into her at an arcade.

Well, that was at least the standard pattern.

The text he got instead had been from Astrid.

It turned out that more than her belongings needed packing.

Leo stood up and paced down the silent hall, eyes lingering on Cale's room, which had been empty for years. Only a thin sliver of light through closed curtains lit the space.

There had been discussions on and off about using the room for storage or inviting a fourth person from the community to help with the mortgage. But none of them could see the room as anything but Cale's.

There were two boxes for Cale. Just old game consoles that Leo was sure Cale would never touch again.

He passed Astrid's room, right at the top of the steps.

Her line of various plants, which Leo had kept watered, still lined the wall. But her books, paintings, the record player, her small collection of classical orchestra records, clothes, and remnants of college all sat in boxes near the front door.

He didn't need to look in the room to feel the presence of emptiness.

As he reached the bottom of the stairs, he averted his gaze from May's boxes.

He had tormented himself enough by sitting in the armchair, contemplating unpacking them and refusing to let her leave. He had been there for almost every one of those trophies, every collected piece of game merch.

There had been no tears, of course. What would they solve?

The boxes upstairs were the most recent addition to the community of brown containers he had built.

They were his own.

There was no way he could afford the house; they had been barely scraping by when covering Cale's contributions for two years. With May, they might have made it work, but he couldn't plan around her anymore.

The idea of finding new housemates filled him with dread. It wouldn't feel right, and he didn't have the mental bandwidth.

The hum of the refrigerator broke the silence as he entered the kitchen.

His eyes drifted through the room, preparing a game plan and calculating the supplies he would need.

His focus was interrupted by the sound of the front door creaking open, causing his head to turn.

A cautious Astrid poked her head in through the door, carrying an unwelcome glow.

Leo met her eyes through the wide connecting archway, and her face collapsed, shocked by whatever she saw on his own face.

He turned away, leaning forward on the counter. "Where's Cale?"

He had no interest in any of the words of sympathy that he knew Astrid carried. They couldn't solve anything.

"Leo—" Her voice approached from behind, so he left the counter and moved away from the reaching hand that would otherwise have found his shoulder.

"Where's Cale?" he repeated, turning and leaning against the refrigerator.

Astrid's hand was, in fact, hovering mid-air where he had stood. Her hand slowly fell while her lips parted.

"Working. He will be by on the weekend to help move the furniture. He thinks you should stay with us. I can—"

"No. I'll be fine at Mom's."

"They are traveling; who knows when they'll return. You shouldn't be alone."

"Then you shouldn't have left."

The words expanded to fill the entire room, enveloping them in layers of unfelt pain.

He regretted saying them the second they left his mouth.

He looked down and realized his own arms were crossed in a distinctly Astrid manner. What was he doing? He unfolded them and attempted to soften his face, looking back up to his sister.

The damage had been done, though. Astrid's averted eyes and half-open mouth told a story of hurt.

Leo took a step forward. "Astrid, I'm sorry, I—"

"No. I understand, trust me." Astrid took a deep breath and closed the distance between the two.

Leo froze as he found her arms wrapped around him. He could feel her head shake lightly against his shoulder, though she remained silent.

His mind stuttered with input lag; this was a new interaction that he had not prepared for.

Her support for Leo had always been deep and profound, but it was verbal. Logical, as if she could rationalize and wrangle the emotions with cold, hard facts.

But this...

It was warm... affectionate.

For reasons Leo couldn't logically rationalize, he relaxed, even if only slightly.

Astrid was soothing emotions with emotions, as if suddenly less afraid of wielding her own. What in the hell had Cale accomplished in a mere week?

"I don't suppose you want me to say the words 'It will all be OK', do you?" Astrid asked, taking several steps back.

Her warmth lingered, but much of it was sucked into the cold vacuum of the house.

Leo shook his head. "I don't know. I want to hear it if it's true."

Leo strode to the living room and took a seat on the couch, in Cale's old spot.

Astrid followed him, finding her own place next to him.

"It will all be OK."

The words escorted a deep breath into and out of Leo's lungs, providing the barest sliver of warmth.

He felt them emotionally, but logically...

"What if this is the end? Astrid... I... It's May. How the hell do I live without her? How would you have felt if Cale had left?"

The answer came almost too fast. "I would have shattered, spending months determining which pieces were even worth picking back up."

Leo's head turned to find her staring distantly beyond the TV, like she was reliving something traumatic. The way she had responded, almost poetically: that reality was something she had very clearly considered.

She did understand.

Leo knew she would; she always had understood others' emotions well, almost better than her own, he suspected.

Astrid reached for a controller and turned on the last remaining gaming console.

Leo let out a sound that couldn't quite decide if it was a sigh or a laugh. "Astrid, you hate games."

The sound of hauntingly epic orchestral music filled the room, cutting through days of still air and stale silence. The controller felt somehow heavier, almost unfamiliar.

"Just because I don't cuss out underage brats online doesn't mean I didn't enjoy the time playing." Astrid flipped the cursor to a co-op mode and hit start.

Leo could practically hear May calling Cale or a nasally preteen online an "ass-wipe".

Astrid's phone buzzed right before they began.

After a quick read, she added, "Cale's coming over. He took the rest of the day off to help."

"He doesn't—" Leo started.

Astrid looked over sharply. "Let him help, Leo. I spent a decade pushing away the very person who was best positioned to make my life better. Don't you pick that mantle up."

Leo sighed and started up the co-op campaign; it was an older shooter. Astrid and he had played it through when they were much younger.

Energetic rock music and fantastical alien blasting to fight off a less rational reality.

He couldn't see the path that led him and May back to being in sync. They were missing something obvious; they *had* to be. Other people made this work, people less equipped than they were, right?

But it felt like they were slowly running out of lives, and the idea of a game-over screen horrified him.

He loved her.

"You really think we'll be fine?" Leo asked.

"Leo, when you two are together, you are the happiest people I know."

How could Leo explain, though...

It was one thing when they could keep their arguments contained to specific periods, but the "silent" arguments had become more frequent. They were piling up, and Leo had no way to resolve any of them.

They were getting worse. Not better.

And it was his fault.

Leo squeezed his eyes shut, trying - and failing - to clear his head.

Astrid leaned her head against his shoulder.

His hands found the familiar rhythm of the game, though the sounds of plasma and explosions never reached his heart.

Something dripped off Leo's chin and onto his arm. He looked down at the point of impact, but found nothing. He squeezed his eyes shut but felt no moisture.

His face remained dry.

What would tears solve, anyway?
