**Uni**

Before we get too far in the weeds with Asuna and Hawthorn, I need to double-check on Lara. Lara has a tendency to... well, "wander", as it were.

One minute, everything is going fine, sometimes even Naina and Zoey have figured themselves out by this point. Suddenly, *bam*, Lara's in New Zealand... or Bali, or Vietnam.

I once found her in Antarctica.

*Antarctica!*

I still, to this day, have no idea how a journey "to find yourself" leads you to an isolated, even if cozy, research station in a frozen tundra.

Who am I to judge, though? "Journey before destination" or something, I suppose.

Oh, fantastic, she's still in Japan.

Quick stop. A few hundred words, max.

**Lara**

Lara wandered.

She stood on a small arching bridge across a lightly burbling brook in a perfectly crafted garden. Her phone rose, lining up the perfect shot of a large bonsai tree that sat on an island trapped in the middle of a pond.

She stood on her tiptoes, leaning forward with elbows stabilized against a well-aged wooden railing. The tip of her tongue peeked out between her lips in concentration.

The occasional cherry blossom would drift in front of the camera, so she waited for...

Just...

The right...

Moment...

*Click.*

With the picture taken, the phone went away, and she continued down the path through the garden.

Each step fell with an airy grace, like gravity was merely a concept she entertained but didn't need to follow. Her bushy hair swayed behind her as eager, amber eyes took in every possible sight.

The hair was tied up in a ponytail, but it spilled out over her back, unable to be contained. Mist-grey button-up and kelp green travel trousers billowed with every motion as if sails. They were one of only five outfits she kept in her small, travel-ready suitcase.

She ducked to the side to let several kids run by. They yelled something; she wasn't quite sure if it was Japanese or English, but she understood the positive energy they carried.

She grinned as her head turned to follow their path.

The small, watery garden gave way to a winding path through rolling, bright-green fields. The fields were spotted with grand trees, each with its own unique shade of white, red, or pink.

The trees were surrounded by the buzzing of activity.

Children played, students studied, and couples sat, embraced in their own pockets of happiness. What appeared to be a wedding party stood under one particularly grand tree, framed by bowing branches.

Her gaze landed on a small vendor kiosk advertising a variety of drinks and small bites, reminding her of her slightly dry throat.

She scurried up, with a slight bow, and gave her best Japanese.

"Hello!"

The attendant, an older woman, said something she didn't understand. Lara smiled and pointed to the picture of a bottle of water.

"Please."

The attendant, yet again, said something she didn't understand, slowly turning down into a cooler to retrieve the requested bottle.

"Thank you!" Lara said as she placed the yen on the table, accepting the water bottle with another slight bow.

She almost bumped into someone as she turned to resume her wandering, and gave a quick, "Sorry!" before dodging around them and continuing on down the path.

And there they were.

The only four words you ever needed, in *any* country.

So many people got caught up in language, the need to vocally communicate every piece of nuance, when in reality, the more words you used, the messier it made things.

Out here?

Navigating the open and free oceans of the *real* world?

You didn't need any of that.

It was *great;* she was *free*.

Lara wandered into a small gazebo on a slightly elevated hill. Her feet crunched lightly against the stone path. Her hand fell lightly onto the rough wood bannister as she stepped up into the structure.

Various gardens of the park stretched out in all directions from this central location. The trees and colors surrounding the small structure felt meticulously placed to embody the term 'picturesque'; she gleefully took photo after photo.

But something stood out against the joyful pastel pinks and greens, causing her head to tilt.

Show lowered her phone.

Sitting on the bench on the opposite side of the gazebo was a boy in a black shirt with messy black hair. His head rested against a hand, eyes on the ground.

Did she recognize him?

He looked familiar, but she couldn't quite place where from. Maybe work? She didn't really know any of the other stagehands who worked for Miss Morita, not well enough to recognize them.

His face was *not* joyful, though... those eyes were vacant and distant in all the worst ways. It felt like a thorny tangle of black stains against an otherwise bright and cheerful portrait.

What was going on in his head? Had he been rejected? Abandoned? Was he escaping from something? Or remembering something?

It didn't matter why, she concluded.

She walked up and tapped on his shoulder with a smile. He looked up, seeming to recognize her, too.

Their eyes met.

Her heart fluttered for a moment, and her smile flickered to parted lips.

For a moment, it was as if the cherry blossoms themselves fluttered through her stomach.

She had never been one for crushes; the idea of growing attached to someone else was entirely counterintuitive for her entire current life direction.

She didn't need anchors; she needed *sails.*

She pushed the flutters away, returning to her original goal.

Her hands extended out with a broad smile and raised thumbs in a silent moment of support.

**Sho**

Sho blinked.

It was the quiet girl from work.

Her hands were held out? What was she doing?

The demon lord's army retreated around her, clearing his mind, even if slightly, for the first time in what felt like an eternity.

Sho stood, not quite sure what was expected of him.

He had never seen this moment in any Anime. Had heaven sent her to fight the hollows? A dragon in disguise to bring him joy? A goddess to grant him some blessing and whisk him to another world?

He looked at her outstretched hands.

In any case, she seemed to want a hug?

Sho could do a hug, he guessed. Touching a girl felt weird... but... if it was what she wanted?

Sho stepped forward, and his hands rose slowly.

Was he supposed to place it on her hips? That felt too intimate. Her back? Her-

The warmth of the girl as he embraced her cut off any further thoughts.

She tensed. Was he doing it wrong?

**Lara**

Oh.

Not quite what she had in mind.

But she could roll with it.

They exchanged warmth, and the muscles in his back noticeably softened as his hands rose to her back.

She held the hug while the thorny mess of tangles in him softened.

His hands almost imperceptibly latched onto her waist as she pulled away.

She took several steps back, satisfied that his frown had shifted to a smile. She flashed him her own wide smile and turned away to resume her path toward finding herself.

No, speech was *not* important in communication; it might, in fact, be the *least* important part.

Why speak when you can just smile?
