Tenacity.

It's a powerful word. Less used than the word "determination", slightly more clinical than "hard-headedness", and more universally understood than "implacable". I would, in fact, go as far as to describe human beings, collectively, as "tenacious".

My little collective of mostly-hairless bipedals are most certainly “tenacious”, though Cale, sitting on a couch overlapping pinkies with his ten-year obsession while *still* not managing to muster the words "would you like to get coffee?" had him giving "implacable" a run for its money.

It's not quite the best descriptor of "humanity", however.

The word I would personally lean towards is "isolated".

On its own, it's a neutral word.

In practice, it's a nuanced word.

In the context of a romance novel, it's practically a curse word, unless tragedy is involved. Said tragedy typically comes with quite a bit of brooding and overthinking. The message in such books is often clear: "feel, don't think".

Said "brooding and overthinking" is, coincidentally, often associated with a particular organization of neurons that could be described as "isolated", even if extensively developed. Trying to tell someone with this neurological blueprint to "feel, don't think" would likely make them furrow their brows, then spend the next ten years of their life trying to categorize every physiological reaction they experience to map it to something they "feel".

Astrid, on the receiving end of the aforementioned pinky-touching and pushing the limits on her own form of “implacability”, would most certainly give you a glare that brought you several steps closer to your grave if you gave her an instruction like “feel, don’t think.” This would be followed by a considerable amount of brooding, none of which would result in the thought, “hey, maybe he doesn’t need to be the one to ask me out for coffee”.
