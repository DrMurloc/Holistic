**Sho**

"What, Mimi and that American?" Ren exclaimed.

The four of them: Ren, Haruki, Sho, and the bushy-haired girl, sat in a line of seats before Miss Morita's office door. The office space sprawled behind them with interconnected desks, otherwise empty for the evening.

Sho was *pretty* confident that Ren was at fault for the leak.

"There's no way. You're making that up. That's what he was asking about?" Ren continued.

The biggest hint had been when Ren bragged to Sho hours after the event, saying, "I just posted about Mimi and that American kissing, I'm going to get so many followers."

Sho, of course, didn't bother saying anything.

It wasn't worth the effort.

Haruki chimed in, "Ren, I was right there with you; we both saw it. The girl was there too." He nodded towards the bushy-haired girl, who was buried in her phone.

Haruki had it covered, anyway.

"Think we can blame her?" Ren asked.

Sho glared at Ren, finding words worth his time. "Ren, don't."

"Sho can talk?" Ren laughed. "How do you know she *didn't* post anything? Prove it."

Of course, you can't prove a negative, but explaining that to Ren just wasn't worth it.

"You know, the more you talk like that, the more attention you're going to call to yourself," Haruki noted.

"I don't know what you're talking about!" Ren said with a laugh.

"Ren, shut up. You're going to get us all in trouble."

This entire situation was embarrassing.

Sho felt like they were children sitting in the principal's office. The last time Sho checked, at around twenty years old, they were adults. They were meant to be better than this, right?

The door opened, revealing the red-dyed hair of Kenji. Sora exited, shoulders trembling as if he had just been tortured.

"You all are free to go. Thank you for your time. We'll see you at the next show." Kenji bowed.

Sho stood and returned the bow, though none of the others did.

"What? No one's getting fired?" Ren asked. Haruki shot him a glare.

Kenji calmly explained. "Miss Morita was quite explicit in that no one was to be punished, even if we *had* found out who spread the rumors."

Their manager's eyes narrowed at Ren. It was the closest to "angry" that Sho had ever seen the typically flustered man.

"Do we get paid for today?" Ren asked.

Sho sighed and took several steps towards the exit on the other side of the mostly empty office space. Hopefully, the motion would indicate to the others that it was time to go.

"Ren!"

Haruki did, in fact, follow suit. He lifted Ren by the shoulder and urged Sora forward.

"Yes, it'll reflect on your next paycheck," Kenji said with his own sigh, turning back into his office and shutting the door with finality.

The girl's eyes continued to bounce from speaker to speaker, wide eyes and parted lips as if absorbing every detail of what she couldn't understand.

Her gaze landed on Sho as Haruki dragged the other two past and toward the distant door.

Sho gave the girl two thumbs up and an overexaggerated smile.

The girl hopped to her feet with an enthusiasm that Sho could only describe as "cute", then fell in step beside him towards the exit.

The silence that traveled with them was thick with unspoken words, only disrupted by the distant exchange of ramblings between Ren and Haruki. The office's mostly bare state—empty desks and boxed supplies—only added to the pressure.

He needed to say something, though, right?

It would be rude not to?

But what was the point of saying something if she wouldn't understand?

What strange paradox.

What would he even say, "Hi, thank you for spontaneously lifting me out of the darkest moment of my life in the cherry blossom garden. That memory has been the only thing keeping me sane when alone in a house haunted by my parents."

Didn't quite fit... what would that Anime arc even look like? Would he find out she's some demon hunter from another world and was here to recruit him?

Sho tried to picture the strange Anime's title, "I Couldn't Find My Place in This World, So a Cute Girl Kidnapped Me to Another, and Somehow I'm Thriving?"

He liked the idea of that Anime.

As the two emerged onto the lazy backstreet of Shinjuku, they lingered. Puddles from an earlier rain reflected distant neon lights and digital billboards.

It was an awkward lingering.

Sho wasn't really good at lingering in the first place, and he was quickly finding that he wasn't a fan of the base concept.

They weren't quite looking at each other, which felt more telling than if they had stared one another directly in the eyes.

He should just... But what?

"Come on!" Haruki yelled back from down the block.

The girl turned to leave in the opposite direction.

"No! No!" Sho exclaimed, causing her to turn back.

He gestured towards the others and said a word he desperately hoped she understood, "Ramen."

She did, in fact, understand it and *beamed*.

Those smiles were going to be dangerous. They were addictive.

They began their pace down the street, making no attempt to fully catch up with the others.

"I'm Sho."

Her head turned as if seeing him would help her understand the words.

Not even something *that* basic?

This was going to be a lot of effort. Was she worth it?

He most definitely wanted more of those smiles.

There had to be some way to communicate...

Wait, of course.

Sho pulled out his phone and opened a translator app.

He typed out: *"Hello. I'm Sho. I'm happy to meet you."*

It translated into English, and he stared at the words for a moment. Somehow, this felt more natural than speaking. He could just type the words and focus on *what* he was saying, not *how*.

It was... efficient.

Huh.

He shrugged the thought off as he passed the phone over. It was received with that cute enthusiasm.

The girl switched the target language to Spanish.

"Sho." She said the name aloud as if trying it on for size, then typed back.

Her finger taps were rapid.

*"I'm Lara. Thank you for inviting me to ramen. It's been months since I last ate alone."*

Sho assumed the translator meant "didn't eat alone". This was likely to have some speed bumps, but they could work with it.

He tried her name out. With his accent, it came out as "Rara".

They both laughed, and he typed out, *"Do you have a gamer tag?"*

She typed back, *"Like computer game name?"*

Sho nodded, so Lara typed, *"Wander. But spelled VVander."*

"Wandaa." Sho tried the game tag out loud. It got another laugh from her. He liked her laugh; it was airy and light.

Sho pointed at himself and said, "NekoJoy. Or... Neko."

Lara cocked her head. "Neko? El gato?"

Sho shook his head, not understanding.

Lara's hands raised to her head, and she made cat ears and emitted a mewing noise.

Now, Sho had not talked to many girls in his life.

The scripts he had in his head on how those interactions would go typically filled him with anxiety. The social expectations regarding how boys and girls interacted were quite strict.

When he was already focused so hard on making sure words formed correctly, the idea of subjecting himself to a second layer of nuance just... never seemed appealing.

But here he stood, in front of the first girl he had ever talked... well... communicated with beyond a simple passing pleasantry...

And she was acting like a cat.

In public.

Social expectations be damned.

Sho grinned and nodded, raising his own hands to mirror the posture, feeling a touch of warmth on his cheeks at making such a childish pose.

Sho nodded eagerly. "Cat! Yes! Gato? Gato."

Lara *beamed*, *thrilled* to have known the Japanese word for cat.

"Sho! Are you and your girlfriend coming?" Ren yelled back as the other two entered the ramen shop at the end of the current block.

What? Girlfriend...

Said cheek warmth expanded, encompassing Sho's entire face and neck as he realized his hands were still up, acting as ears.

Sho's hands dropped, and he stumbled slightly.

How does someone who *doesn't* have a crush walk? Was he walking too quick? Should he put his hands in his pockets? Should he raise them? No, that wasn't right.

Lara either didn't notice the fluster or didn't care, and they both scurried to catch up.

"Welcome in!" The staff exclaimed as Sho was greeted by the salty smells of garlic and soy.

Sho bowed in respect, although he suspected none of his companions had.

Lara studied him, mouth open, then copied the movement, nearly perfectly.

She was learning, with no shame.

It was so rare to see that; most people were self-conscious about what they didn't know. She just... cared more about learning than her own image? It was relieving in a culture built on social perception, where fitting into the norm was a priority over all else.

The observation made Sho laugh, and he immediately had to cover his mouth.

"Sorry! Sorry." He stated as they sat at the table next to the boys.

Lara looked confused, so he typed out on the phone, *"I should not have laughed. You did it right. I apologise."*

She shook her head and smiled as if he hadn't just been absurdly rude.

Ren, naturally, began to run his mouth about Mimi. To Sho's disappointment, Sora and Haruki joined in on the gossip.

Lara pushed her phone over, catching Sho's attention.

*"I hope you feel better."*

He considered, then typed, *"You are a very kind person. It helped a lot. Thank you."*

She read the message, then looked up at him. Sho realized she was trying to read his body language, so he gave a broad smile that mirrored the joy she had brought him.

Lara seemed satisfied, turning back to the phone and typing, *"I'm glad. What was wrong? If it is ok to ask."*

Sho's mind flickered through a series of potential answers.

He didn't want to burden her with the full details.

He settled on typing out, *"It was my first time visiting Shinjuku Gardens for the cherry blossoms alone."*

Lara read the message several times, then turned her gaze back to Sho, lips parted in concern as she tried to read him for further clues.

He offered none.

Lara typed back, *"I'm sorry."*

Apparently, the lack of reaction had been a reaction in itself.

He shook his head, typing, *"I appreciate your concern. I'm ok, though. Where are you from?"*

Her lips twisted in skepticism, but she accepted the deflection and typed back, "*Santo Domingo. Dominican Republic. My parents live in the US now, though. I don't really live anywhere."*

Sho did some quick searching on the country.

It looked beautiful... Turquoise water... Jungly cliffs... waterfalls... Even the cities had a distinct charm.

Why would someone leave *that* and come to some place as bland as Tokyo? Even the US seemed preferable, depending on the state, from what he had heard.

He typed back, *"Did you like Santo Domingo?"*

Lara's eyes went wide with enthusiasm at the question. She typed furiously for almost half a minute, the glow of her phone reflected in focused amber eyes.

She paused and appeared to re-edit a few messages before passing the phone back.

*"It was beautiful. The cultural differences are fascinating. They value expression and personal relationships, like the US. Japan is focused on collectivism and an aversion to conflict. It's really cool."*

Sho nodded slowly, typing, *"It's difficult. Everyone has to put effort into making sure we stay within boundaries."*

"Sho! Hey Sho!" Ren's voice called out.

Sho continued to type, *"And respect is everything. Especially for elders."*

"Sho! Don't you agree that Miss Morita is kind of a bitch?"

*"And finding the balance on how to communicate without being too intrusive is always difficult."*

"Sho! Tell us about your girlfriend!"

Sho pushed the phone over for Lara to read, but didn't quite hear the others.

"Sho, what's her deal?" Haruki chimed in.

Sho took in Lara's inquisitive and determined gaze as she read.

His eyes quickly averted.

This girl clearly had gotten into Sho's head. Much more than he had initially thought.

"Helloooo! Sho! Space cadet, radio back! Are you two on a date?" Ren laughed.

This *was* a crush, right?

He suspected that's what those stomach flutters were.

She was charming... Sho was charmed.

"I don't think Sho's dating her," Sora stated.

But what could he do about that? She didn't even speak the same language.

"Of course he is! Did you see that smile? Sho doesn't smile."

It would be imposing for him to ask her out, given the context. "Hey, we literally can't communicate, but would you go out on a date where we spend the entire evening studying each other's body language between mistranslated messages?"

"Sho smiles, don't make stuff up, Ren." Haruki sighed.

No, he couldn't do that to her.

He didn't have anything to offer.

He was just Sho.

With no direction, no real-life skills, and a meager-at-best inheritance, he was one minor contract job away from being classified as a NEET.

"Sho?" Sora leaned forward in an attempt to get Sho's attention.

Sho shook his head, eyes turning back up to the others.

"The girl. What's her deal?"

Sho's mind rewound and replayed through the past minute of the other's chatter, internally sighing at more than a few statements.

"She's from the Dominican Republic. Speaks Spanish."

Lara looked up at hearing "Dominican".

"Why's she here?" Haruki asked.

That's a good question.

Sho typed out, *"They're curious about what brings you to Tokyo. You probably know, but foreigners are rare beyond tourists."*

While Lara typed back, Sho filled out the small paper with his order: tonkotsu miso with five-level spice. He exchanged the paper for the phone with Lara.

*"I just travel. I spent my whole life with parents fighting over me. It was the only thing I was. One has answers I don't want, and the other offers none. So until I know who I am, I travel."*

Sho had to read it several times. His brow furrowed, attempting to picture such a... homelessness?

Where were they in the story?

Typically, you didn't find out about the female lead's magical backstory until the end of the first episode. Sho supposed he had asked, but he had expected a response that was... more vague?

It had likely been rude of him to not elaborate on his own backstory.

Or... was *he* the one with a hidden past who would escort *her* onto a magical journey?

No, that-

A sudden tapping on his hand snapped him out of the thought.

It was Lara.

She was pointing at the spice level with that cute questioning look: head tilted, lips parted, eyes widened.

He typed out, *"Do you like spicy?"*

She shook her head, then paused, holding up two fingers to indicate "a little".

Sho held up two fingers.

Lara nodded with a broad grin and finished filling out the paper. It was collected shortly after.

"So?" Sora asked. Sho realized the other three were studying him intently, still waiting for an answer.

How much should he say?

It felt like revealing "parent issues" to the others was not wise.

"Lara just likes to travel," Sho stated.

"Come on, Sho! We need more than that," Ren stated.

"Ren, Sho isn't going to give you more to gossip about. You know it. Ask her yourself if you're so desperate," Haruki stated.

"What, talk to a foreigner? You hear the stories of what happens when you talk to foreigners in Shinjuku."

"You know for a fact that those are a different kind of foreigner, Ren. You also don't talk to the girls in Shinjuku." Haruki laughed.

Sho was already busy typing. *"Where have you travelled to? I've never left Japan."*

Lara contemplated the phone for a moment, then pulled up YouTube and handed it back.

Sho skimmed the open channel; it was her own.

There were quite a few videos.

Sho couldn't translate the titles, but they looked to be travel vlogs of sorts. They had a considerable number of views, considering the videos were in Spanish.

"She's a YouTuber," Sho offered, hoping it would satiate the others.

"What? An influencer? Are we about to get offered a million dollars to play a game? I want a free car." Ren leaned over to look at the phone.

Between the titles and the images, Sho was able to identify a series of cities: Montreal, Washington DC, Rome, Paris, Istanbul, and then Tokyo. He did some quick mental math on the spacing of the videos and determined that she would spend about two months in each location.

"You think a rich influencer would be working as a stagehand for Mimi Galaxy?"

"It could be an undercover thing, you don't know."

Sho had been seeing her for about a month now? By his estimates, that meant she was about halfway through her trip in Japan. The idea of her leaving hurt.

How could someone have such a profound impact on him, then simply... vanish?

"I don't think thousands of views get you the money you need for doing million-dollar games, Ren."

"Only thousands? Oh, she's just a wannabe."

Sho shot Ren a glare and handed the phone back to Lara while the ramen was dropped off. The air filled with the warmth of savory umami. Sho breathed in the aroma, soaking in the comforting whispers of garlic and soy.

"Let's eat!" The four boys said at the same time.

Lara cocked her head at the statement and attempted to repeat, "Itakdamas?"

Sho grinned and typed out the word in romaji on his phone, repeating each syllable.

"Let's eat!" Lara repeated with enthusiasm, earning applause from the table.

She was legitimately proud of the small win.

The butterflies in Sho's stomach developed their own butterflies.

Or maybe that was hunger.

As they ate, Sho could make out Lara watching everyone else. She mimicked the way they ate the ramen, even finding the right level of slurp to give, something Sho knew foreigners struggled with.

Her favorite seemed to quickly become the "mini-ramen", which was Sora's preferred style. Lara would pack pieces of ramen into her renge spoon, piling on just the right amount of vegetables and meat, then eat it in one bite.

The entire experience was intoxicating.

For gamers like Sho, it wasn't about being good; it was about the art of getting good. The meta game of being able to pick up and master rules or mechanics on a dime. It was what distinguished "the greatest" gamers from "good" gamers: "learning how to learn".

This girl clearly understood how she learned; she was an observational learner, and dove into it headfirst with an appetite.

Her head turned up from her ramen, and suddenly Sho found himself lost in pools of amber.

Neither moved.

He should look away, right? That's how it worked in Anime. They had a whole three seasons before they were allowed to acknowledge feelings in one another.

He didn't want to look away.

He wanted to milk this moment: the way his chest felt like it might implode, the hairs on his arms that stretched out from nerves that were on high alert, the way his breath felt forbidden.

Ren had different ideas. "Oh my god, I think Sho actually has a crush."

Sho's head snapped over. "What?"

"Uh, Sho, you were in fact just staring into one another's eyes," Haruki noted.

Sho waved his arms in a cross, half for them, half to Lara. "No! I just got lost in my thoughts."

He bowed towards Lara repeatedly. "I'm sorry! I'm sorry!"

She cocked her head in confusion, so Sho typed, *"Ignore them. They are being dumb. I'm sorry."*

As she returned to her ramen, Sho noted more than one stolen glance in his direction, likely to check if he continued to stare.

It *had* been rude.

The remainder of the dinner was finished in mostly silence; Sho couldn't help but think that he had ruined the mood.

As the three others dismissed themselves into the early evening of the city, Lara and Sho once again lingered.

It was, once again, an awkward lingering.

The kind of lingering that told a million stories without a single spoken word.

Maybe he *should* ask her out?

But the right length of "linger" had passed for such a thing, and it didn't feel appropriate to do over the phone. At this level of lingering intensity, the most Sho could do would be to keep the night going.

Lara passed her phone to Sho, *"Are there arcades nearby? I need pictures of an arcade."*

A truck passed on the narrow street, causing Sho to briefly look up before returning to the impossible message.

She had just invited herself into his own magical world.

And she had no clue.

His mind flipped through a catalog of arcades; he knew all of them. He would want one that was quieter... but in a safe location... Ideally opened later...

Many arcades would just be crane or gacha games...

His mind picked up the adorable tilt of Lara's head as he churned; it was hard to tune out.

He finally grinned and nodded, gesturing down the street and leading with a steady pace.

Not too eager, don't come off as excited...

It was hard not to.

Lara took picture after picture of buildings and shops they passed while they walked.

Sho noticed how she avoided capturing people in her pictures. It was a restraint that not many foreigners showed. Yet another notch in the rapidly building list of observations that kept Sho's veins thrumming.

Lara continued to take pictures as they entered the arcade, met by a city-like maze of UFO catchers and gacha machines.

As hoped, it was simply several couples on dates and foreigners.

The fourth floor was exclusively rhythm games, and to Sho's relief, it was empty.

Lara's eyes went wide, and she ran forward, picking up the guitar on the Guitar Fever machine and testing its buttons.

Sho was left shocked.

Now, it wasn't far-fetched that a westerner would have experience with one of the guitar-based games. The entire world had been swept up in a craze for this subgenre years earlier.

But the image of specifically Lara, the girl who was slowly teaching Sho the definition of "enraptured", silhouetted against the neon glow of a game which was physically attached at the hip to Sho's personal escape from reality, while looking back over her shoulder with widened eyes of invitation and hope, well...

The way Sho's mind did a full-factory reset in that moment left him worried that any semblance of skill in such games had just been wiped out by that carefree smile.

As his processors slowly booted back up, he stumbled forward.

Yen was inserted, and he took his seat in front of Drum Fever.

Sho nodded to Lara, indicating she should pick the first song.

She did eagerly, settling on a relatively easy option. The language was Japanese, of course, but the interface was well-designed enough that a toddler could figure it out.

It became quickly apparent that both of them were holding back.

Lara was comboing with ease. Her eyes were on Sho's screen, not hers.

Sho intentionally made several mistakes, making a grand show of false frustration with each one.

It earned him a laugh. A treasure more valuable than any full-perfect-combo possibly could have provided.

The song ended, and Lara pulled out her phone and typed: *"You pick. No holding back."*

Sho took the phone and returned, *"Are you implying I was holding back? I'm so bad."*

It earned him another laugh; she nodded towards the game.

He picked a rather challenging song, one that was particularly well-suited for losing himself in. An Anime opening that had strong metal tones with a grand climax that could cut through any fog of demonic energy, no matter how thick.

The song enveloped them, bass thrumming and reverberating into Sho's very soul.

His hands instinctively kicked into motion, drumsticks vibrating with every collision, providing the sweet feedback of calculated and controlled rhythm.

Sho inhaled, closing his eyes as he conceded his body to the familiar motions of the chart.

He had always viewed rhythm games as a conversation.

The notes were syllables, letters, and hiragana. They composed words: combinations of easily recognizable patterns that each carried instructions. You combine these words into cohesive, directed sentences.

To play a game, you not only learned to read and predict those words and the sentences they composed, but you also learned to speak them.

Whether it was feet, fingers, hands, voice, or some games even engaged the whole body, you were speaking a language of arrows, dots, and lines.

Sho didn't exist right now.

Annoying coworkers who needed to grow up didn't exist.

His empty, haunted house with absent parents didn't exist.

The ever-persistent knowledge that Sho was doing absolutely nothing with his life to honor those absent parents didn't exist.

The only things that existed were music, notes, vibrations, lights, the kick of the pedal, the machine-gun pattering of drumsticks on rubber.

But...

There was something Sho *wanted* to exist right now.

His eyes opened, and he turned to Lara.

Her own eyes were trance-like, locked on the screen. Sho knew that look; her own world had ceased to exist.

Lara's gaze flickered to him during a brief pause in the song, and a smile splashed across her face.

As it returned, the music felt like a bubble that encompassed both of them. They had found a shared language, and they spoke it with proficiency, fighting off the demon lord's armies of uncertainty and doubt.

It was somewhere between twenty minutes and four hours before the two were interrupted by a staff member looking to close the arcade.

Sho refused to check his wallet to calculate the damages. Whatever they had been, they had been worth it.

They found themselves on the front step of the arcade, sobering up from the extended time submerged in bass and neon.

He needed to ask her out.

That had been simply too perfect, and he *needed* more.

He just... couldn't come up with the words that felt right.

Instead, he found himself typing, *"Where are you staying? I can walk you home."*

Lara typed back, *"I need to find a hostel, my one-month rental ended."*

Sho did an exaggerated yet authentic double-take. *"You don't have a place to stay?"*

Lara laughed. *"It wouldn't be the first time."*

The thought of this girl being alone without a place to stay in any city horrified Sho.

He fought himself for a moment, contemplating whether he would come off as a pervert if he offered to let her stay with him.

But... they were both adults, right?

And he was reasonably sure they had progressed beyond the "random stranger" level of acquaintance at this point.

It would most certainly prevent him from making any romantic moves for the foreseeable future... it would just make things awkward...

He could accept that.

Sho typed back, *"I have extra rooms."*

Lara's eyes went wide. *"I couldn't impose!"*

Sho shook his hands and head. *"It's not imposing! I'm lonely. Just watch some anime with me. That can be your rent."*

Lara tilted her head. *"I've never watched anime."*

Sho gave an over-exaggerated offended look. *"No anime? We need to fix that."*

Lara laughed, typing back, *"Are there any in Spanish?"*

Sho quickly evaluated his level of infatuation and compared it to his level of opinion on the dubs-versus-sub debate. Lara barely won out.

Sho typed, reluctantly, *"I'm sure we can find some with Spanish dubs."*

The subway ride seemed to only prolong the lingering; all Sho could do was keep his leg from bouncing the whole way. Lara seemed immune to such lingering effects.

It wasn't long until they found themselves at his two-story, fenced-in house. It sat nestled in a street too narrow for even a car to fit, squished in by similar houses of varying shapes and sizes.

He stepped into the familiar haunted darkness, silence enveloping him and reminding him of what once was.

"I'm home!" he exclaimed to the ghosts of his parents.

As usual, there was no response.

Sho removed his shoes and stepped up into the room.

"Todama?"

Lara's voice fought the silence off like a flaming sword of joy.

Sho turned, heart beating rapidly as he found the image of her in his doorway. "Ta. Da. I. Ma."

"I'm home!" Lara exclaimed, removing her shoes and stepping past him to study the house.

Sho moved to a portrait of his parents that hung near the base of the stairs.

Per ritual, he bowed as low as he could, which wasn't terribly low considering his fit, mid-sized build.

Sho prayed, hoping for their approval of the girl he had brought into their house.

They seemed to consent, and every step Lara took trailed a warmth that blossomed out and left the air feeling... restored. She only furthered the endorsement by taking her place next to him and matching the bow.

Sho grinned.

It was the first time he had had his parents' blessing since the accident six months earlier.

Once they straightened from their bows, Lara began rummaging through the kitchen, taking stock of the available cookware. The way she shifted and weaved from cabinet to cabinet was as if she already belonged here.

The demons lurking in the far corners would never dare encroach while he had such a powerful guest.

Lara approached Sho with her phone out. *"I'll make breakfast. Allergies?"*

Sho shook his head, typing back. *"You don't need to! I'm just glad I can help. Let me show you your room."*

She didn't protest, but Sho suspected she would still make the food.

He showed her up to one of the guest rooms, highlighting the restroom and the off-limits master bedroom.

It wasn't long until he closed the door to her room, giving her privacy to wind down.

He shut his own door moments later.

He collapsed onto his bed, staring at the ceiling with arms outstretched.

The day's events replayed through his mind endlessly, every memory re-igniting a broadened grin orchestrated by an elated cacophony of firing nerves.

The feeling was unlike *anything* Sho had *ever* experienced.

This was impossible.

It made no sense.

Ren was wrong.

Sho didn't have a crush.

Sho was head-over-heels enamored with a girl he couldn't talk to.
