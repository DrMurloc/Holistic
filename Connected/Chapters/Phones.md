It would most certainly prevent him from making any romantic moves for the foreseeable future... *that* would most certainly put him in "pervert" land, flirting with someone he was hosting.

He could accept that.

Sho typed back, *"I have extra rooms."*

Lara held the phone up for several moments, likely contemplating the right way to turn Sho's offer down.

**For the sake of clarity, let's do another perspective switch here.**

**Lara**

Extra rooms?

Oh, he actually was offering for her to stay with him.

She... actually *did* need a place to stay. But that hadn't *quite* been the point.

All the energy that had charged her skin drained into a puddle on the street, leaving her feeling drained in every sense of the word.

In some ways, it was relieving, even if slightly disappointing.

Lara clearly had been rushing things.

She could roll with this, though.

**Sho**

Lara finally typed back, *"I couldn't impose!"*

That wasn't a rejection, right?

It didn't feel like one.

Lara tilted her head. *"I've never watched anime."*

Sho gave an over-exaggerated offended look. *"No anime? We need to fix that."*

Lara laughed, typing back, *"Are there any in Spanish?"*

**Now, I think this is a great time to talk about "opinions".**

**An opinion is a belief in something. Whether the opinion is subjective or objective in nature, coincidentally, doesn't matter. This is good, as humans are distinctively awful at objective opinions. If they weren't, there would be quite a bit more copepod worship in the world.**

**It is, however, the commonly held opinion that holding an opinion is a good thing, whether or not it is the right opinion. I'd like to identify the rocky waters of an ongoing opinion war that Lara just found her metaphorical ship navigating.**

**Subs versus dubs.**

**For those not familiar, this is the debate about whether or not it was "right" to watch a foreign movie, or Anime in this case, with your familiar language represented as either spoken "dubs" or text "subtitles".**

**Much like most opinions, ultimately, it was personal preference. But the existence of "it depends" seems to be a concept that the average human struggles to acknowledge, so the commonly chosen path was typically to "die" on the colloquial "hill".**

**All that to say, Sho was now being faced with an existential crisis of evaluating his level of commitment to dying on the hill of "no dubs allowed" against his newfound besottedness for this girl.**

**Lara barely won out.**

Sho typed, *"I'm sure we can find some with Spanish dubs."*

He felt his soul drain a little with each letter.

The subway ride felt like a whole new level of "lingering"; all Sho could do was keep his leg from bouncing the whole way. Lara seemed immune to such lingering effects.

It wasn't long until they found themselves at his two-story, fenced-in house. It sat nestled in a street too narrow for even a car to fit, squished in by similar houses of varying shapes and sizes.

He stepped into the familiar haunted darkness, silence enveloping him and reminding him of what once was.

"I'm home!" he exclaimed to the ghosts of his parents.

As usual, there was no response.

Sho removed his shoes and stepped up into the room.

"Todama?" Lara's voice fought the silence off like a holy sword of pure light.

Sho turned, heart beating rapidly as he found the image of her in his doorway. "Ta. Da. I. Ma."

"I'm home!" Lara exclaimed, removing her shoes and stepping past him to study the house. She trailed her suitcase behind her. It was turquoise and covered with all sorts of stickers featuring different buildings and country shapes.

Sho moved to a portrait of his parents that hung near the base of the stairs.

Per ritual, Sho prayed, pressing hands together and bowing forward slightly.

They seemed to consent, and every step Lara took trailed a warmth that blossomed out and left the air feeling... restored. She only furthered the endorsement by taking her place next to him and matching his posture and bow.

Sho's breath caught.

It was the first time he had had his parents' blessing since the accident six months earlier.

He stared at the phone for a few moments while Lara began rummaging through the kitchen, taking stock of the available cookware. The way she shifted and weaved from cabinet to cabinet felt as if she already belonged here.

Sho's mind raced back to an unfinished thought from dinner.

Low context.

Sho finally forced himself to type out, *"My parents passed away six months ago. It's been very lonely. It's nice having you here."*

He approached Lara and extended the phone, averting his gaze. He waited for her to go silent or to suddenly act awkward. That was what happened when you broke the script.

Instead, he found her arms wrapped around him for the second time.

The warmth she had been radiating through the house suddenly seemed to concentrate on him, seeping through the fabric of his t-shirt. His arms slowly rose to embrace her back.

And just like that, the demons lurking in the far corners retreated. They would never dare encroach while the house contained such a powerful guest.

She finally pulled back, and Sho noted a tear on her cheek.

She typed on her own phone, *"I'll make breakfast. Allergies?"*

She didn't protest, but Sho suspected she would still make the food.

He showed her up to one of the guest rooms, highlighting the restroom and the off-limits master bedroom.

*"The demon lord lives in there,"* Sho typed to her, gesturing at the Master bedroom. He laughed.

She probably didn't get the joke.

He shut his own door moments later.

He collapsed onto his bed, staring at the ceiling with arms outstretched.

The day's events replayed through his mind endlessly, every memory re-igniting a broadened grin orchestrated by an elated cacophony of firing nerves.

The feeling was unlike *anything* Sho had *ever* experienced.

This was impossible.

It made no sense.

Ren was wrong.

Sho didn't have a crush.

Sho was head-over-heels enamored with a girl he couldn't talk to.

**Lara**

Lara exhaled slowly, pulling the suitcase into the room. It was a simple room, just a bed, a wardrobe, and a sliding door into a closet. It was much more comfy than a pod hotel or a cramped hostel, though.

Six months ago...

He lost his parents.

Six months is *not* a long time.

Not for something like that.

Lara couldn't even begin to imagine what he had been living with... alone? In an empty house? He couldn't be much older than her. There was *no* way to know what that would be like.

She had been running away from her parents, and here was a boy who had *lost* his.

The thought twisted her stomach as she fell back into the bed, staring at the ceiling.

She clearly had feelings for Sho. Right? That's what this was?

This was all messy.

Anchors were messy. She didn't need anchors.

But the idea of leaving Sho here alone to fend for himself...

Lara felt a sob shake her body. It coalesced into a single tear that dripped back across her cheek and dripped to the bed.

She would figure the messiness out later.

For now, she just closed her eyes and let the exhaustion of the day sink into her.
