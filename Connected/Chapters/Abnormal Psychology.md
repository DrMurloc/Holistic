## Abnormal Psychology

**Uni**

Okay, who next?

Let me pan this camera all the way to the opposite coast.

Many of you native to the United States may be familiar with a college settled in the dead center of the rather large state of Pennsylvania. This college is overlooked by a statue of tan weathered stone. The statue is of a “Nittany Lion”. Why they insist on specifying that the lion is from Mount Nittany, rather than calling it a “cougar” or “puma” like the rest of the world, is beyond me.

This statue overlooks a rather large campus.

This statue has seen some things.

The main campus of Penn State is well known as being, by all definitions, “bustling” and “boisterous”. Alumni from this campus carry a certain pride in having survived the wildest of keg stands, competitive flip cup, and enough bad decisions to keep the entire college’s Psch Degree graduates employed for the rest of their lives.

It is not just a college; it is a destination.

Sprawling green gardens with squirrels that make you question what “too friendly” is for a rodent, shops, restaurants, festivals, events, you name it. College football was a religion, and alumni and enthusiasts alike would swarm to observe a rather popular physical sport where a ball that was not shaped like a ball, referred to as a "pigskin," though it contained no such thing, was carried by hand from one side of a field to another. Said sport was referred to as "football," though feet were rarely involved.

I don't pretend to understand sports; I tend to switch perspectives to one of my favorite sea-bound tiny crustaceans whenever Astrid watches them.

Regardless, by all definitions, Penn State Main Campus is “alive”.

This is not that campus.

Welcome to Altoona.

Altoona was a rather "dry" campus in a town about as interesting as a light-grey rock in a pile of white stones. What a "dry" campus meant was that instead of openly bringing alcohol into a loud party, students would place the bottles in their backpacks and drink in their dorms.

The most intense this campus ever got was a single time in 2019 when four students soaked a hacky sack in kerosene and almost burned down a bench.

My point being, it was quiet and removed, though admittedly quite pretty, with pleasant rolling hills and more than a few flower-blooming trees speckling the landscape.

And currently skipping through the hallways of the campus–sporting yellow overalls, a light blue T-shirt dotted with hearts and stars, and perfectly combed straight black hair, lined by a single pink stripe–we find Zoey.

**Zoey**

Zoey's steps today carried her with a level of perk and bubble that could only be described as "frolicking".

She was prone to a good frolic on the way to class. Because Zoey frolicked.

It was the number one rule of Zoey. Zoey did not stroll; she did not pace; she did not even walk. Sometimes she might skip; on occasion, she would scamper; but most importantly, she would *frolic*.

**Some of you might be asking, "Is this girl** ***actually*** **wandering around and listing out rules for herself in her head?"**

**The answer is yes.**

**Yes, she is.**

Today, Zoey's frolic had extra lift; her feet and the ground played the part of rivals as she swirled and twirled through the halls. Her hair swayed behind her in tandem with her hips.

She, of course, needed to ignore the occasional "Zoe, watch out!" or "Incoming Zoey!" that came from various members of the passing crowds. They didn't understand, they didn't *know*. How could they? If they did, then they would be frolicking too.

No, if they had known what Zoey knew, an entire musical chorus would have broken out; the whole college would have erupted into an impromptu and appropriately cheesy refrain.

For on Zoey's phone, sent last night by her cousin, Cale, was a text certifiably proving of the most crucial fact in life.

It was a simple fact, but it was the glue that held the entire universe together: That Love. Would. *Always*. Triumph.

Well... with one exception, but it was a complicated and dumb exception.

Anyway.

**For those of you now wondering, "Is this girl really deflecting her own thoughts?"**

**The answer is also: yes.**

**Yes, she is.**

Finally, though, Cale and his not-so-secret lover, Astrid, could be open about their own love.

It was guaranteed that he would whisk her off on some romantic getaway almost immediately. And it was *most* certain that they would be married in a year.

It would be *so* romantic.

Oh, Astrid had spent years denying Zoey's assertions. Zoey had known from the beginning, years ago when Cale first told her that he had met a girl, those two were *destined*.

Despite what Leo said, it wasn’t complicated. Love was simple. People made it complicated. And complicated things were *dumb*.

Zoey wondered how long it would be until Astrid discovered Cale's secret job as a super spy. What if *Astrid* were a counter spy? Oh, their love would *blossom* as they realized the agencies they worked for were seeking to destroy the world!

She performed yet another twirl as she rounded a corner.

"Zoe, cuz, yell corner or something," Kev yelled back to her as she barely avoided a collision.

She ignored him, beaming at Asher as he passed a short way behind. The boy met her gaze briefly, then kept walking, mumbling to himself and holding a spiral notebook to his chest like a shield.

It wouldn't be inaccurate to describe Zoey as a popular girl. But it wasn't a rule of Zoey; Zoey did not need to be popular. It was more of a side effect.

No, the second rule of Zoey was that Zoey was social.

It wasn't quite like how her cousin was social; Zoey didn't *network*. However, Zoey had *no* qualms about initiating conversations or asking questions when someone or something caught her eye.

Which led to the third rule of Zoey: Zoey was charming.

She needed Zoey to be charming. How else would people let her explore their love lives? How else would she know that Mia was standing next to the window and pretending that Asher *hadn't* just passed? Or that Kev and Justin had a favorite broom closet?

It was never about her, though.

That was the final rule of Zoey: never focus on Zoey.

She didn't think about that rule a lot.

It was a dumb, complicated rule, and not the type to make one frolic, and not frolicking, as established in rule one, would be distinctly *not-Zoey*.

But it was still a rule, a piece of the delightfully and perfectly crafted Zoey. Every thread, every button, every patch had a purpose. And that purpose was: to bring *joy*.

She neared another corner, emitting a soft sigh at the image of Cale carrying Astrid aboard a plane to Greece... or maybe… Japan? Oh, the cherry blossoms would make for such beautiful pictures...

Of course, her distinct lack of focus on herself meant that when she rounded the corner leading up to her next class, she walked smack dab into someone.

They tumbled to the ground, a cliché of books and papers spilling around them with a variety of flutters and clatters.

Zoey remembered that present tense existed as reality snapped into focus. "Oh my goodness! I'm so sorry!" She reached down to help pick up the mess.

A delicate, feminine hand, with the thumb through a sleeve hole, reached out for the same book and accidentally touched Zoey's fingers.

Zoey froze, the tingling electricity from the contact leaving her quite paralyzed.

The hand *lingered*.

Was this it? Like in the movies? She surely was about to look up and meet eyes with the love of her life. She felt her breath catch.

*Don't raise your head too fast; this needs to be perfect.*

Her eyes dared to lift, inch by inch, taking in the image of her new soul mate.

Sneakers, shorts over black leggings, a black t-shirt with some J-pop idol–Mimi Galaxy?--on it, a green hoodie…

Was that a Japanese loopwheel-knit cotton? The hoodie was *sensory-friendly?!* Zoey had the same exact fabric in her swatch kit! She *needed* to show her!

And the hoodie had thumbholes. *Thumbholes!*

Where did she possibly find-

Focus, Zoey. You’re mid meet-cute.

Her gaze continued to rise, finding *red hair*.

Zoey's heart fluttered. It was *just* the most perfect, radiant red hair, cut and layered to the chin. The hair curled in slightly to frame a freckled face, and the most adorable glasses.

And... and... behind those glasses, green eyes.

Wait, hold on.

Those eyes weren't making eye contact. Doesn't she know how these scenes work? It was like they were looking *slightly* past Zoey, not quite at her.

Zoey moved her head several times, attempting to intercept the look. The girl merely shifted her gaze to avoid her each time. This girl *clearly* had not watched enough movies. She was doing it *all wrong*.

"I'm... I'm sorry," the girl mumbled, quickly scooping up the papers and books.

Zoey was left dumbfounded, still crouched, as the girl stood and walked away. Maybe she wasn't into girls?

Was Zoey into girls?

Was Zoey into anyone?

She supposed the answer didn’t matter.

She stood and followed the red hair as it disappeared down the hallway. She was tempted to chase her, but Zoey's phone vibrated twice, telling her she would be late for class.

Into girls or not, it was unusual for Zoey to go so... ignored. It didn't *bother* her. Zoey wasn't a narcissist. But something about how that girl had scurried didn’t feel right. It wasn’t a good scurry. Zoey knew what a good scurry looked like. And she knew what a bad scurry looked like.

That had been a bad scurry.

That girl needed a good frolic.

Zoey shook her head and resumed her path towards class, thoughts of her cousin pushed out by an image of freckles and red hair.

**Naina**

Naina sat on a couch, keenly aware of the presence of... *people...* around her. A textbook lay open on her lap; the title on the front read "Abnormal Psychology."

The TV in the far corner gave murmurs from some reality show, ignored by everyone except the corner of Naina’s mind.

It wasn't that the people *bothered* her. It was that they were *there*.

He brain was quite comparable to an owl. Every motion, every laugh, every shift in the air, and that owl’s head would swivel and fixate. It tracked every risk, every potential confirmation that she was an intruder in this otherwise social dorm lounge.

*She* of course wouldn’t look. Looking would be rude. And she didn’t want to look like a skittish mouse, so she kept her vision fixated on the text that she was most definitely not reading.

A group of three students sat on the floor, not too far away.

The owl swiveled.

It would be rude for Naina not to give them the couch, right? Was Naina hogging it? Or was she overcompensating for a social awareness that she hadn't come pre-equipped with?

Someone laughed nearby.

The owl swiveled.

Were they laughing at Naina? Was she doing something wrong? Maybe sitting weird?

Naina logically knew they weren't. She knew, logically, that everyone in this college was too caught up in their own lives to focus on some random quiet girl. But emotionally, feeling all of that was another question.

It was so hard to tell where Naina ended, and well... where *Naina* began.

She didn't want to have a negative impact, so every interaction and every sentence needed to be prepared.

Talk-tracks upon talk-tracks, and an aversion to anything she didn't have a talk-track for: an entire wiki of dialogue trees ready for every scenario except *actual* conversation.

And right now, out here, she had no talk-tracks. Casual socialization was scary and unstructured. So her mind tracked every person through the room, mentally preparing for any potential confrontation.

Wait, when had she started reading her phone?

She supposed she might as well finish reading the fanfic; she definitely wouldn't be able to focus while that chapter was only half read-

No, that was a trap.

It would leave on a cliffhanger, and she would just start the next chapter.

She returned the phone to her hoodie pocket, though several threads of her brain remained connected to it as she restarted the current textbook paragraph for what felt like the hundredth time.

Why had she left her comfortable room? Naina didn't really know. But she knew one thing: something needed to change, or else she would not graduate next year. And her family simply didn't have the funds for her to do another semester.

All of which brought her to the dorm lounge, trying something different. For the first time in months since her roommate moved out.

In this case, *different* meant pushing back the mental screams to run and hide from the complexities of social settings.

The fear of being intrusive and rude was not the only thing that kept her mind distracted, though. That girl from earlier had triggered a hyperfixation of sorts.

That’s where the other threads of Naina's brain were: reaching desperately towards the image of that girl in an attempt to latch onto some perceived path to the sweet, intoxicating release of dopamine.

It wasn't quite the same as Naina's other fixations had been, but it was close enough for her to identify it. She had gotten somewhat good at tuning those out since she was diagnosed with AuDHD, but sometimes one would sneak on in and refuse to leave.

And this girl, like the fanfic world's strange obsession with shipping K-pop idols, *refused* to leave.

"You!"

The owl swiveled and cocked its head.

Naina sighed to herself; she could even *hear* her now. Although auditory hallucinations were not really a symptom of her diagnosis.

Naina returned to the passage, determined to make it to the end of the second sentence.

A presence invaded Naina's space. "What's your name? What are the chances we are in the same dorm? I've never seen you before. Are you a freshman? What are you studying?"

Naina blinked several times and looked up to find her hyperfixation fixated on her.

*What did she just ask? I think the first one was name... major was in there somewhere... what was the order?*

"Uh, Naina? One in four. Uh... Junior. Hold on, one at a time, please." That was three, right? There were four questions.

The girl's head leaned over to study the book; several strands of black and pink hair fell onto Naina's hands. Naina caught a whiff of strawberry and immediately blushed at the fact that she had smelled someone.

*Don't be rude.*

"Abnormal Psychology, that's cool. I'm Zoey!"

The girl... well... she plopped. There was no other word for it. The couch bounced as she fell backward down next to Naina.

There had not been many people Naina had sat next to in her life, at least that she could think of. But she was *pretty* confident that the amount of space that this girl, Zoey, had left between them was *just* a tad less than what would be expected in a standard interaction.

Naina *could* be wrong, but "knees brushing together" didn't feel like a necessary part of such a casual social exchange. But this Zoey seemed to be a *tad* more social, so Naina deferred to the expert.

For reasons beyond her comprehension, Naina decided to commit to the exchange instead of scurrying away and hiding, which would, of course, have been the logical decision.

"They say most Psych Majors study it to understand their own uniqueness. I feed that stereotype." Ah, yes, straight to the neurodivergence, that's how you make friends. Right?

Zoey seemed captivated by the statement, though, for reasons Naina was sure she would never understand, "Huh. Wonder what they say about fashion majors."

This was a no-brainer, and Naina responded as such. "Control and obsession with aesthetics is often masking behavior for insecurities around one's self-perception of worth. In some cases, associated with childhood trauma."

Zoey's very animated body froze as if Naina had hit pause. Her face flickered somewhere between a deep frown and a shaky smile; for a heartbeat, the glow that powered her perk seemed to fizzle.

Naina re-evaluated her encroacher.

Yellow overalls. Where do you even buy those? Small friendly shapes were meticulously patched onto several points across the denim.

Was that glitter applied across the bridge of her nose? And what college student had the money to get their hair dyed? That was a *vivid* pink stripe. Did she dye it herself regularly? Who took the time to do that? And what was that frown?

*Masking insecurities around their self-perception of worth.*

*Oh.*

The realization smacked Naina in the face like an unexpected dead dove tag.

Naina so desperately hoped she had not been accurate about the childhood trauma as she added, "But like, also... clothes are pretty."

A short, nervous laugh erupted from Zoey, but her body relaxed, resuming a slight bounce on the couch. "Clothes *are* pretty. I like you, Naina, you're smart."

*She likes me? What does that mean? Didn't I just insult her?*

This was clearly some social nuance that Naina didn't understand. She was OK with accepting that.

Naina studied the girl's face for a moment, avoiding the eyes, then found herself looking down at her textbook, which was opened to a page titled "Symptoms of ADHD".

Wait.

Everything clicked into a pattern. The way she talked was like she needed to get the ideas out before they evaporated. Her bouncing was indicative of some level of self-stimulatory behavior. Low awareness of social boundaries and environment.

Naina knew how to interact with that pattern.

She lived that pattern.

Naina had an open assertion to respond to. What would this girl want with *Naina* of all people? She was patiently waiting for a response, well, if bouncing eagerly counted as patient.

Naina's eyes rose slowly up to Zoey's to find... some... some... clue...

Hazel.

Her eyes were hazel. Was "Hazel" a clue?

What a silly word, hazel. What color even was that?

This color, of course. The color of those eyes.

Zoey's eyes.

The word played through Naina's head a million times.

Hazel.

The urge to look away finally won out, and Naina found herself staring instead at the widened smile, not quite a grin, that had encompassed Zoey's face.

How long had Naina been distracted there?

What a *strange* hyperfixation.

"I like you, too?" Naina finally said cautiously, deciding to simply mirror the interaction. "You're–” Oh, what word would describe this girl? "--enthusiastic?"

Zoey, who had been saying something about “17-micron Merino Wool”, paused and tilted her head. Naina looked down.

During Naina’s spelunking into the world of “hazel”, Zoey had pulled out a stack of small rectangular fabric sheets and spread them across both of their legs and the textbook. Naina’s mind attempted to play back what she had missed, but the most she was able to pull up out of the rapid-fire texture deep dive was “long fiber”, “Tencel blends”, and something about “French terriers”.

She wasn’t quite sure she got that last one right.

The girl dropped the swatch and extended a hand with yet another beaming smile. "Well then, it's settled."

"Something is settled? I'm sorry, I don't follow."

"Do I need to spell it out for you?" Zoey teased.

Naina tilted the book back, causing several of the fabric sheets to slide down to her chest. The book clearly read "Abnormal Psychology" on the front cover, with a flat look on her face.

Zoey raised her hand to her mouth, and she giggled.

People actually giggled? Naina wasn't sure she had ever heard the sound out loud, though it was used *way* too often in fanfics.

"Friends. I want to be friends," Zoey clarified.

Well, *now* it seemed obvious.

"Why?" Naina asked without thinking, quickly adding, "Wait, no, you don't need to answer that. Sorry, sometimes my words are faster than my brain."

Zoey contemplated, then began to collect the fabric back into a neat stack. "I don't know. Want to go watch a movie?"

Naina gawked. "Wait, you can just… ask people to do things?"

"Yeah, why?" The girl's bouncing had increased, causing Naina herself to bounce with the couch.

Because of social contracts, a delicate balance of expectations that had to be met before you could simply ask people for things. Right? Or was that a Naina thing?

Naina conceded to Zoey's flawless logic, "I mean, I don't have a counterpoint, but I'm not sure that means you're right."

Zoey toned down the bouncing and simply sat there patiently, her head bobbing from side to side to a tune no one else could hear.

Naina's eyes dropped back to the textbook. There was no way she would make more progress. Her mind was on this bubbly girl and her supernatural ability to ask for what she wanted. Something about watching movies with Zoey sat very well within her fixation, anyways.

"OK. What are we watching?" Naina gathered her books and papers up and stood.

"Super cheesy romance movies. And tacky horror movies," Zoey said with delight, hopping off the couch.

Naina found herself smiling. When was the last time that had happened? "You are very decisive."

"Yeah, why?" Zoey beamed, then looked at Naina's books. "We need to get you a backpack. Or a messenger bag."

"I don't like straps on my shoulders. They feel constricting. This isn't one of those 'popular girl makes the quiet girl pretty' things, is it?" Naina fell in step behind as Zoey took off at an energetic pace. Was she frolicking? Who frolicked?

To Naina's surprise, Zoey spun around and began walking backward.

Zoey's head tilted, looking Naina directly in the eyes. "Why would I need to do that? You're already pretty."

Naina yet again found herself trapped in a sea of hazel for several moments before her eyes shifted to the floor. Several people had to dodge out of the way of Zoey as she kept her backward momentum.

*Did… was that… Did she flirt with me? Or is this girl-talk?*

Naina suddenly wished she had spent more time making friends to better understand the expected dynamics between two college girls.

Zoey spun forward, slowing enough for Naina to keep up.

It was moments later that Naina found herself in a foreign dorm room.

Naina felt slightly less bad that she had smelled Zoey earlier, as the faint, pleasant scent of strawberries wafted across her.

Given the suspected ADHD, Naina had expected to see cans, papers, and unfolded clothes: an organized chaos, like Naina's room.

The organization was there, but the chaos was absent.

Textbooks lined the desk neatly and appeared to be alphabetized. School supplies had dedicated containers; even the snack stash was organized based on... healthiness?

Naina spent a moment puzzling over how the snacks were organized, but she tore her attention away from the task in an attempt to focus on her new friend.

From what Naina could remember, over-organization was often a compensation, masking, or defense mechanism for ADHD. If true, Naina was sure the effort was exhausting and counterintuitive.

"Your room's much more organized than mine," Naina noted, not really sure what else to say.

"It's how I was raised. Everything has a place." Zoey began to switch some wires from a cathode-ray TV to a flat-screen TV. Both sat on a wooden TV stand a few feet away from the foot of her bed.

Naina cocked her head. "To help... stay focused?"

"No, it's just... how my dad is." Zoey froze briefly, then quickly added, "Your room isn't organized?"

Naina was bad at reading people, but she couldn't help but think the girl had just deflected. It would be rude to pry, right? Naina quickly concluded it would be rude to pry.

"It's... I know where everything is. Except when I don't. Sometimes things... teleport." Naina didn't know how to put it into words. She had a vague impression of where everything in her room was located. But there was a learned uncertainty with how accurate that impression was.

Zoey gasped, her head briefly peeking out around the TV. "You know about the time gremlins!"

Naina blinked. "You mean... like from Twilight Zone?"

Zoey squealed at Naina's recognition of the reference.

"You *do* know about them! They're sneaky. You put a phone down and suddenly it's across the room."

"Are you implying that working memory deficiency–" Naina paused and re-contemplated the sentence. "--that the tendency to lose things is caused by reality-shifting creatures between the seconds?"

Zoey nodded enthusiastically. "It explains so much! Doesn't it?"

Strangely, it did.

A misfiring of a series of impossibly complex and deregulated tiny connections that composed the brain, versus supernatural critters that simply screwed with people.

Naina fought off the sudden urge to watch The Twilight Zone. She could *not* afford to pick up that rabbit hole of fandom again right now.

Naina briefly contemplated the available seats in the room, "Would your roommate mind if I sat on this bed?"

"Probably not?" Zoey said from behind the TV. "I haven't seen her since Freshman year."

Naina carefully sat on the edge of the bed and spent a moment wondering what she was supposed to do with her hands. She settled for leaning back on them.

"What happened? Does she not like you?" Naina asked.

Zoey giggled. "Oh no! We're friends. I introduced her to her boyfriend. She sort of just... stays in his room now."

"Is that allowed?" Naina asked.

"Who's going to stop them? We're in coed dorms." Zoey giggled again.

"I assumed there would be a rule for that."

"There might be. But who follows rules? What are they going to do? Kick them out of college for having an adult relationship?"

**Now, for those of you wondering, "Didn't Zoey start her introduction listing off an entire list of rules for herself?"**

**Three for three! Your intuition is on point this chapter, well done!**

**Welcome to Zoey.**

The assertion left Naina speechless. The rules were important, right? Why make them if you're just going to ignore them situationally? Or, once again, was that a Naina thing?

"I don't have a roommate either," Naina said. "They transferred to Main Campus this year. And I think the college just forgot about me."

"Oh, that sounds lonely."

It had been hard. Naina's entire routine had been built around her roommate's schedule. They hadn't interacted often directly, but the urge to at least come off as functional to someone she lived with had been enough to get her moving most days.

Once that was gone... well. Who was going to notice when Naina slept til four in the afternoon? Or when she literally never left the bed?

She had plans to get everything together, and *oh,* the plans were *grand*.

Homework would get knocked out, she would learn *everything*, and get 100% on every test, while picking up extracurriculars. She was going to pick up pottery and make beautiful pieces to bring back to her parents.

Tomorrow, of course.

And tomorrow would come, and the vibes wouldn't be right, anxiety would be high, the perception of a change in routine would horrify her, and nothing would change.

She was locked in a–

"Are you OK?" Zoey's voice cut through Naina's thoughts. The girl's head was cocked as she studied Naina's zoned-out stare.

"What? Oh. Yeah! Sorry." Naina shook her head.

Naina regained focus, finding the opening scene of a movie called "Accidental Soulmates."

"No, no apologizing. That's the new rule one," Zoey said.

"Didn't you just say no one follows the rules?" Naina asked, legitimately confused.

"I did." Zoey nodded confidently, as if there was no contradiction, and plopped onto the floor at the foot of her bed.

Naina blinked as a smile slowly crept onto her lips. It felt foreign, yet satisfying. An unexpected dopamine hit, she supposed, made sense given her new hyperfixation.

Naina leaned forward in an attempt to see the TV. She had to squint. "Can I sit on your bed?"

Zoey giggled again. She did that a lot, it seemed. Maybe the fanfics didn't lie about giggling endlessly. Naina just hadn't actually expected golden retrievers to exist in real life.

"Rule two: No asking permission. And rule three: no overthinking," Zoey said.

No overthinking? Well, clearly, Naina needed to think to some extent. She couldn't just... do everything that popped into her mind. There were many things that flew into her mind. The more intrusive ones, especially, were not to be acted on.

Maybe she could set a timer for herself, two seconds per thought.

How would she do that? That timer would get started a lot. She wondered if someone could invent a stopwatch that could be controlled by the brain. Would devices like that get interfered with by the fact that her brain wasn't wired quite the same way?

*Wait.*

"No promises on rule three. I'll do my best on rules one and two." Naina transferred herself to Zoey's bed, lying forward and propping her head on her hands.

"See! You *do* know how rules work." Zoey giggled.

Naina laughed in unison.

It shook her body and pushed back a layer of fog. A warmth spread through her veins, thawing a thin layer of morning frost.

Her cheeks almost felt sore as clearly unexercised muscles strained.

Was that... A decrease in cortisol, maybe? Had her cortisol levels been high?

It made sense, Naina supposed, in hindsight. She had her anxiety under control, or so she had thought. She understood it; she had compartmentalized it and everything. That was the point? Right?

"Your laugh is cute, you should do that more," Zoey stated with a giggle, spurring Naina to laugh again.

She felt warmer, finding herself trusting this girl more than she probably should, considering she was a stranger until about twenty minutes ago.

Oxytocin. Definitely oxytocin.

The bonding hormone.

The thought filled Naina with yet another wave of warmth and comfort.

For the first time in Naina's life, she was bonding.
