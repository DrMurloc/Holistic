**Asuna**

The vending machine flickered, causing Asuna to jump.

She was alone, even if briefly. The safety and warmth that radiated from the door down the hallway called for her to run back.

Why wasn't it dispensing her sparkling water? She pressed the button again. Nothing.

Footsteps picked up around the far corner, causing her heart to race. She glanced back, then remembered her contacts weren't in.

She hit a random button.

Wait, that was an energy drink. That was the *opposite* of what Asuna needed right now; that would likely kill her.

The footsteps grew closer, and the vending machine was left abandoned. Asuna didn't look back as she walked briskly down the hall back towards the door that seemed to have a golden glow around it.

Her hand reached into a pocket, ignoring the slight ache in her side from tugged stitches.

Wait, had she not grabbed the key?

The footsteps paused at the vending machine, and a masculine voice called out in Asuna's direction. "Excuse me!"

She refused to look back.

Did they recognize her? It wasn't like her hair or profile was particularly identifiable. How would they recognize her? There was no emotional comfort in that logic.

She knocked frantically on Hawthorn's door without looking back.

**Hawthorn**

The damn girl locked herself out.

Hawthorn sighed and opened the door.

A small flurry of black hair ducked under his arm and pressed her back to the wall. He felt her eyes immediately latch onto his face.

Takanawa, his neighbor, approached, confused, holding out an energy drink. "She left her drink."

Hawthorn smiled and shook his head. "Ah, don't mind Naomi, she's shy and forgetful."

He accepted the drink, bowed, and closed the door, turning his gaze to Asuna, who was composing herself with casually crossed arms and a lean.

She caught his stare. "What?"

Hawthorn looked at the energy drink, then the girl, who was clearly putting effort into controlling her breath. He shook his head and placed the drink on a counter.

"You know, you could have asked me to come with you," Hawthorn noted.

"I need to start going out on my own," Asuna said assertively.

"It's been three days since you were *stabbed*. You've been out of the hospital for *thirty minutes*. We haven't even been back for *ten*."

Asuna turned her head with narrowed eyes. "Well, I need to start sometime."

"I think you're missing the point," Hawthorn asserted.

He moved across the room and to the fridge that was right around the corner, and pulled out the last of his soda waters.

Asuna's eyes went wide when she saw the bottle.

She scurried over, extending her hands with an optimistic, pleading look. She grinned in success when he dropped the bottle into her hands.

This girl's layers were persistent.

He wasn't sure if the flat-faced Asuna was *truly* her or just the one she liked wearing around him. Had he really cracked through layers at the hospital, or just made her switch masks?

Hawthorn shook his head as he headed back towards the couch and flipped on the TV, which was tuned to American news. His eyes trailed the stock ledgers beneath the news. It was more calming background noise than a real update; his stock updates came in real time on his phone.

"The point is to get better, right?" Asuna finally noted from behind him.

"Yes."

"Then what do I do?" Asuna asked. Hawthorn heard the fluttering of clothes and the soft patter of feet behind him.

"Nothing."

"That sounds inefficient."

Hawthorn looked over his shoulder and found Asuna performing some sort of dance routine.

Was she practicing? The motions were stifled, likely to avoid disturbing her stitches.

"I swear, you are ten different people crammed into one," he stated.

"I'm pretty sure we could fit ten 'me's inside of you."

"Not my fault you're tiny."

Asuna growled, exiting her little routine. "I'm not tiny, you're big."

"Not as big as you'd think."

Hawthorn found that damn smile fighting for a place at the corner of his lips. It seemed deeply attracted to this girl and found every opportunity to present itself before her.

Hawthorn agreed with it.

Honestly, Pre-Vanessa Hawthorn would have already bought a ring. And then would have proceeded to put off the actual wedding for nearly a decade.

Asuna sat down next to him, leaning into his shoulder and pretending to watch the news. Hawthorn winced at the touch, causing her to slide a few inches away. The frustration and disappointment seemed to emanate like an aura around the girl.

Post-Vanessa Hawthorn, however, had some problems to work through.

In Hawthorn's self-due-diligence, he had established that there were three fundamental facts at play right now:

They had formed a *deep* connection in that hospital room. Their hearts had synchronized and were *very* connected.

Asuna *needed* a physical anchor right now.

Hawthorn could not provide that physical anchor in his current state.

Logically, Hawthorn was the bottleneck. One that had significant emotional debt to work through. That backlog of debt went *far*, and much of it was contingent on unmade strategic decisions. The majority of that backlog was a year's worth of tears, held back by a dam of stone.

Hawthorn had no inclination to weigh this girl down with that emotional debt payback.

So he acted tactically.

Hawthorn's hand found its way to Asuna's leg and squeezed. Not quite intimate, but he could feel her tension release slightly.

Of course, tactical moves alleviated, but did not solve, systemic problems.

Those tears *would* come.

"What *do* we do, then?" Asuna broke the silence.

Hawthorn watched as her fingers hovered over his hand, unsure of how far she could press. Hawthorn himself was still uncertain.

"Pardon?"

He longed to grab that hand out of the air, but he knew he would immediately let go. The perception of abandonment was *not* what Asuna needed. She was brilliant enough to know that wasn't what it was, but, well, logically knowing that and emotionally feeling it were two different things.

"Project Galaxy. I'm home. Well, out of the hospital. What do I do?"

Her fingers lightly made contact. Hawthorn wished she had put the whole hand down flat; the sensual, feathery touch filled him with desire. And that desire forced him to look away and tune the feeling out.

"Oh."

Hawthorn tried so hard to ignore the feeling, but the memory of those same fingers on his cheek at the hospital played through his mind. Why did she need to be so fucking beautiful? Those eyes...

Business, talk business. "Do you think you're in a position to do anything?"

She paused, as if running a diagnostic check, finally responding, "No."

Her fingers curled lightly around his, forcing Hawthorn's eyes to squeeze shut. They were different fingers... colder and surprisingly calloused. Asuna's movements were unfamiliar, but that didn't help tune out the deep, primal craving to cradle Vanessa's longer, more artful digits.

Business.

With a deep breath, he strengthened his stone dam. "Then *you* do nothing."

"Hawthorn, I told you. I don't do cushy or lazy." Her hand, thankfully, flattened against his and curled lightly between his fingers.

His eyes opened and rested on the overlapped hands, "You are a single point of failure. You need to let others step up. It's not lazy, it's energy efficient."

Bright eyes turned up to Hawthorn. "And when they fail?"

He met them. "Salaried employees are a strategic investment, especially if you're looking to scale. Not a tactical resource. Failure is part of growth; it's healthy."

"We're barely in the black as is, you really think we have the risk tolerance for that?" Her fingers uncurled and began to lightly brush the back of his hand again.

Her hands were so small compared to his.

She was so small.

It would take him no effort to just pick her up, press her against a wall, and-

*Hawthorn. Stop it. You know the memories that come at the end of that thought.*

"Compartmentalize the growth. You're not turning into an industry leader in one year; we're talking at least a decade here. You do have an advantage with an existing successful product, though."

"When did I go from asset to product?"

The fingers stopped mid-stroke, and the sudden stop in motion only further caught his attention. What would those fingers feel like on his back?

He tried to picture Asuna's moan; all he could hear was Vanessa. His whole body went rigid.

*For fuck's sake, Hawthorn, BUSINESS. Come on!*

He stood up and turned away; he knew her hand would be suspended mid-air with longing, and the thought pained him.

They both wanted each other so bad; he was the fucking bottleneck.

Business.

"There's someone you know that could and should be doing more."

"Kenji." The answer came with no hesitation.

Hawthorn stood and grabbed her phone off the counter, holding it out for her, still averting his eyes. "Call him."

She took the phone. After a few moments, she pressed it back into his hand while it rang.

Hawthorn moved to the large sliding glass door overlooking the city and exhaled. How long had he been holding that?

"Moshi moshi. Kenji. Is everything OK, Miss Morita?" An optimistic voice piped through the phone. Hawthorn liked the sound of that voice; that was the voice of someone who cared. And caring brought passion. And passion brought quality.

"Kenji! The famous Kenji! It is an honor to meet you. I'm Hawthorn!" Hawthorn felt Asuna join him in his city-gazing, well within personal space but not touching.

There was a pause. "Oh, uh... With all due respect, I would like to talk to Miss Morita."

Hawthorn grinned, letting his leadership personality cut through. Kenji was, in fact, going to be a valuable asset.

"Absolutely, Kenji. I'm so happy you asked."

He passed the phone to Asuna. Her face instantly shifted from a flat stare to a soft, caring smile, her eyes sharpening over the city.

"Hello, Kenji! I hope you're doing well."

Hawthorn shook his head and held back a laugh as yet another Asuna was discovered.

"Yes, well, no. I caught a minor case of..." Asuna glanced up at Hawthorn, who shrugged. "The stabbings."

"Did you say 'the stabbings'?" Hawthorn let this laugh out, a good feeling. Maybe he shouldn't hold those back.

It was Asuna's turn to shrug.

The phone was lowered for a moment. Asuna sighed and rolled her head, foot tapping.

"Not what he expected?" Hawthorn asked.

"I think he tripped and fell. He's probably looking for the phone now." Asuna said it as if it were a regular occurrence.

"I like Kenji," Hawthorn stated with another laugh. Asuna nodded with a smile, raising the phone back to her ear.

"No, Kenji. That's not the important part. I'm fine. Listen, I'm on vacation as of now. I'm going to give you back to Hawthorn. You're going to follow his instructions on establishing a consulting contract. Give him a list of our standard salaries; he's going to..." her eyes turned up to Hawthorn's, "... 'advise you' on an appropriate price point. I would very much appreciate it if you took his advice."

Hawthorn winked, happy that she had picked up on the implications of putting himself in a consultation position.

He would not make decisions. He was done with decisions.

"No, Kenji. I've not been kidnapped. If I had, my assailant would soon find out that he had picked a very poor target for ransom. I'm transferring you. I'll see you in the office in a few days or so."

Two weeks. But Hawthorn didn't need to win that battle right now.

Hawthorn took the offered phone and held it to his ear. "OK, Kenji. Have you ever been audited before? No? Well, you're gonna do great. It's completely painless; the goal is to only provide existing documents, nothing new. Completely non-invasive. You're a busy guy, and I don't want to interfere."

*Welcome to hell, Kenji. You're going to create so many new documents this week.*

**Asuna**

Asuna watched Hawthorn move down the hallway and into the bedroom, shutting the door behind him.

Her body uncoiled, and she watched a single tear escape in the reflection of the sliding door. She refused to acknowledge it.

Mimi's passive routine overtook her; the familiar and ingrained motions soothed her. It was frustrating to hold back while the stitches healed, but after several days of being cooped up in the hospital, returning to any movement felt great.

In Asuna's self-analysis, she had established that there were three fundamental facts at play right now:

They had formed a *deep* connection in that hospital room. Their hearts had synchronized and were *very* connected.

Hawthorn needed an emotional anchor while he worked through grief.

Asuna's complete lack of experience in interpersonal relationships and boundaries made her unable to be that emotional anchor.

Logically, Asuna was the bottleneck. She kept pushing too far for her own selfish reasons, when she knew all he needed was some time to work through himself.

But Asuna did not know how else to work through her own emotions.

She had lived a life constantly moving forward and never looking back. In her opinion, it was necessary, but it had apparently left her with many feelings and anxieties that she didn't understand, and many that she hadn't even been aware of.

She just... needed a release valve, something to let the dam of emotions out.

And... well... she could not get the image out of her mind of those strong, protective arms lifting her up, pressing her against the wall, and... opening a valve. Granted, the nurse had explicitly said the words "no sex" until she was completely healed. So she was only tormenting herself with those thoughts.

Asuna took stock of the room while she moved.

It was pretty spartan: a couch, a TV, and two bookshelves with financial self-help books that looked significantly untouched.

One wall of the room had a small pile of boxes lined up, likely in preparation for the move he had expected to come soon.

The walls felt so bare. Asuna supposed it made sense for his personality; he was efficient to the core. But its size made it feel extra... hollow.

There were several colorful stains splotched across the floor that she couldn't quite identify.

Asuna stopped and stared out the window over the city.

What had he suggested?

Do nothing?

The implication: efficiency in inefficiency... Asuna couldn't quite wrap her mind around that.

Everything changed as responses to actions: people, the world, and industries. It was all cause and effect.

To do nothing implied no change, no change meant no results. No results meant zero efficiency. Right?

Logically, it was sound. But Hawthorn was confident in his assertion to "do nothing". And he was also logical.

The illogically logical paradox it painted left Asuna's head spinning.

Her head turned back to the hallway, as if she could see around the corner to her powerful new... what was he to her?

Did this qualify as them dating? There was clearly an intent to date. Maybe this was why that "it's complicated" status existed on all the social media that she used for Mimi.

She didn't have much to compare to, though. Did all relationships start off this... uncertain?

Asuna's dating experience had been limited to an innocent relationship in high school and a one-time mistake of dating an overzealous fan.

Granted, the fan had been charming. She enjoyed the dinner. And the endless rows of Mimi Galaxy figurines in his room had been endearing.

But the *very* used body pillow of not Mimi, but *Asuna...* that tiny detail had been a dealbreaker. It had entirely turned her off to the idea of dating. She didn't really have time for it, anyway.

Asuna still could not figure out who produced those pillows. Maybe Hawthorn could help hunt them down so they could cease and desist.

But Hawthorn... Those eyes had unlocked something in her. And the way he had so casually said, "I want to explore every inch of your body," using corporate lingo like it was his first language...

Asuna closed her eyes, falling into her new breathing exercise.

In and out.

In and out.

She could think of something else that-

*Stop it, Asuna.*

She looked for a distraction.

Her eyes fell on one of the boxes that was partially open. Asuna could make out an upside-down portrait in it.

She glanced down at the hallway, where she could hear Hawthorn's cheerful yet commanding voice still coordinating with Kenji.

It wasn't even a battle; curiosity overtook Asuna, and she moved to the box, picking up the portrait.

Hawthorn in a suit briefly launched Asuna's mind into a craze, but it reeled back quickly as her eyes fell on the brunette next to him. She wore a billowing, shoulderless white wedding dress.

She was gorgeous, with her hair layered short right above the shoulders. Bright and playful green eyes.

And that grin...

Asuna's heart stopped.

She moved to the sliding door and flashed her own grin, holding the picture up next to her reflection.

They lined up nearly perfectly.

Asuna's face drained, and her knees suddenly felt inadequate as support.

*Oh god, Hawthorn. What have I been doing to you?*

She knew that Hawthorn had been struggling with this, but...

"Asuna?"

Without thinking, she turned, the photo still held up, her grin still pasted on.

Hawthorn stared blankly.

They simply stood there, the murmur-level whisperings from the TV suddenly felt cacophonous.

There was no monitor to measure time, and her heart remained stopped, so it felt like an eternity that Asuna was trapped in this torturous moment.

She dropped the grin and frantically replaced it with... anything.

A frown.

Go with that.

Hawthorn turned away and dropped to the couch as if stabbed by a knife. That powerful back slumped forward, and his forehead rested on his hands.

Asuna put the photo back and slowly approached Hawthorn, reaching out a hand, but it stopped inches from his back.

She was doing this.

This was her fault.

This man had saved her life twice, then thrown himself at her mercy to provide her stability... while she returned the favor with repetitive hurt.

The hand retracted.

Asuna needed to leave.

She couldn't do this, not to him.

She moved towards the door and reached for the handle.

"Asuna. Stop." The words came out sharp and direct, a command.

She shook her head, but turned back.

Tears?

His face glistened with tears, which ran freely down and off his chin. Her mind couldn't compute it for a moment; his face contorted, any semblance of the stone wall gone as if she had taken a mallet to it.

He wasn't hiding it; he wasn't bottling it up.

He was presenting it.

Asuna had seen a glimpse into his heart through those clouded, tired eyes the other day. But now...

Why? What was he doing?

"I don't..." Why was her voice broken?

Her body shook.

*What?*

Something dripped off her own chin and onto her arm. She looked down at the point of impact, realizing that the corners of her vision had blurred with water.

She was crying? Could she cry? She legitimately had thought that her tear ducts stopped working at the age of ten.

Her gaze turned back up to Hawthorn, who extended a hand out to her.

She cautiously approached, tears still flowing, and took it. She found herself pulled into an embrace. The back of her head grew wet while she soaked Hawthorn's shirt.

It felt... good.

Why did it feel good?

They were truly making a mess of one another.

Not quite how Asuna had pictured it, but apparently, Hawthorn had found an emotional valve for her.

This wasn't an intimate embrace; it wasn't passion-driven, and it wasn't even an act of affection. Hawthorn was clinging to her like she was the only lifeline keeping him above water.

The realization made her almost laugh through the tears that still flowed.

They were so fucking hopeless.

This wasn't Asuna being held above water by someone more powerful and experienced. They were two broken and mangled survivors of *different* shipwrecks clinging desperately to one another as a storm raged.

Support. It was Asuna's turn to support him, right? She needed to be his stability. He needed her to lead with the important questions.

She pulled her head back, turning her eyes up to his face, and started, "Could..."

She had asked this question several days earlier, and it had made him nearly leave. But had that been simply to hide the tears that she was now seeing?

He was opening his heart to her in ways she was sure he had never planned; it felt like an invitation to help fix it.

His eyes turned to meet hers. They were soft, pleading, as if they were clinging.

It gave her the push she needed. "Could you tell me about her?"

He didn't immediately react.

After several moments, he pulled away and stood up from the couch.

Asuna's heart sank. She had fucking done it again. What the fuck was wrong with her?

"Hawthorn, I'm-" She started.

"She had the most beautiful smile." Hawthorn moved towards the hallway.

Asuna hesitated, then cautiously slipped off the couch and followed. Her feet fell softly, as if any noise would disrupt whatever spell had just been cast.

Hawthorn entered his room for a moment. Asuna peeked in. It was also fairly bare, like the rest of the relatively large apartment. Only a bed, a nightstand, and a wardrobe. It also felt small for a master bedroom.

He pulled something out of the nightstand and returned to the hallway, passing Asuna and stopping at a door about halfway back.

He stopped and stared at the doorknob.

"She painted. And drew. It was my goal to make sure she never needed to work a day in her life."

Asuna looked around at the bare walls and began to understand why the apartment was so underdecorated.

"I..." Hawthorn's voice broke on a sob. He inserted a key and turned the knob.

"I was successful."

The door opened up to what was clearly the actual master bedroom, washing Asuna in the scent of oil and varnish.

It was lined from wall to wall with picture frames and canvases.

Asuna could make out landscapes, cafes, abstracts, vases, portraits, a vast array of styles and subjects. She was not, by any means, an art expert— at least not in this type of art— but they looked *good*.

Hawthorn stepped into the room, inhaling deeply, seeming to let the energy of the paintings fill his lungs. The exhale brought a new wave of tears.

He maintained a distance from the pieces.

Asuna silently stepped past him, her face beginning to dry as tears were replaced with wonder. She picked up what appeared to be a canal near Gion in Kyoto, with beautiful pinks and reds scattered gently for cherry blossoms.

"Needed to get them valuated as part of... well... tax purposes."

Asuna looked back and found him looking fondly at a painting of deer in what appeared to be Nara Park.

He continued, "She would have quite a name for herself if we put these out there. There's more in storage. I couldn't bring myself to sell a single one. I'm selfish like that."

Asuna placed the painting down delicately, her mind replaying everything he had just said. His entire career had been devoted to this woman. And he was deliberately avoiding any direct responsibility in Project Galaxy.

He had made it clear that he would be in an advising role, at most. He was very familiar with anxiety attacks and had honed in precisely on how Asuna had put herself in a position of burnout and overwork.

Asuna finally broke her silence. "Hawthorn, you put yourself through hell for her. How is that selfish?"

A weak smile took over his face, and he shook his head, "It would be a whole lot easier to think that if I had been there for even a quarter of these paintings."

That struck something deep within Asuna, though she couldn't quite name it. Despite her best efforts, her own well of tears refilled, leaving refreshed rivers down her cheeks.

She watched as the powerful man placed his back to the nearby wall and slumped to the ground.

Asuna had thought it impossible for him to look so small, yet there he was: knees curled up and face buried in arms.

Asuna found herself next to him, leaning into an occasionally shaking shoulder. Her mind attempted to piece together anything, absolutely *anything* she could say, but... what do you say to *this*?

"I don't..." she began, pausing briefly before following through, "I don't know what to say."

"Honestly," a noise somewhere between a laugh and a sob caused Hawthorn to pause, "I don't either. I don't know what I want. Spent an entire year trying to find the right words, the right rationalization, the right perspective."

"For her death or for your decisions during her life?" The words slipped out before Asuna had time to even think them through.

They caused Hawthorn to pause, and for several moments, all Asuna could hear was his steady breath. His head finally tilted up.

Soft, vulnerable eyes met Asuna's.

"Both. But... Those... *are* different problem spaces, aren't they?"

Asuna's eyes began to dry up as she realized somehow she had helped.

She let her stream of questioning continue. "What would knowing the answer to either question help with?"

Hawthorn gave a sharp, mock laugh. "Are you using reframing techniques on me, Asuna?"

Asuna sighed. "You can call it 'reframing', I call it 'survival'."

Silence enveloped them for a full minute. Her mind swam with potential advice and recommendations. Surely, though, Hawthorn knew it all. What could Asuna possibly hope to coach him on?

"I shouldn't have laughed. It was a great question," Hawthorn noted. His legs extended, and Asuna mirrored the change.

"You said you spent the entire year trying to find the right words... I meant what I said the other day. It is always the next step. No regrets."

"But those steps have a compounding impact. I spent my entire life making the 'right' investments, taking those next steps, only to find out years later that it was all in the wrong direction. Asuna, I've made decisions that led to entire departments losing jobs."

Asuna let out a hollow laugh. "I don't think I ever stopped to question what direction I was even going. The number of people I've hired just to lay off a month later? Sticking to the next step falls apart when each step leads you in circles. Maybe 'no regrets' has its limits."

"I don't think either of us is moving anywhere in our current state, regardless."

The silence returned, interrupted only by their synchronized breaths.

The picture of the deer stared back at them: the third participant in this retrospective.

"You two would have been great friends," Hawthorn noted. Asuna felt his body shake with another stray sob. "Although I doubt I could ever look away without you two getting involved in some dumb scheme."

Asuna tilted her head against his shoulder, not quite sure how to respond. She got the feeling he didn't need a response to everything, though.

Maybe there *was* efficiency in silence.

She tucked that thought away for later.

Several moments passed before he continued. "You said no regrets? Not the first I've heard that philosophy from."

When had she adopted that?

It was not something she had ever put into words until today, but it was most definitely how she lived.

"I don't know how much was philosophy and how much was survival. I think it turned into both. I never had the bandwidth - time or mental - to dwell on the past."

"No regrets," Hawthorn said the words out loud, though he didn't sound completely sold.

It had been easy for Asuna to shift into the mentality. She had started it early in her career, when she had a relatively clean slate. Hawthorn most definitely had much more to work through.

She felt him shift, and moments later, they were both standing. The room had begun to darken as the evening crept along the city. The edges of the sunlight just barely lit the deer at this point.

Hawthorn placed a hand on her shoulder. "Thank you. I couldn't... I couldn't do this on my own."

There was finality in the statement, as if this was the end of a session; Asuna suspected there was more to come.

She lingered after he left, watching the shadows creep along the army of paintings.

"Ramen? I can run downstairs for it." His voice was back to its flat and rough state, any indication of vulnerability gone.

Asuna let herself smile.

Compartmentalized growth indeed.

"Udon. I'll join." She walked out of the room to join her fellow survivor.

She left the door open, offering the portraits a breath of fresh air that they desperately deserved.
