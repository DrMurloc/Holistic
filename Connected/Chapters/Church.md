**Lola**

Lola basked in the murmurs and ruminations of worship.

They stood surrounded by their altar: a walnut countertop encircling an island of communal offerings. The offerings were, of course, poison: an assembled choir of vodka, tequila, whiskey, you name it. Whether you came for escape or confession, Lola had your forbidden apple.

Stars and moonlight flooded the dimly lit room through floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the sprawling city, giving them the presence of the heavens themselves.

Mass was well underway, with a good turnout for early evening on a weekday. The line of worshipers who sat along the bar was currently being served by Marcus, the head bartender.

Lola scanned the room, brown eyes against a warm golden canvas beneath voluminous and styled black hair.

Trays of food flowed seamlessly to and from the kitchen, all tables on the floor appeared to be satiated, and all guests seemed to be lost in the intimate vibes of chill R&B.

Business was good.

Lola began their rounds around the bar.

Marcus was lost in the rhythm and appeared to be on top of things. The new barback chugged away at the steady flow of drink tickets while maintaining light chatter with various guests near their station.

Lola dropped off the Pakari twins' usual lemon drop martinis, a quick beer refill… and a Long Island for Jillian.

As they completed the circle, Lola noted two faces had joined the congregation.

Cale, of course, would get his standard Cosmo to start, but who was...

Lola nearly tripped on the edge of the barmat as their eyes fell on the blue ribbon and blonde hair.

Stray whispers of "Astrid" rippled along the bar, though the girl appeared to not hear as she was lost in a menu.

Cale, the hopeless fool he was, simply smiled like the young woman who sat next to him had *not* been the topic of *hundreds* of hours of discussion in that very seat.

"Lola! I'll take a -" He began.

"Uh-uh, honey." Lola snapped. "You do *not* pull that nonchalant crap while escorting a *guest of honor*."

The statement finally got Astrid's attention, and she looked up as Lola found their place leaning forward against the bar, hands flat against its surface.

"Cale, really? I -" The girl's eyes narrowed at Cale.

Oof.

Lola interjected. "You were right, Cale. She *is* gorgeous, razor-sharp eyes and all."

What dumb game have you been playing with this poor girl, Cale?

Whatever words Astrid had died on her lips, cocking her head back at Lola.

"You know me, I don't lie." Cale lied.

This visit wasn't about Cale; he had more than his share of sermons.

"Cale, honey, shush." Lola extended a hand towards their guest. "Lola. I have been waiting years for my little sanctuary to be blessed by your presence."

Astrid accepted the hand with a *firm* grip. "Astrid, it is a..." Her eyes widened with recognition. "Oh! You must be the wise bartender! Bourbon old-fashioned, please."

*A bourbon girl, excellent.*

Lola grabbed a top-shelf bottle and a tumbler glass.

"I'll take a -" Cale started.

Lola cut him off. "Did this fool really bring you in blind? Oh, honey... This man finds too much amusement in the things he *doesn't* say."

*Splash of water, sugar cube, bitters, muddle with cherries...*

"Hey! I don't -" Cale found Astrid's finger up against his lips.

"Cale, shut up." She turned back to Lola. "So that's not just me? I thought he did it to torment me."

Astrid, satisfyingly, picked up on the game. Lola was already liking this girl.

*Ice cube, bourbon...*

"Oh, I don't doubt you get special treatment. But no, the fool treats information flow like a game. We're all punch lines in a joke that only he finds funny."

Cale sat up and leaned forward. "I'm right here!"

*Flash the orange peel...*

"No one cares, idiot." Astrid shot Cale a quick glare before turning back to Lola with an inquisitive look. "How do you deal with it?"

Lola stole a brief glance around the room, noting an empty beer glass. Marcus was already moving on it, though.

"Simple, honey. Ignore him. It drives him nuts."

Lola winked and dropped off the drink in front of Astrid, before turning to continue their rounds without letting Cale finish his order.

"Bringing you here was clearly a mistake," Cale stated.

Lola couldn't help but smile.

That damned fool and his prepared lines.

A rather dramatic sigh of satisfaction was heard from Astrid, likely after her first sip. "I like this bar. Good call, Cale."

A piece of Lola beamed at the assertion.

The other pieces were fixated on two newcomers, both of whom had eyes fixated right back on them.

One was quite the tall drink of water. European suit vest with matching grey pants. The way he looked at Lola, a nervous yet anticipatory smile, meant he was here for the sermon. That smile did nothing to hide the pain that hung like an aura.

The other new guest was the *puta*.

The faltered step and eye twitch were likely imperceptible to anyone not closely studying Lola, but they tuned the woman out.

Lola grabbed a martini glass and shaker while greeting tall, pale, and handsome. "Honey, you're going to need to get that suit dry cleaned if you soak in that hurt any longer."

"Am I that obvious?" The man asked, shyly looking away.

*Gin... dry vermouth... olive juice... shake...*

"It *reeks*."

It earned a chuckle. "Lola, I presume?"

"My reputation precedes me. What's your poison?"

*Blue cheese olives...*

"Surprise me."

The martini was placed in front of the bushy-haired woman, though Lola refused to look at her.

"Diego-" She started.

Lola ignored her; there was no Diego here, not right now.

**Now, let me camera-shift a little for the sake of clarification.**

**Those of you attuned to pronoun choice likely already have picked up on a nuanced detail.**

**Lola was a stunning woman by most people's standards. They stood about as tall as Cale, which was fairly tall. The wig, as mentioned, was voluminous and fell to their back. Their brown eyes carried wisdom and experience that few in the world would ever achieve.**

**Like many in their particular avocation of interest, if there were a scale for emotional intelligence similar to IQ, they would be well qualified as a genius.**

**They also had an Adam's apple.**

"I need a *little* bit more than that." Lola rinsed the shaker, maintaining focus on the man. "Are we talking sweet, spicy, bitter, strong, weak, smooth?"

*Vodka, cranberry juice... Cointreau...*

"Sweet and smooth, I guess? I don't drink often; a friend pointed me this way... They said I would find value in your... other services. To be blunt, I don't really know what to expect."

He *was* here for confession.

Excellent.

*Lime juice... twist of lemon...*

Lola took another scan of the room. Another bartender had just arrived for the closing shift.

The bar was covered. Lola could go big tonight.

"And can I get a name for the order?" Lola flashed the man a smile while pouring the Cosmo into a martini glass.

"Blair." The man nodded.

Lola grunted in approval, rolling their head back with a smile. "Blair. Stoic, calm, collected. A restrained grace. It's a pleasure to meet you, Blair."

They winked and returned to their new favorite customer and her fool.

The Cosmo was deposited unceremoniously in front of Cale, sloshing slightly against the rim, and Lola's mind latched lightly onto their conversation while scanning the bar for ingredients.

Astrid's voice was sharp with frustration, "-just told stories about me to everybody? What do they know about me?"

"Lola!"

Lola grunted in acknowledgement, though they didn't directly respond as they grabbed a shaker.

*Gin, lime juice, lemon juice… heavy cream… simple syrup…*

Where was the orange flower water? Were they out?

"Tell Astrid everything I've told you about her, no holding back."

Lola paused, contemplating the request.

*There's the orange flower water.*

What would Cale have a line for?

Lola's smile was hidden as their back remained to the couple. "Well, he has this one dream where -"

Cale cut in, "No! Maybe not everything!"

"*Cale*, fantasies? You told people about *fantasies*?"

*Ice, and shake shake shake...*

"Only Lola, I'm not a weirdo or anything."

"*Telling anyone about them is weird*." Astrid snapped.

Lola froze.

Oh.

Astrid had taken that literally.

So much of what Lola had heard about this girl suddenly snapped into place.

Lola might need to get a *little* direct and literal. Cale would most definitely leave them alone for a moment later. The conversation Lola was going to have with Astrid was *not* going to be the one Cale expected, though.

For now...

"In all seriousness, honey, the fool never had a bad word to say about you." Lola cut in, turning to the couple while shaking the drink.

The assertion clearly grabbed Astrid's attention.

Lola continued, "Cale would walk into this bar reeking of defeat and failure, and would sing your praises to the heavens. The first time you beat him at a video game, going crazy over pandas at the zoo, how you helped him pass English in high school... the list goes on."

*Strain the shaker, lose the ice... egg whites... and here we go. Shake shake shake...*

Astrid was stuck in a state of shock, lips parted and cogs turning.

She needed another nudge.

"Astrid, honey, people come to me to vent. I hear the *worst* words leave the lips of men, women, and everything between at this bar. This man, however, in *years* of storytelling, has *never* once had a negative thing to say about you."

Astrid's gaze flickered across the other bar patrons, some of whom were staring. Lola shot them a look that got them to avert their gazes back to their own damn business.

For the briefest moment, Lola received what Cale referred to as "the stare", narrowed eyes so sharp they almost made Lola drop the shaker.

But it was gone quickly as Astrid seemed to conclude, correctly, that there was no deception.

Instead, she turned and placed a firm kiss on Cale's lips, whose arms went limp like the captured prey he was.

Lola couldn't help but grin widely.

As hopeless as he was, Cale deserved this win.

Lola poured the contents of their shaker into a tall glass, placing it aside to sit for a moment.

"Diego." The *puta's* voice cut through the increased chatter that followed the lovebirds' little display, causing any semblance of satisfaction to drip right off of Lola's face.

In three large steps, Lola found herself in front of the woman. Their voice dropped multiple octaves, and Diego responded in a whispered growl through gritted teeth.

"What do you want? Did you really fly out here to bother me in my own bar? Are the never-ending phone calls not enough for you?"

"Am I not allowed to order food?" Carmen responded.

It irritated Diego to *no end* how calm she always remained despite the vile beliefs they knew swam through that twisted mind. The yellow designer coat and pants only reinforced the image of someone who faked a level of confidence she had never earned.

Diego contemplated just throwing her out.

They knew every single sentence that would be exchanged between them. There wasn't a single one to lead to anything productive.

Diego glanced over to Blair, who waited patiently with folded hands for his own drink and moment.

Reluctantly, Lola re-emerged and straightened up, hands moving to their waist and thrusting their hip out slightly.

"What can I get you, then, *honey*?" They threw extra sweetness into that "honey".

Lola knew precisely how much they were pissing the woman off right now, addressing her with this voice.

And they loved it.

"Chicken Caesar," Carmen said simply, never breaking eye contact, taking a pointed sip of the martini Lola had made her.

Lola rang the order in and returned to the near-finished drink, placing it in front of Blair.

They grabbed the fountain nozzle and cautiously poured in soda water. The foam of the drink rose straight up, slightly above the glass. The man's eyes widened as he studied the effect.

Lola topped it with another drop of orange water, using a toothpick to shape it into a heart with a wink, then pushed it forward.

"OK, honey, that much soaking has had to brew some mighty strong tea. Spill it."

Lola glanced only briefly at Carmen, who was clearly listening to the conversation.

"Tea?"

"Yes, honey. You were sent to me; there's a story, and I will hear it."

The man took a sip while thinking. The satisfaction of his eyes rolling slightly with the first taste filled Lola with a deep warmth of validation.

"What is this?"

"Ramos fizz. Fair warning: the entire staff has orders to gaslight anyone who attempts to order it on a busy night like this—we 'don't have the ingredients'." Lola threw another wink and leaned forward. "Now, tea. All over the table."

Their finger jabbed down into the bar counter with a raised eyebrow.

"I... It's a guy." Blair paused.

"Of course it is. It's either that, a girl, or somewhere in between or beyond. Keep going."

"He's my partner. We... It's stupid."

"Trust me, honey, you'll never top the level of stupidity going on over there." Lola nodded towards Cale and Astrid, who appeared to be arguing about the definition of "legume".

The man lounged back with his hands behind his neck and elbows flared wide, though his face remained flat and tense. "It's... He wants to adopt."

Oh.

That was most definitely *not* stupid.

Lola straightened up, crossing arms and tapping a foot. "Mhm. Honey, I said *all* over the bar top, not daintily dripped onto a napkin."

Blair took a deep breath, finally confessing, "We started dating two years ago. It was supposed to be a one-time hookup... And it was. One time. And another. And another."

Lola couldn't help but flicker eyes over to Carmen. She met the glance, then looked away.

Blair continued, "We were happy. We never really talked about our relationship status since we just assumed we were on the same page. And suddenly, a few weeks ago... kids are the only topic of discussion."

This was going to be like navigating a minefield with Carmen sitting right there.

Why the Lord sent Lola this challenge, they could *not* begin to understand. But here they were.

"Honey, sometimes people think a kid can solve relationship problems. When you are insecure about your relationship, there's this dumb idea that kids can fix it. That they can be your glue."

Lola avoided looking at Carmen very assertively.

"But we're happy. What is there to be insecure about?" Blair asked.

"Being happy is unfortunately not enough to prevent insecurities."

The mine went off, and Carmen interjected, "Are you suggesting we should have never had Lara?"

Oh lord.

"This isn't about you, Carmen." Lola did their best to maintain control, keeping focused on Blair, who was blinking at the intrusion.

"How is it not?"

"Blair..." Lola ignored Carmen, getting Blair's attention back. "Children are a *big* commitment. I don't know anyone who has gone in without underestimating the level of impact it has."

Carmen tried again, "Diego, are you really saying-"

Lola's voice rose, keeping Blair's attention on them. "They are also the most validating success you will ever feel in your life."

This was enough to get Carmen to sit back and disengage.

Blair re-engaged. "But if I'm not interested?"

"Kids don't fix relationships, honey. They test them. If your partner is *actually* interested, he needs to find a connection stronger than a series of hookups. And if you are just trying to keep your existing connection, that will go away the moment you have a third in the household."

Her chicken Caesar salad was dropped off, but was ignored. Lola knew she had never wanted it in the first place.

"So you *are* saying you regret -" Carmen started.

Diego snapped in, stepping over in front of the *puta* and leaning forward, growling. "I regret *nothing*."

"How can you say that? She's out there *alone.* She's squandering her college fund."

Here we go.

Diego did their best to keep their voice under control, but their best wasn't very good with this woman. Their voice wavered, threatening to rise.

"She would have failed out of college, and you know that. She barely speaks English."

Carmen continued to maintain her calm state. As if being the calm one made her right. "You never got her a teacher."

Diego, meanwhile, elevated their voice with each response. "I did! You know that's not how she learns! She needs to *want* to learn before she will."

"That's nonsense, she just needs to focus."

"*You* need to focus and actually *see* your daughter for who she is, not who you want her to be."

"Diego, the answer isn't just letting her do whatever she wants."

"It's most certainly better than calling her every day and reminding her of her failures."

"How else would she learn? It's how I -"

"I don't give a *fuck* how you learned." Diego realized their voice had risen above the bar noise and toned it back. "Lara is *not* you."

"Then what do you suggest we do? Because she's just wasting money, and one of these days she's going to get kidnapped. She's only *twenty*, Diego. She doesn't know how the world works."

"And either of us do?"

Carmen's voice actually picked up to match Diego's. "Yes! Just because both of you are caught up in the chaos of a mental disorder-"

And there it was.

Diego straightened up and pointed to the hallway leading to the elevators.

"Out."

The soft murmurings of the bar slowly re-enveloped them with the command.

There were definitely several eyes that either averted or stared at the confrontation. Only Cale and Marcus were aware of the context of what was going on, and Diego could see Cale's flat stare turned their way past an oblivious Astrid.

Diego had no doubt that Cale would go as far as to throw punches to defend them, even against a woman.

Carmen's eyes darted around the bar, recalculating her platform. She likely had expected public exposure to keep Diego "in check".

"Diego, you need help, this mask is -"

"Mask? You think I'm *masking*?" Diego stepped back, stretching arms out and turning to face the section of the bar surrounding Cale, and projected their voice. "Who here knows that I'm biologically male?"

A scattering of hands rose, including Blair's.

Astrid, bless her soul, glanced around in confusion. Cale grabbed her hand and lifted it into the air.

Diego snapped back to Carmen, leaning forward on the countertop, still projecting their voice.

"I am *expressing*. I'm not the one with the mask. You hide behind words of venom because you are afraid of what you don't understand. You hide because your simple, *small* intellect can't properly comprehend the *true* complexity of a human's identity. I will not hold myself back from self-expression just because simple-minded people like you cannot understand the depth of a multifaceted individual."

All conversation at the bar had ceased.

Diego leaned in, dropping his voice to a growl. "And our daughter is not *masking*. She is *demasking*. She is *recovering* from your bigoted generational trauma and learning who she actually is. And if you can't see that, then you are *not* properly looking."

Carmen attempted her sympathetic "caring mother" eyes, soft yet focused, with a head tilt. "Lara needs structure. She's going to get herself hurt."

"She needs *guardrails*, not *authority*. You-"

"I'm *giving* her those guardrails, I-"

"You are giving her *blame* and *self-hate*."

"She's disabled, she can't-"

"Out," Diego growled.

Diego's chest heaved, widened eyes *dared* the *puta* to say even a *single* word further. Distant chatter from tables and soft crooning from the speakers were all that kept Diego and Carmen anchored in reality.

Marcus's presence approached from behind, but Diego reached an arm out to keep him uninvolved.

Her voice returned to her calm state. "Diego, you need -"

Diego pointed again, cocking his head.

"*OUT.*"

Carmen took another glance around the bar.

Without looking, Diego knew that all gazes were on her. He heard a bar stool scoot along the floor and a small gasp from Astrid as Cale stood up, ready to step in.

Carmen stood, attempting to maintain some level of poise as she finished the rest of her drink.

Gazes followed her to the exit. Each step she placed carried a grace she did not deserve, as if refinement implied victory.

As the distant elevator doors closed, Diego finished their sermon, turning back to Blair, "So no. I have no regrets and nothing but blessings with my daughter, but if you are only in it for your partner and they want more, I would get out."

Light and uncertain applause rose from several at the bar, guiding Lola back into their place.

One last deep breath, and Lola straightened up and tossed their hair. They shifted their weight and beamed across the bar.

"Marcus, pretzel shots, all around."

This got much more enthusiastic applause.

They paced away from Blair, leaving him to reflect, and made several slow laps around the bar as their head cleared. They moved along the bar, their gaze bouncing between drinks, topping off several beers on the house as Marcus distributed shots.

The congregation collectively toasted and drank to Cale and Astrid. Lola deeply appreciated that they let the energy shift away from their "moment" and back to a more important one.

This bar was, in many ways, their family.

Cale and Lola made eye contact as he stood and excused himself to the restroom.

*Speaking of family.*

Lola nodded and placed themself close enough to catch Astrid alone for a few moments, but not close enough to encroach.

Just as Lola had guessed, Cale had brought Astrid in for more than the best old-fashioned in the city.

Astrid needed her own confession.

Lola whipped together another old-fashioned, giving Astrid several moments to soak in her own thoughts alone.

"Honey, you most certainly have something strong steeping. Care to spill a little?" Lola offered as they swapped in the new drink for the empty glass.

Astrid absent-mindedly swirled the drink with the stirrer.

It took her several moments to answer.

"He's... changed," she finally said.

Lola shrugged. "Yes."

"It's like I'm dating someone completely different than I fell in..." Her mouth caught on the word.

Yup.

There it was.

"Oof," Lola said, shifting into a casual lean against an arm, eyes trailing along several other patrons.

"Oof?"

"Big oof, honey. So what's being solved?" Lola pretended to study their almond short acrylics.

"What's being solved?" Astrid asked.

Lola turned their gaze back to find the girl stuck halfway through a drink.

"Mhm. Those are the words I said."

The cogs turned behind Astrid's glassy eyes as the drink in her hand lowered slowly.

"I... there's not really a question, is there? I've been trying to answer an assertion, not a question."

Lola nodded slowly with pursed lips.

"Cale always said you were clever. I can't say I've ever had someone reach the proper conclusion in so few words."

It got a smile out of the girl, and she finally met Lola's eyes. "I should... I should just talk to him about it."

This wasn't what Astrid needed.

Diego emerged as they took a few moments to fully absorb the reality of this girl sitting here.

"I'm going to be blunt, Astrid. I gather you like blunt. Are you OK with that?" Diego asked.

Astrid blinked, and her mouth opened as if searching for a script. Diego knew she wouldn't find it.

"No one has... ever offered that. I don't know-"

"No. There are no scripts here. Some people need the scripts, they need the theatrics, the expected. You are *not* 'some people'. Tell me I'm wrong and I'll give you some dumb scripted lesson on love that Cale expects me to dump on you. As much as I'm sure you'd love to listen to Corinthians, I don't think it's the full picture of what you need."

Astrid, notably, did *not* tell Diego they were wrong.

She spoke cautiously, as if navigating her own minefield. "I... thought you... were Cale's friend? Did I misread that?"

"As far as I can tell, one of the closest he has. It's hard to get a full picture with his words alone. I imagine I'm up there with whoever Leo and May are, but definitely not at the levels of Zoey."

Astrid shook her head, though her eyes remained on her drink, "I don't think May is Cale's 'friend'."

"You see my problem. But to your point, yes, I am Cale's friend, and this is why we are having *this* conversation. I want you two to work out. As much as it'll hurt business, I do *not* want him back in here plowing through three cosmos and circling on the same self-destructive thoughts four times a week," Diego said.

Diego re-settled on his forearms and met eyes briefly with Cale across the room, who had emerged from the bathroom and now leaned against the neighbouring wall, acting "nonchalant".

"I... that's... You said it's the things he *doesn't* say. That information is a game to him," Astrid looked up and attempted to meet Diego's eyes. He pointedly looked away. Eye contact was not part of this exchange.

"Everything is a game to him," Diego said.

"That's... I knew that, I think. I... like the games. To some extent."

"Have you two talked about that?"

"No," Astrid said with a sigh.

"What *have* you talked about?"

Astrid slowly stirred her drink. Diego glanced over his shoulder, clocking the various states of guests and staff while she thought. Cale remained leaning, patiently tapping away at some game on his phone.

"It's mostly been him... spoiling me. The dates... are... nice. It felt like playing... catch-up? Compensating for... years of... whatever the hell we were. We talk a lot, but it's a lot of 'what was the core problem', and some topics just feel... taboo?" Astrid finally answered.

Diego nodded, "That's important. Lord knows, that's important. You'll get there, but the more pressing matter is how you keep it from happening again. 'Taboo' is not good. Don't get me wrong, the honeymoon phase is bliss, but it's what comes after that matters."

"That... yes. Agreed. Hence... we need to talk."

Diego gestured between them, "This, right here. You liked it, right? No nonsense, no guessing, just honest takes?"

Astrid nodded. "Yes."

"He wants it too," Diego said.

"But-" Astrid started.

Diego shook his head firmly. "He doesn't know how to. I have been trying to get this into his head for *years* that he just needs to lay *everything* on the table. *Many* people have told him that. But the more people who suggested it, the less likely he was to do it. My daughter is the same exact way, it's *infuriating*. But it's just how they are."

Astrid's eyes narrowed at her drink. "I don't think I've had that problem with him."

"That's not surprising. But I *promise* you." Diego placed a finger firmly on the table. "He has *dreamed* of being able to just speak his thoughts to you. You were starting to say earlier that you didn't know how to do 'brutally honest'? He doesn't either. One of you needs to escape this game of chicken, and he won't listen to me on that topic."

Astrid looked over her shoulder, likely realizing how long Cale had been "in the bathroom". Cale, the fool he was, immediately pretended to take a work call.

"He's not going to come over until I wave him over," Diego stated as Astrid turned back to them.

"May says he's manipulative," Astrid said with a sigh.

Diego couldn't hold back a laugh. "Lord, yes. Absolutely is. I suspect there are very few things in this world he couldn't get if he wanted them. Fortunately for you, he understands boundaries, just not his own. And though I don't think he understands the word fully yet, he does love you."

"What... What isn't there to understand? It's... a simple word, right?" Astrid asked.

"Deceptively *not* simple. I think *you* have a better grasp on the word than *him*. Why can't *you* say it yet?"

"He... makes me happy. Always has, in the most... twisted way..."

"That's a piece of it. Why can't you say the word? Say the quiet part out loud."

Astrid took a sip of her drink, then exhaled.

"It's like he..." Her mouth hung open for a moment. "I keep seeing him change. He does it for me. These last few weeks were... perfect? That's... good, right? I'm supposed to... want a partner who can change?"

"He *has* changed. I would be careful with 'supposed to's, however. Society is *constantly* teaching itself the wrong lessons based on 'supposed to's. The 'scripts' we discussed earlier. What do *you* want?"

Her answer came immediately. "I want *him* to be happy."

Diego closed their eyes, letting the moment seep deep into their soul.

*The Lord has done great things for us, and we are filled with joy.*

Diego straightened up, arching their back in a stretch. Cale lowered his phone and fixed his gaze on Diego, waiting for any signal to return.

"*Is* he happy?" Diego asked.

Diego gave the bar another scan, letting the clinks of glasses and smell of fresh calamari envelop them back into the busy night.

"I don't know," Astrid responded after a moment of thought.

Diego grunted and nodded to Cale, who began to walk over casually as if *anyone* was still buying that he had just been on a business call.

Astrid looked up with parted lips.

"I can trust him, right?" she asked.

Astrid had the right questions.

The rest was up to her.

A hip thrust out, a knowing smile splashed across lips, an eyebrow raised, and Lola emerged. "Oh, honey. I can give you my answer. I couldn't give you yours."

It took *all* of their restraint to hold back the words "and for the love of the lord, find that boy a therapist."

They would get there.

As Cale reunited with Astrid, Lola returned to a steady pace around the bar. Their eyes fell on the still-empty seat before an untouched salad and an empty martini glass.

Carmen...

*Carajo.*

Diego would need to call Lara.

Carmen was going to double down on her own calls, and Diego needed to help fortify her.

But, to Carmen's point... what was the balance between fortification and control?

It was a thought for later.

For now, church was still in session.
