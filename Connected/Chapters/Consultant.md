**Astrid**

Astrid wove around the musty storage room; her box of papers was slowly diminishing, but she still had a ways to go.

The Longing's case had resolved well, so she was in a good mood. The state had clearly been overstepping boundaries on that one, so she hadn't been surprised. Part of her had hoped they would escalate so she could see something a bit more on the appellate side. But they came to their senses at the last minute.

And now she had filing to do.

Tedious, miserable, filing. Bright and early, before the caffeine even set in.

The bureaucracy marched on.

Her phone vibrated. She pulled it out, hoping to get a lunch checkup from Cale. She would welcome a dose of dumb wit to fill the time.

Naomi?

That was the girl they had met in Osaka, right?

Astrid picked up. "Go for Astrid."

"Moshi moshi! Naomi! I was calling to follow up on potential consultation services." The voice carried a distinct level of "cheer".

Astrid switched her to speaker and placed it on one of the shelves. "Oh, yes! We're interested, but will not have bandwidth to support a new client until December, unfortunately."

"Completely understandable, January or February is our current target date for any sort of movement in that area. But I'm glad we have an option. I'll reach out to you when we get closer to the date."

Silence fell for a few moments.

Astrid switched around several boxes, cursing under her breath at the idiot before her who decided N came before M.

"Is that all?" Astrid asked.

Naomi's voice faltered. "Uh… No. Can I confide in you? I need to talk something out, and you're the only friend I have."

Astrid paused, eyeing the phone.

They were friends?

She supposed so, they weren't not-friends.

But only one conversation over sake, and Astrid was her best option for "confiding"?

That was depressing.

"Sure. I have time to kill." Astrid brushed out several cobwebs in the back of a shelf.

The woman didn't continue, but the phone vibrated again. Astrid looked over to see a new message with an attachment.

Astrid's face evolved into a frown as she skimmed the legal document that Naomi sent over.

"I gotta say, Naomi. I can't say any of my friends have come with NDAs attached before."

Astrid let out a sigh with a head shake as the words "Mimi Galaxy" scrolled into view, and the whole picture began to form.

"I know, I know... I'm sorry. I hope you understand." Asuna at least *sounded* apologetic.

"No, I get it. I get it." Astrid signed the doc and fired it back. "OK, Asuna, or still Naomi? What's up?"

"Asuna is good."

The phone went silent.

Astrid finally found the box she was looking for and slipped the paper in. Why were the Ts and Es mixed up? In what backwards world… She started sorting the boxes.

"He kissed me."

Astrid stopped, a folder halfway out of the box. "Is this a 'lawyer up' kiss, or a 'swoon and change your life plans' kiss?"

"Definitely the latter." There was clearly a mixture of delight and anxiety in that voice; it practically seeped through the phone.

Astrid smiled. "I'm assuming 'he' is Hawthorn here. That's exciting. That man's built like a quarterback; I would be lying to say there wasn't a touch of envy here."

"We need to do a cover-up. It sounds like three of the stagehands saw; fortunately, no photos were taken. These NDAs seem to only work on people who actually give a shit about the importance of legally binding contracts."

"Yeah, that is sort of the trick with the concept of the social contract." Astrid stood and stretched, taking a break from the cramped position she had maintained for the last ten minutes.

"But how do you do it?" Asuna asked.

"I'd just sue their ass."

"No, not that. Well, maybe that, let's follow up on that later."

Astrid paused. "To be clear, Asuna, that was not an official stance or legal consultation from me or any employee of Feldman's Legal."

Asuna clarified with a laugh, "Of course. But... That whole... 'love' thing. How do you find time? We're going to be busy with work. There's... a *lot* to do."

Astrid was becoming an entirely different kind of consultant.

It was a timely question.

"If you figure that out, let us know. It's been... It's been hard. A... recurring topic of discussion. I've been used to the words 'emotionally unavailable' my whole life, but the phrase has a whole new meaning now."

Asuna paused, then followed up, "I'm... sorry, you're fighting?"

Astrid paced the small aisle she was in slowly, keeping her head turned to the phone. "No! We don't fight anymore, we talk. But we're… coping. Once we got out of the 'over the top romantic gesture' honeymoon phase and dove into our jobs, it was different. Not bad. Just different."

"So there's not a magical solution."

Astrid stopped several feet away, eyes gazing at a pile of dust in a corner. "Not one we've found. You can try scheduled date nights, but it banks on you both not being emotionally exhausted. And when Cale gets stressed, he gets a bit spontaneous, so... planning is hard. He's been doing a lot of things for himself."

"That sounds... selfish?" Asuna asked.

"No! It's good. He... was bad at focusing on himself before. It's... hard to explain, but it's good, it's just... we're working out the balance there."

Saying the words out loud caused Astrid to exhale a breath she hadn't known she was holding.

"I wish I had advice. I don't really know what to say," Asuna responded.

"Just venting. Nothing to be solved. Call it a consultant's fee."

Asuna went quiet, and Astrid returned to filing papers and folders away. The hum of the ancient yellow light bulb that lit the room filled the otherwise silent moment.

"Am I making a mistake?" Asuna finally asked.

In two swift strides, Astrid was back at the phone, switching it to handheld mode. Her grip was tight; the edges bit into her palm.

"No. If Hawthorn matters to you even a fraction of how much Cale matters to me, you grab onto that love and you never let it go. I promise you, having spent a third of my life pushing it away out of fear, you do not want to miss out on a single minute of an important connection. Especially not for something as stupid as 'time and stress management is hard'."

The light flickered with a soft buzz as Asuna processed the response.

"Thank you, I need to go."

Astrid felt the shadow of a smile play across her lips. "Glad to be of help, my consultation hours are always open. I do hope you know *you're* on call now for the days when Cale decides to up his stupid game."

"I look forward to it—an exclusive backstage pass."

They shared a laugh.

A moment later, the call ended, and Astrid returned to her remaining two folders.

It most certainly had been difficult.

They never seem to stop discovering layers of themselves and each other.

But it was worth it.

As Cale now put it: "iterate, analyze, consult, repeat."

The last folder slipped into place.

The white light of the hallway filled the room as she opened the door, briefly fighting back the yellow glow.

She turned off the light, leaving the stuffy room behind, hopefully for a good while.

**Asuna**

Asuna hung up the phone, staring out over the Tokyo skyline from Hawthorn's apartment.

A light breeze rippled her hair and silk blouse as she collected her thoughts.

Astrid hadn't told her anything she hadn't known; she just needed to hear it said out loud.

Asuna was just scared.

She rolled the hem of her blouse between two fingers while gazing at a not-so-distant building-mounted LED billboard with Mimi's own face staring back.

That day, the stabbing and the anxiety attack had shaken her to the core. For a moment, she had been convinced that dropping Mimi altogether would be the only possible outcome that didn't see her dead from a heart attack.

And she liked Mimi.

Mimi was part of her.

"No regrets" had been easy when those who felt the impact of misinformed decisions were either herself or employees who could just find a new job.

But... now...

The more she was able to think about her... no, *their* future, the more she understood why Hawthorn had such a hard time accepting his past.

Asuna silently opened the sliding door and stepped into the apartment, eyes trained on the back of the man she loved, who sat sprawled with arms hooked around the back of his futon. The American news played softly, though she knew his eyes were staring mindlessly past stock updates.

She stepped softly along the carpet, surrounded by walls covered in paintings of various shapes and sizes. Half of them had been picked out by her, half by Hawthorn.

Hawthorn's head turned as he picked up on her presence, noting her soft smile. "They're open for consultation?"

Asuna shrugged. "They provided some light off-the-record advice. But availability for anything more formal will need to wait; it should line up with the schedule I have in mind."

"Well, that's excellent. What was the immediate advice?"

Asuna tapped the remote, and the TV turned off, before positioning herself with her arms crossed in front of her "less-complicated".

Her phone buzzed.

Her hand twitched towards it, but she caught herself.

"Skill gap analysis," she stated, crossing her arms with a smile.

It was the wrong smile.

Shit.

"That smile. Y'all keep using it. I got suspicions, but I need you to say it."

She averted her gaze to help herself think.

"Work-life balance," she finally said.

"Ten four. Like I said. Forget everything. Let's restart, and I'm going to actually be good at my job and properly listen."

"But you *were* listening, weren't you?"

"Yes. I was listening. But not hearing. Same fucking thing the entire world does. Y'all deserve better than that. You got my full attention, Asuna."

She looked back in time to find him sitting forward, eyes locked on her.

"I don't... where do I begin?"

"The beginning. High school. From 'nearly dropping out' to 'running an entire business'. Y'all took on the full stack of responsibility. Did you *want* to?"

Asuna could remember that first month clearly, the thrill of something so new and fully in her control. "Yes. Well, mostly."

Hawthorn raised an eyebrow, "Talk to me about 'mostly'."

Asuna slumped to the floor and rested her head back against the TV stand. "Some things I just... couldn't motivate myself to do. But it's like they say, 'just push through'... 'Good leadership is down in the trenches', 'you can accomplish anything if you put your mind to it', 'just do it'..."

"Who's 'they'?"

"Everyone. The internet. The self-help books. Leadership books. Shia LaBeouf."

"How much time do you spend procrastinating because you *legitimately* don't want to do it?"

Asuna didn't respond.

That was a hard question.

She wanted to-

"Say the quiet part out loud," Hawthorn interrupted her thoughts.

Asuna sighed. "I *want* to do it all, but never quite... can. It's like I... got good at tricking myself into motivation? Really good at it. I keep a lot of things moving because... then there's always something I might be able to get an angle on. The motion forward, it's... satisfying. Addictive."

They went silent for a moment while Hawthorn collected his thoughts.

How much of the quiet part should she say out loud?

"I..." she paused, second-guessing herself, "Was diagnosed with ADHD when I was in high school."

"Mimi."

Asuna tilted her head back, studying Hawthorn. She had expected a bit more shock. He was nodding his head, arms sprawled against the couch, and looking at the ceiling.

"Vanessa?" Asuna asked.

"Yup."

It all clicked into place.

Asuna continued. "The way they described it... It always felt like half the picture. The advice just never... never worked. I could never figure out if they got something wrong or if *I* was just... wrong?"

"So you compartmentalized."

"Yes."

"Mimi isn't a mask."

"Sometimes she is? But most of the time, she's an outlet. Blurry line."

Asuna felt like her heart was hovering between them. These were truths she had barely acknowledged to herself, but hearing them reflected by someone she trusted... by someone who trusted *her*...

Finally, Hawthorn shifted, leaning forward again. She noted his eyes didn't quite land on her. That should be odd, right? He *always* made eye contact. The intense gaze was his entire thing.

It felt relieving.

"Autism," Hawthorn said simply.

Asuna blinked several times, not really sure how to respond to that assertion.

"Like Rain Man?" she asked.

Hawthorn laughed, "You are the *master* of tact. Yes, Asuna, I have specific sets of underwear set aside for each day of the week and can tell you what the weather was on any given day."

"Can you?"

"Fuck no!"

Asuna groaned. "I don't know shit about the spectrum other than it being a part of my target demographic."

Hawthorn took a deep breath. "Sorry, no. It's not well understood, not your fault. Should probably fix that. Thought for later. Back to business. All that advice, 'just do it', was not designed for *you*. Idiots on the internet do the same fucking thing I did to you. Hear the surface-level, then provide repeatable, dumb phrases like 'work-life balance' that say nothing and give all the wrong contextual answers."

"You did your best-"

"I know I did. You taught me that. Now I'm doing better."

Hearing him say those words caused Asuna's heart to skip.

*She* had helped him.

"You're saying all advice out there is wrong? Hawthorn, are you implying that Shia LaBeouf was *wrong*?" Asuna asked. Her eyes absently trailed a crack in the ceiling.

Hawthorn snorted. "Maybe right for someone. Likely right for someone. Not right for you."

"What does that mean?"

"Getting moving isn't your problem, Asuna. You're not like anyone else I've met."

Asuna grinned. "Your entire revelation is that I'm unique?"

Hawthorn groaned. "If you want to reduce-"

"I'm fucking with you. I know what you meant."

"That one crossed a line," Hawthorn said firmly.

"Understood," Asuna nodded, the wood of the TV stand pressed lightly into the back of her neck.

Asuna could feel the lingering phantoms of the phone buzz from the earlier message. She should check it, right? There was still a lot moving tonight.

Hawthorn cut off the thought. "The aquarium. You did Naomi's smile. Couldn't figure out what you were hiding. Was too caught up in my head. What was going on in there?"

"It was supposed to be relaxing. Right? Doing nothing is relaxing? I spent the entire time feeling like I was going to need a trip to the emergency room."

"That would be the line from the 'contextless bullshit' I referred to earlier," Hawthorn said with a grunt.

Asuna frowned, then hopped to her feet. She began a steady pace around the couch. Hawthorn remained staring ahead. Her legs ached slightly, but the motion still felt calming.

"What did you *want* to do?" Hawthorn asked.

"Vertical research."

"Why didn't you?"

"I was supposed to be relaxing."

"Would research have been relaxing?"

"Yes," Asuna paused her pacing while the word slipped from her mouth.

It was true.

Work *had* always been a relaxant for her. Why had she ever questioned that?

She had an anxiety attack.

"Where's the line?" Asuna resumed her pace, trailing her eyes along several paintings on the walls.

"I think you identified it earlier. 'Just push through it.' That's the stuff you're supposed to delegate. To another person, a service, or software. Your time is limited. More importantly, your mental bandwidth is limited. Sometimes you'll need to do something you don't want to, but in *your* case, delegation is efficiency and not laziness. That is *not* true for many people."

Asuna nodded, passing by between the TV and the couch.

"OK. So let me get this right. Work is good for me. But also, work is unhealthy for me. I clearly can't trust myself to feel when something stresses me. So I need to rely on you to check me on if I'm over- or under-exerting. And it sounds like we need to do the same for you."

She fell onto the couch near Hawthorn, leaning her head back against the fabric.

"High level. Yes." Hawthorn shook the couch with a silent laugh. Asuna felt a grin creep across her lip.

"That's a lot of trust," Asuna said with a resigned sigh.

"It is. You're good at stating facts."

"Easy."

"Easy."

Their hands overlapped between them. Asuna turned her palm up and squeezed. The squeeze was returned.

She felt... safe. That's what this was? Somewhat crazy considering they both acknowledged they were committing to the absurd.

She hoped he felt it too.

Asuna reached over into her pocket and pulled out her phone, physically feeling a thread of mental bandwidth free up with the action. Hawthorn said nothing while she checked the earlier message.

It was a draft from Kenji for a PR statement on the kiss leak circulating.

"Kenji's checking if gaslighting the public is OK."

Hawthorn laughed, "Same thing everyone else fucking does. Why not. That's the script."

Asuna grinned and typed back, *"Send it."*

"We need a contract," Hawthorn stated a few moments later.

"Is that... wrong? I literally don't understand how any of this works. I'm horrified by all of this, and somehow 'fear of getting stabbed again', despite all logical reasoning, is nowhere near the top of the list."

"No, not wrong. Honestly... Laying out expectations sounds... relieving? It's just not something I've done before. I sort of took control of the contract for Vanessa; it worked for her, but I don't think that's what you need."

Their fingers lingered briefly as Hawthorn stood up and moved behind the couch. He stepped to his position by the sliding door, overlooking the city. Asuna hopped up and found her circular pacing motion behind him.

She needed something obvious to start this.

"No kids," Asuna started.

Hawthorn grunted. "Wasn't on the table, not for the foreseeable future. Easy start. I'm remaining as consultant. I know better than to think a C-suite title with implied accountability wouldn't put me back on a bad path. Separation of legal entities makes things cleaner for us, too."

Asuna pulled her phone out and whipped up a new document. Within moments of tapping fingers, the structure of a contract was whipped up.

"You said trust earlier. You... trust?... me?"

Asuna wasn't sure she trusted *herself*.

"Big on the 'trust-but-verify'," Hawthorn said. "You never violated any established terms we've agreed to. Don't think I'll ever be able to leave you alone with a box of takoyaki unless I say 'This is mine'. Lucky for you, I find the guile to be... endearing."

That was fair.

"Guile... *endearing*," Asuna muttered loud enough for Hawthorn to hear as she typed several lines into the document.

She *was* someone who saw something they wanted and would get it. Her mother had always referred to her as "cunning and ambitious". She knew that about herself, and it was unlikely to change.

"I'm going to make decisions you disagree with," Asuna stated.

"Would hope you do."

"Good."

She felt like she was missing an elephant in the room. Something obvious.

"The business." Hawthorn offered, as if reading her mind.

"Separate entity of mutual interest?"

Hawthorn grunted. "Consultant makes legal relationship clean. It's all you."

"Fine. And if I get sick of it and it turns into a stressor?"

"Sell it. Treat it like a hobby. Not a job."

Asuna rolled her eyes. "A hobby with dozens of jobs depending on me."

"Good reason to delegate."

Asuna opened her mouth to make a counterpoint. She found none.

"Fine. Business... separate entity... treat employees as... dispensable."

Hawthorn snorted. "Really? Trying to bait me? Y'all know that's not what I said."

"That's the joke."

"It's not funny when you explain it, Asuna."

She had walked right into that one. Though she noted both of them wore the shimmers of a smile.

After another moment of pacing silence, Hawthorn offered his next term.

"Artichoke. Safe word."

This got Asuna to pause and lower the phone, look over her shoulder, narrow her eyes, and study Hawthorn's back. There was no hint he was joking; he simply maintained his contemplative gaze out the window.

"Hawthorn, what kind of kinks are you-"

"No, not just for sex. If I see you doing something actively destructive to yourself, or if you see me losing myself, or if I'm lost in my head, 'artichoke' is a 'no, really, listen to me'."

Hawthorn looked over his shoulder, meeting Asuna's gaze.

"What, we expect the other person to change on a dime just because we said a word?"

"No, not at all. But from experience, both relationships and work involve some level of boundary pushing, especially early on. It's not a 'change your mind', it's a 'hard boundary, stop until we can process.'"

His face remained resolute, though his eyes averted to a painting on the wall. Asuna briefly followed it to find the Nara Park deer staring back at them.

"*I'm* the one who pushes boundaries, Hawthorn. *You'll* be OK using it?"

"Yes."

The confidence his voice held lifted what felt like a mountain of anxiety off of Asuna's shoulders.

The weight was replaced with a warmth that she was quickly learning to call "love".

She would have an emergency stop button. And the confidence that he had one, too. And it would be *quite* clear when it was used.

*That* was the kind of contract she needed.

"What if I really want to order artichokes?" Asuna asked, beginning to step towards the towering American.

Hawthorn snorted, "Asuna, I'm not going to stop everything to have an existential conversation about our relationship just because you ordered artichokes in a restaurant."

"It's a valid question! I... fine. Context is important."

Her phone came up.

"Hawthorn... has kink... for artichokes," she pretended to type. It earned her another laugh. She loved the sound.

There still felt like another elephant... something that had sat in her head since the first day in his apartment.

Asuna came to a stop in front of him, and he turned to face her.

"Are we dating?"

"Do you care about labels?"

"No?"

"Please don't make me play the 'what are we' game, then. I'm not a fucking high schooler anymore. We are what we are."

"Deal," Asuna stated, extending a hand.

"Deal," Hawthorn stated.

He ignored the hand and picked her up by the waist.

And they kissed.

Their first kiss had been passion-driven and lustful. If it hadn't been in the middle of a stage... well...

But here, now, outside of the outfit, outside of the chaos that was a concert, Asuna got to *truly* kiss the man she loved.

She inhaled, his musk consuming her as every piece of her melted down into the singular point of connection between their lips.

This kiss was different from the one earlier. It didn't channel emotions; it didn't carry hunger, possession, or illogical, emotionally driven commitment.

It was a signature.

A promise.

Asuna leaned back, staring into brown eyes—the only eyes she felt comfortable with staring back.

Hawthorn shook his head slowly with a creeping smile. "You really put those terms into a formal contract?" His words ghosted across her lips.

"You really said, 'Then we burn together?'" Asuna snickered.

Hawthorn groaned, but she noted his body shook them both with a silent laugh. "Can never tell when you're serious."

"Good thing we have a word for that now. I expect the contract to be signed and on my desk by Saturday."

"It's Friday night."

"Better get on that, then."

Asuna leaned in again and kissed deeper this time as a hand ran up the back of her blouse.

Now that the necessary paperwork was signed and filed, the real business could begin.
