**Lara**

Lara smiled, satisfied, at the filled refrigerator. Late afternoon light filled the house through the window shades Lara had thrown open.

For a boy who spent so much time fixated on "being an Adult", Sho's definition of a stocked kitchen had been underwhelming.

Lara was confident that the boy's entire molecular structure was composed of instant ramen, toast, and eggs at this point.

She put aside the plastic bags, closed her eyes, and mentally ran through what she needed to make dinner.

For the last week, Lara had prepared breakfasts with whatever she had on hand. But the level of hospitality that Sho continued to provide called for more.

Lara's cheeks warmed at the memory of the tour Sho had given her of the local area. It was a more suburban piece of Tokyo that she likely wouldn't have made it to otherwise.

Having a personal guide had felt... well... intimate.

The feelings she had for Sho left her conflicted.

Hypothetically, it would be only another three weeks or so until she moved on to her next destination, likely Korea, since it was right there. She was out here travelling, exploring the world.

The money she used was a gift —a privilege that few people in the world would be offered.

The idea of using that privilege for... what? Getting distracted by a crush on a boy she couldn't even talk to? Well, it felt wrong, disrespectful of that gift.

So she was riding it out.

Lara hypothetically had about an hour until Sho got home from the arcade, based on his normal-

The door opened, leaving Lara staring into the brown eyes of a Sho, who, in that moment, evoked flashbacks of a startled deer in Nara Park.

His eyes turned to the skillet and cutting board Lara held in her hand.

"I'm home?" Sho stated.

Lara flashed a smile, which she noted caused him to visibly relax, as always.

The exchange always satisfied her; she knew this boy had demons that he hosted and refused to acknowledge, even to himself. But he deserved to be happy.

Sho's head tilted in question.

"Rice and steak," Lara said in Spanish.

She pointed to the packaged thin beef on the counter, which sat next to a collection of other ingredients: rice, corn, onions, garlic, pepper, and a menagerie of seasonings.

This seemed to only further confuse Sho.

Lara took her phone out. *"Rice and steak. Should be ready in an hour. I wasn't expecting you back so soon."*

Sho studied the message, and gave the various cooking equipment Lara had out a blinking stare as if shocked to find they even existed in the house.

He then typed, *"I want to help."*

Lara contemplated only briefly.

It *would* be good for him to learn to cook properly, or at least get into the habit of it.

And cooking with him *would* be fun...

She nodded eagerly.

Sho dropped his bag full of dance game supplies and moved into the kitchen. He didn't seem sweaty; maybe today was a false start. Lara hadn't seen him play much of the dance games first-hand, but had been quite impressed by his proficiency.

Lara pointed at the onions, making a chopping motion and indicating rough half-moon sizes with her fingers.

Sho, still looking quite off-guard, picked up a knife and cut the first onion in half, with its skin still on.

Lara laughed and shook her head. She grabbed one of the halves, demonstrated removing the outer layer, and rinsed it.

She placed it back in front of Sho.

It took every ounce of mental focus not to think too hard about their skin touching as she grabbed his hands and showed him the proper cutting technique: thumb tucked in, with a chopping motion.

The touch felt sensually taboo, earning a skipped heartbeat. Still, she preferred that over an impromptu trip to the emergency room.

He paused slightly after she let go, hands stuck mid-air where she had left them.

Was he afraid to try himself?

Lara was going to guide him further, but he began to proceed with the appropriate motions.

Satisfied, she typed, *"Did your parents ever cook for you? What would they make?"*

The phone was placed down between them, and Lara moved to mincing garlic; the movements came naturally to her. It was one of the reasons she preferred short-term rentals, budgetary practicality aside. They typically had a full kitchen.

Sho was still cutting onions when she finished the garlic, so she moved on to washing the rice.

Sho emitted a sniffle, and without a second thought, Lara reached across the sink and opened the window to vent out the fumes of the onions.

She looked back while the rice rinsed, reading the phone.

Sho had written, *"The normal stuff. Omurice, onigiri, fried rice, sauteed pork... I never helped, though. I probably should have."*

Once the rinsing was complete, Lara typed back, *"I didn't help too much either when I was younger. Mom and Dad cooked a lot together before they started fighting. I think it's how they met. Once they broke up, they cooked with me. I liked it, it made me feel like we were still a family despite the harsh words that got thrown around."*

It was satisfying to be able to info-dump, knowing he could read it at his own pace.

It let her say much more than she could out loud.

Lara set the phone down between them again and placed the peppers in front of Sho, indicating he should work on them once the onions were done.

She gestured to the peppers and the sink, making small hand gestures which she hoped came off as "wash the seeds away." Sho nodded in understanding.

While he worked on those, Lara whisked together the marinade: garlic, oregano, vinegar, lime, salt, pepper, soy, and oil. She didn't have measurements in her head; it was more of an art at this point.

Once she tossed and set the steaks in the oily mixture, she checked the phone.

Sho had written, *"Fighting parents sounds hard. Probably harder than gone parents. I'm sorry."*

Lara quickly wrote back, then placed the phone back into its communal space. *"No, it's not a competition. I can't imagine what you had to go through."*

Sho was halfway through one of the peppers. Lara pointed at the second pepper and another onion and made a "smaller" gesture with her hands.

Sho hummed in understanding.

Lara collected the sliced onion half-moons Sho had made into a bowl, nodding in approval.

He learned fast; it was satisfying to watch him analyze and adjust cutting strategies.

She found herself grinning at the image of him, bent over the cutting board and ultra-focused on learning how to dice most effectively.

After some rummaging, she found a pot that would work sufficiently for the rice. Oil, oregano, bay leaf, sazon, garlic... medium heat.

Sho was typing on the phone, done with the dicing, so Lara poured the onion and peppers he had just prepared into the pot, and let it heat up. After a minute or so, during which Sho threw on some anime in the neighboring room as background noise, Lara added the rice and let it toast.

She skimmed the previous message on the phone to regain context... missing parents, right.

Sho had typed, *"I like thinking they were transported to another world. I hope they're fighting a demon lord, or farming or something. In reality, I don't think they ever left. They help make sure I make the right decisions."*

Sho had shown her one anime that opened with what he referred to as "truck-kun," which "transported" someone to an impossible, distant land. It seemed that every anime protagonist was whisked off to some alternate world, had a deceased family member, or suffered from some form of amnesia.

Dead family and amnesia just felt like lazy writing to Lara; surely you can find better ways to make interesting characters.

Lara typed, *"I'm sure they're proud of you. I couldn't even imagine needing to own and manage a home,"* and placed the phone down.

Lara pointed to the skillet and the oil as Sho returned. He picked up the implication, heating an oiled skillet while Lara poured broth and corn into the rice pot.

She leaned back against the kitchen counter, mindlessly gazing at the TV, which seemed to show a goggled boy in a green coat chasing a light-brown-haired girl into some other world.

After several moments, Sho placed the phone back down.

He moved the marinated steak into the skillet without being told, which earned him an encouraging smile from Lara.

She shifted over to the phone.

He had typed, *"I just pay bills and mow the lawn. Occasionally, something needs to be replaced. It's not much. It's not like buying plane tickets, learning what is or isn't allowed in other countries, navigating a new city, and ensuring hotels are affordable. Your parents must be proud, that's a lot."*

Lara audibly laughed, getting a cocked head from Sho.

She typed back, *"My mom says I'm wasting my life by not going to college, but I know she just hates that I listen to dad and not her. My dad thinks that anything I do is perfect and worthy of praise. The parent I want to distance myself from pulls me back for her own selfish purposes, and the one I want to spend time with pushes me away with love."*

Sho contemplated the message for several moments, nodded in understanding, and began typing back.

Lara was sure some of that didn't translate well, but she didn't have a better way to word it. At some point, she would need to learn Japanese...

The thought hurt her.

Learn Japanese, to talk to the boy she was going to leave?

It could help her text him.

She would at least stay in contact, right?

Sho was hesitant when passing the phone back. Lara could make out the vulnerability in his face, the way his eyes averted. He briefly pulled the phone back and double checked it before letting Lara take it properly.

What could he possibly say that would cause him to act so nervously?

Lara pointed to the steak while she took the phone. Sho moved to flip it.

He had written, *"Aren't we supposed to have this all figured out? We're out of school. We're adults, right? Why would they let me own a house, or let you just go where you want, if we couldn't be expected to handle it? I've been told I'm brilliant and have such potential, but all I have used it for is hitting buttons in response to lights on a screen."*

Lara read the message several times. It struck at an insecurity she never let herself admit.

She typed back, *"I left home with the goal of 'discovering myself'. All I've learned is that I dread when the money runs out. I delay it by working. But it will run out. And then I'll need to move home with one of my parents and figure out what to really do with my life. Mom is right, I'm wasting time and money."*

While Sho read and responded, Lara lowered the temperature on the rice now that it was boiling. The steaks were moved to a plate, and she placed another plate over them to contain the heat.

With a little bit of added oil, the onions and peppers Sho had cut were added to the skillet.

Lara nodded to the softening vegetables as Sho handed her the phone. He understood and moved to keep an eye on them.

He had typed, *"It would be nice if someone made an instruction manual. Everyone else figured it out. It can't be just us making it up as we go, can it?"*

Lara paced around the room, thinking through the assertion.

She had conducted extensive research during her travels. Family and the transition from "youth" to "adult" varied widely across cultures. Japan was undergoing a cultural transition that seemed to have people of Sho's age demographic at its center.

Lara's grandparents had giant families that lived together. Marriage was what prompted a separation into a distinct household, not reaching some magic-number age.

Lara's parents, who only ever wanted one kid, adopted the North American and European standard: Lara was expected to be an independent individual once she turned eighteen.

It sort of made sense when considered in the context of housing markets, differences in perspective on "individual freedom", and the differing views on family versus career.

Granted, American cities had their own newly evolving problems with the recent housing market. It seemed the US's solution to "family vs individualism" in regions of high population had turned into "make your own family of individuals" or "work yourself into a pulp to afford your own place".

For Sho...

Japan still had strong family-oriented values. But the recent shift had been toward adopting the more "independent at 18" approach. It was a strange balance between family- and society-oriented mindsets, while also demanding strong self-motivation and independence.

All of that to say, Lara didn't really have answers for Sho, considering the world didn't really have good answers for itself.

She wrote back, *"I think many people would want those instructions, not just us. We just do our best, and that's the best we can do."*

Sho studied the response reverently.

Lara once again hoped that the translator had done an ok job of carrying the sentiment.

The room had long since filled with the glorious scent of onions, peppers, and garlic.

Lara felt at home.

It was a strange thought, one that scared her.

Her answer to the questions and uncertainty that Sho shared had always been to just keep wandering, sailing, with the faith that she would figure something out. She had been raised religious, and while much of it didn't stick, the concept of good faith did.

The implication of an anchor in the form of a friendly and warm house full of nostalgic smells and sounds, and a welcoming smile of a boy who had an active interest in seeing her safe and cared for... maybe "conflicted feelings" was an understatement.

While Sho typed, Lara added some garlic and vinegar to the cooking onions. She cut back the heat on the rice as she added steaks back to the glazing mixture in the skillet.

Sho passed the phone back and began to gather plates and silverware for the two.

He had typed, *"It is weird. It feels better knowing that you don't have answers either."*

Lara flashed him a smile, which he returned.

She agreed.

Being lost together did feel better than being lost alone.

It wasn't long until the two found themselves on the couch over a hot meal, watching a silly anime about a young couple collecting memories for a girl who had lost hers.

They watched in silence, occasionally making jokes through the phone translator. Sho would identify cross-overs with other anime made by the same studio. Lara would poke fun at silly tropes that kept popping up.

The tapping of fingers against the phone and the strange anime noises played out against a backdrop of clinking knives, chopsticks, and forks.

It was absolutely a waste of time.

They accomplished nothing.

But Lara wouldn't trade the moment for the world.

They likely went to bed significantly later than they should have; the light began to peek through the various windows of the house.

Lara found herself lingering at her door with her hand on the doorknob. She could hear that Sho had not opened his door either.

An unspoken and near-instantaneous conversation passed between them; they didn't even need to meet eyes.

It had become clear, over the course of the last week, that Lara was not the only one pining.

But what could they do with that?

It would be irresponsible for either to push or ask for more, given the many implications for both of their lives.

They had so much to figure out about themselves, their own aspirations, their own goals. How could Lara possibly encroach on Sho's journey with her own?

She wasn't ready to stop moving, and she couldn't expect him to want to start moving.

He had his games, his escape; games that most certainly would not exist in the majority of the world.

He had his house, fully paid off, and in a good neighborhood.

Lara had her own unprecedented gift from a parent; she understood the implications of abandoning such a thing.

So, despite Lara's greatest desires to walk over, join Sho, and truly start pushing the limits of what they were allowed to do as "adults"...

Lara squeezed her eyes shut as her heart attempted to rip free and join Sho.

They silently turned their respective doorknobs and entered their rooms, alone, not even sharing a glance.

She couldn't.

It was the responsible decision.
