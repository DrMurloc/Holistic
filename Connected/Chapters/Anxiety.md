**Asuna**

Asuna glanced over her shoulder, heart pounding. She couldn't seem to find a populated street to save her life… possibly literally. Why was Shibuya so empty tonight?

The man had clearly followed her on four laps of her apartment building. Her mind raced, he couldn't have recognized her, could he? What would he want if he had?

A laundromat, with people. She ducked in, trying to hide slightly in the back, and glanced down at her dead phone.

“Excuse me, I'm sorry. Could I borrow one of your phones? I have a bit of an emergency.” A part of her brain was holding back on the urgency, this couldn't become public news. She caught a glimpse of the man walking by outside. She held her breath.

The two people looked at each other. Asuna's heart sank as they finished packing their dried laundry and left. A soft “please, wait” died on Asuna's lips. The man outside backed up, and turned his head to look directly at her. He smiled, a slow and wide smile.

“No…” She desperately looked around, no doors, no restrooms to lock herself in, nothing. She had trapped herself.

The door opened, and the man walked in. There was… desire on that face.

“No… no no no…” She looked around for any sort of weapon.

“Mimi Galaxy.” He said, licking his lips, “I've been to all of your shows. I was one of your first fans! You probably don't remember me.” Her mind was trying to plan out an escape, she would have to throw him off balance somehow.

“Please, sir, I don't know what you're talking about. I'm Naomi. Just a college student.” Had her wig slipped? A contact fallen out? How did he know her? Did he just know her face that well?

“You don't need to pretend with me. You'll have an eternity with me, and the others.” He pulled a knife out. This was infinitely worse than she had thought, the blood drained from her face, all of her plans of escape felt impossible.

The reality sank in. She had wasted her life alone, not letting anyone else in to help. She had raised her mother's empire back from the grave and made it her sole prerogative. And in that moment she realized she didn't care. The only thing that stuck out to her was how distinctively lonely she felt. She had lived alone, and would die alone. Nothing but a headline and a tragic story to her name.

“No, I'll do anything you want. Please… you don't need to do this…” She looked out the window, a broad shouldered foreigner was outside reaching for the door. She screamed as the stalker dove at her with knife brandished.

**Hawthorn**

Hawthorn heard the scream, he turned his head into the laundromat. His brain didn't need to process much to react: small girl, bloody knife. His laundry dropped as he threw open the door and dashed in, grabbing the creep with the knife by the shirt and throwing him across the room. The knife skidded across the floor, leaving a small line of blood.

The creep attempted to get up, but found a very firm foot launched into the side of his head. He slumped over, unconscious.

His phone was out in an instant… 119. He quickly grabbed a white shirt from his laundry outside while the phone rang, turning to the girl.

“Hello, emergency services, what is your emergency?” A woman on the line said. He placed the phone on speaker next to the girl.

The cut wasn't too deep, but it was bleeding. A slightly deep gash across her stomach, likely not enough to cause real damage. She was in shock though, her eyes wavering.

“Attempted murder, armed. Attacker is unconscious, victim has a long laceration on the stomach. I'm applying pressure. Laundromat in the Nakamoto Building, Shibuya.”

He lifted her shirt enough to get access to the wound, her eyes darted to his hands, “I'm… Naomi…” she said in Japanese, very dazed. Her eyes shut.

“Emergency vehicles are en route.” The voice said from the phone.

“Hawthorn. Hey, no sleeping. I need you awake. You're going to be fine, but stay awake.” He barely knew what he was doing, tapping into too many ER shows from a previous life. He had no practical experience. He bunched the shirt up, digging it into the wound slightly, and applied firm pressure.

The girl’s eyes opened, focusing on the wound briefly, she gasped. He had to prevent her hand from moving to it.

“On me. Focus on me.” He said, her eyes flickered to his.

“You are… American?” She said, in English.

“Unfortunately. Tell me about you, I need you to keep focused and engaged.”

“Naomi Yomimoto. Nineteen years old. Studying architecture at The University of Tokyo.” She said, that was a practiced response. He didn't care, she could have her secrets. He just needed her talking. His calmness failed to reflect his inner panic.

“Favorite food?” He heard emergency sirens approaching, colorful lights filled the room.

“The… They can't find out… insurance rates… margins are thin as is…” She mumbled in japanese. Insurance rates? It had to be the shock.

He smacked her lightly on the face and her eyes focused again.

“Naomi. I'm Naomi.” She smiled faintly.

“So I've heard.” He smiled back. Her eyes went wide as the paramedics and police filed in, the creep was promptly handcuffed.

“Asuna. I'm Asuna Morita. Please find me, Hawthorn. I'll tell them you're family.” Her eyes seemed to grab onto his with desperation. He semi reluctantly released pressure on the bloodied shirt. Their eyes remained locked as the paramedics lifted her onto a stretcher and carried her out.

“I liked that shirt…” he muttered while standing up. His eyes turning to the police that approached him.

They questioned him and he answered directly, giving them his old business card. He explained his visa would expire soon, so he may become difficult to contact.

His mind was stuck on that mystery girl though. She lied about her name… it was a prepared lie. But then she gave him her real one? Was she still in danger?

The police began to leave, the crime scene seemed very simple and straightforward so there was not a significant amount of forensics to be had.

“Excuse me.” Hawthorn said in Japanese. The closest police woman turned. He continued, “Where is the closest emergency room?”

“They will be taking her to Tokyo Medical, but they won't allow anyone but immediate family until visiting hours.” She turned and left with the others.

“Well.” He grimaced, “Apparently I'm family.”

Tokyo Medical. He was all too familiar. The last time he had left there, alone and fairly broken, he had thought would be the last time he would ever need to see the blasted place. The universe seemed to have different plans.

He stopped on the way out, grabbing his dirty laundry. He paused, looking wistfully in at the laundromat. He didn't need to get involved. He had his own complicated life. Would adding whatever drama this girl was looking to introduce him to actually help?

He knew he couldn't say no though. Vanessa had said it was his most charming trait. But he really wanted to just do his laundry then go back up and take stock of how poorly his investments were going.

He growled at himself. Fine. For Vanessa. *She would have never let me hear the end of it if I walked away.*

So it was that he found himself two hours later waiting in a familiar hospital lounge. He was sure the nurses would recognize him. Even if they didn't, was he really going to pretend a tall broad shouldered Texan was the family of a small Japanese girl?

This was stupid. He saved her life and got professionals involved. That was the move that had carried him so far in his career: tactically solve the problem, then consult and defer to people much better equipped than he. Of course it was a career on… pause.

He stood up to leave, grabbing his long black coat and throwing it over his shoulders. Laundry would have already been done by now.

The double doors opened, and a nurse he did not recognize stood there, studying him.

“Mister…” She began in english.

“Morita. Hawthorn Morita.” He grimaced at the lie. He had told worse, but not by much.

The nurse looked him up and down.

“Adopted brother. It’s… a complicated story.” He added, in Japanese. She clearly wasn’t buying it.

“Well, she demanded you be let in. I can’t say I’ve been threatened like that by a patient before.” The nurse turned and started to walk. *Threatened?* Hawthorn jogged to catch up. She led him through a maze of rooms, his heart sank as they approached the far corner of the building. Of course. The *exact* same room.

He took a deep breath, and looked to the ceiling. *Can you chill with the sick sense of humor?* He screamed internally at the universe. He was not religious, but he did have a deep belief in the holistic nature of the universe. The way things worked out so often, they just seemed too perfect and ironic to be a raw coincidence.

Well, except for his wife getting terminally sick only two weeks into their marriage. That one had proven to be quite the stickler for his beliefs.

The door opened. He swore he could see a flash of Vanessa laying on that bed in the hospital gown, grinning at him widely. But it was the girl. Her short amber hair fell just above her shoulders, brown eyes narrowed in a seeming unbreakable focus at a phone, just the slightest frown. She typed furiously, the soft taps only matched by the steady rhythm of the heart monitor hooked up to her. He was legitimately impressed with the air of efficiency she carried.

“Miss Morita?” The nurse said. The girl practically threw her phone, her look of focus faded and was replaced with a bright smile.

“Brother!” She exclaimed, throwing her arms out.

“Sister! I came as soon as you texted, are you ok?” Hawthorn wasn’t sure who they were performing for, the nurse clearly wasn’t buying it. He walked up and gave her what was likely the most awkward shoulder hug in the history of mankind.

“Some creep tried to stab me! It was the craziest thing!” She said, with a very obvious fake pout.

The nurse interjected, “Look, whatever… this… is, I don’t care. Spare the energy, don’t do anything to disturb your stitches. Another nurse will be in about an hour to take vitals.” She promptly turned and left. Both Hawthorn and Asuna watched her leave. As soon as the door was closed he backed up.

Hawthorn sighed but kept his grin pasted on, tapping into a personality from a past life. This girl likely needed support, not the vat of depression that consumed him, “You called, I answered. How can I be of assistance?”

The girl looked away slightly, “I just… didn’t want to be alone.” Her melodic voice seemed to drop a pitch for a moment.

“You’ll find I can be pretty good at existing in a space if that’s all you need, I’ve been told I’m annoyingly good at it.” He took a seat nearby. It got a chuckle out of her.

“Thank you, it means a lot. I don’t really have anyone else I can call right now… And for some reason facing your death makes you desperately want to not be alone.” She met his eyes, a touch of vulnerability showing. *This girl threatened a nurse?*

He desperately hoped this wasn’t a “fall in love with your savior” thing. She was too young for him. While he had been a protector his whole life, his romantic life was the one place he needed someone self sufficient. And this girl, nineteen she said? Was not giving off an air of “powerful” and “motivated”.

“Family? Friends? Work?” He asked.

“Passed away. No. It’s… complicated.” She responded.

“Huh. Same, actually. I’m sorry though, at your age losing family must be especially hard.”

“It was… hard. But, it’s always about the next step. No looking back.” She beamed.

“Wise beyond your years.” He stood up and took his coat off, concluding he would be here for a while. He walked directly to the coat closet and hung it up, then grabbed pillows out of a different closet and converted the couch to a bed. Asuna followed him with her eyes, the monitor seeming to keep a steady rhythm to the movement.

“You are very familiar with this hospital.”

“She and I go way back. Story for a different time. How about you? You mentioned college?” He laid back on the pseudo-bed, eyeing the ceiling. His feet hung off the end, he was simply too damn big for this country.

“Oh! Yes! College, I’m studying Architecture! I’m a big fan of Kenzo Tange, his blend of modernism with Japanese traditions is truly amazing!” More practiced lines.

“Is that what Naomi thinks or Asuna?” Hawthorn shot a glance over to her, noting her freeze momentarily. The heart monitor briefly spiked, betraying her otherwise calm but cheerful demeanor.

“I… damnit.” Her voice dropped in pitch again, losing some of its loftiness but still maintaining the crisp tone. It was a few moments before she said anything, when she did her voice was back to normal. Or was this the fake voice?

“I can at least get out of this silly thing.” He turned his head as she pulled a wig off, revealing long black hair, then removed contacts revealing striking light grey eyes. Hawthorn sat up, now *those* were unique.

“I’m beginning to think that the attack tonight wasn’t random, what secret backstory could turn such a young girl as you into a knife magnet?” He leaned forward with a smile, resting his chin on a fist.

“I promise I had never met that creep before in my life.” She said. She either had a very good poker face or was telling the truth, the monitor continued without breaking pace. But it had been established that both of them had their own masks they wore.

Hawthorn contemplated. She disguised herself as another equally young and attractive girl. The colored contacts he could get to avoid standing out, but the wig? How could it be that a layer of a mystery girl gets peeled back and only be replaced with more mystery?

“Yakuza crime lord?” He asked, eyes narrowing. She held up a hand to cover a smile. He stood up and began to pace at the foot of the bed.

“I mean of course it would be hard to picture Tokyo’s greatest luchadora to be so caught off guard.”

“Luchadora? Your next jump was luchadora?” She snickered. The laugh egged him on, he would have fun with this. And it lightened her mood, which delighted him.

“No you’re right, that would be an insult to Japan’s top secret weapons designer for Japan’s Maritime Self-Defense Force.”

“You know I’m not going to confirm if you get this right, right?” Her smile widened though.

“Ah, but there’s something to get right?” He glanced knowingly over his shoulder at her, before continuing to pace. She didn’t respond.

“Of course it would be statistically unlikely for anyone to possibly guess that they had saved the last heir to the long lost Japanese Empire, come to return the country to its former greatness.”

A full blown laugh, “You are ridiculous.”

“Not as ridiculous as the idea that I just happened to bump into *the* biggest international J-Pop sensation.” He laughed. Nearby the heart rate monitor's beeping increased in pace.

“Shit.” He heard her say softly. He looked over to catch Asuna studying the hospital wristband she wore, any semblance of a smile gone.

“Wait-” his statement was interrupted as the door opened, he recognized this nurse. *Crap.* He slid over to the window, facing out. He could hear Nurse Ito go through the normal motions of vitals check.

A J-Pop idol? With that voice he could surely picture it. The crazed attacker was some stalker? Why did she need the wig and contacts? Didn’t idols wear wigs and contacts on stage? Unless her normal persona was part of her public image too, meaning Naomi was her *extra* undercover persona. It all made sense. Of course her reaction all but verified it for him already.

“Mr. Morisset?” Nurse Ito said.

“Shit.” Hawthorn muttered, turning to face the nurse.

“Are you-”

“He’s my brother. Adopted”. Hawthorn heard Asuna say in her lighter voice, although it was missing the enthusiasm.

“It’s complicated.” Hawthorn offered.

“In any case, I hope you are doing well. The loss of a wife I’m sure never fully heals.” The nurse placed a hand on his shoulder briefly on the way to a closet for an extra pillow.

“Thank you, it means a lot. Truly.” Hawthorn continued to look away. He didn’t want to confront this, not now, and *especially* not here. He had a year of avoiding the topic with himself, he wasn’t going to change that for some stupid teenage idol that needed more friends and a better security detail.

The nurse eventually left. Leaving both of them in silence, the moments measured out by the steady pace of her heart. It was likely ten minutes until Asuna spoke up, in her lighter voice still “I’m so sorry. Could you tell me about her?”

Hawthorn couldn’t do this. He couldn’t keep that mask on. Couldn’t pretend. He turned to walk out of the room, he needed to escape this damned hospital.

As he reached for the door Asuna called out, in her more natural lower pitch, “Hawthorn. Please.” He shook his head but turned to look at her. She held out her phone to him, a flat expression on her face. “Sign this.”

He hesitantly walked over and took the phone up. His eyes narrowed as he read, “Is this an NDA?”

She crossed her arms and leaned back, “Look, the increase in risk factor is already going to murder me on Insurance. Each person who knows my Identity and isn’t read in is an added liability that our thin margins just can’t account for. I already had to let go of two roadies last month and it broke my heart.”

Hawthorn read through the document, “Mimi Galaxy?” He had to fight a snicker.

“I know. I hate it.” He determined he preferred this version of her voice much more. It was so much more authentic.

“You hate it?”

“No… hate was a wrong word. I’m just… tired of it.”

He continued to read.

“Note that it includes non disclosure of my identity as Asuna, you are to refer to me publicly as Naomi to anyone not identified by me or an executive of Project Galaxy as having been read in. Both Asuna and Mimi Galaxy are considered intangible assets, and risks or damages to those assets will be pursued to the full extent of the law.” *This* wasn’t practiced, she was speaking this out of pocket. Who the hell was this girl?

“If you’re not familiar-” She started when he didn't respond after a few moments.

“I’m very familiar, trust me. Have you had this reviewed? The terminology on this leaves it open for me to speak via a third party about you outside the country of Japan. I believe you didn’t mean to use the term region there, which you had defined earlier to be specifically Japan.” Hawthorn signed and passed the phone back to her.

She narrowed her eyes, reading through the section he had scrolled it to. She nodded slowly, before proceeding to tap and scroll through several actions on her phone.

Hawthorn re-evaluated her. *This* girl would threaten a nurse to get what she wanted. Why had she wanted him? He lost track of what layer of mystery he was on.

“Do you… you manage your own agency?” He guessed.

“I *am* the agency.” She said without looking up. He felt his heart beat for the first time in over a year.

“You are not nineteen.” He guessed again.

“It’s funny how much people ignore the fact that their favorite idol has been nineteen for ten years.” She answered flatly, still on her phone. He laughed, a real laugh. It caught him off guard.

He leaned against the door frame, “You know you can counter much of those insurance hits by contracting a security consultant. It might be slightly more expensive than the insurance but you’ll get super valuable advice like ‘do not walk around alone at night’.”

“I see security consultants are on the victim blaming train.” She smirked, finally putting her phone down and meeting his eyes.

“It’s kind of their job.” Hawthorn took a seat next to the bed.

“Would you qualify as a security consultant?” She raised an eyebrow.

“Sorry, my visa expires in a week. I will need to leave the land of the rising sun. A short term contract would not qualify me for renewal. Your attempt to keep me around is duly noted though.” He leaned forward.

“What was your previous title?” She asked.

“When did this become an interview?”

“When you, without hesitation, kicked the living shit out of my potential murderer.” She grinned. He studied that grin, mirroring it. He found himself not needing to act, not needing to pretend. This wasn’t a mask. He was enjoying himself, this girl had managed to go from least interesting person in the world to the center of his attention in a single NDA.

“And what am I being interviewed for?” He asked.

“What was your previous title?” She repeated.

“Avoiding questions?”

“Yes. What was your previous title?”

“Chief Information Security Officer. Although the title was rather short lived, a mere six months.” He said with a touch of pride.

She moved her hand to her stitches, her eyes melting to a fake wide and vulnerable state, “Hawthorn I could never afford a CISO, my heart… my wounds…” She pretended to faint.

Hawthorn doubled over with laughter, she was clearly manipulating him and he loved it. She had him on the hook and she knew it. He wasn’t sure if he was upset about it.

“Look, I don't care about pay. I just don’t want to leave Japan. And I’m finding more and more I’m interested in supporting whatever J-Pop pyramid scheme you have running here.” He admitted.

“We’ll figure out something.” Asuna agreed. Their eyes met, and they just existed with each other for a moment. He thought back to the image of her desperately watching him while being brought into the ambulance.

“Why *did* you ask for me to be here?”

“I didn’t lie. I didn’t want to be alone. I was scared. My entire legacy, my mother’s legacy passed down, ended in a single dumb night-time stroll. How *stupid* was I?”

“Your mother's legacy?”

Asuna looked away.

“Ah. Right. Sorry.”

“No! It's been twelve years. I've had my time to come to terms with their passing. You were right though. Losing parents at seventeen, and being left with a financial advisor and a slowly failing business sustained purely on twenty year out of date merchandise… not even a last ‘good luck’. It *was* hard.” Hawthorn did a double take. A *failing* business? She recovered a *failing* business at *seventeen*?

“And you just… what, got in the saddle and got that baby back on the trail?”

“What else could I have done?” Her face twisted in confusion like it was the most absurd question in the world.

“I think the average person would have sold off the remainder of the business and used the money to have a very cushy start to a life in college.”

“Well, call me stupid but cushy sounds boring. I had a legacy to honor.” She leaned back again, staring at the ceiling.

Hawthorn's heart fluttered. *Wait, what? No. No we aren't doing that.* He pushed the feeling back. He was in business analysis mode, no room for falling in love with mystery girls.

“What is the portfolio now?” He asked.

“Merchandising provides the backbone, oddly enough digital collaborations for video games are the best margin since they just need permission for my or mom's image and do the hard parts themselves. But ultimately all of it comes back to me. If I don't get on that stage, we fade out of relevance. It is absurd how competitive the market is for being the most relevant celebrity is. Queen bitch one day, forgotten the next because some shark girl said the letter ‘a’.”

Hawthorn's mind wrapped itself around this. That industry sounded miserable. Granted the marketing industry had been similar. Some shiny new technology would come out and regardless of how dumb and untested it was, if you didn't adopt it and stay relevant you would just straight up lose clients.

“Your team?” He asked.

“Mostly contracted hands for event set up and tear down. I technically have a manager for connecting with venues, but I probably don't let them do their job enough. A small three person design team, and a web designer.”

“Finances, song writing, performance, logistical coordination, communications with partners and collaborators, public relations, human resources… do you even have a management team? Who manages salaries for your team”

Asuna just pointed to herself with a flat look, still watching the ceiling. Hawthorn stared, unwilling to believe it.

“You need to do less.” He asserted.

“I already told you, I can't do lazy.”

“No, you don't understand. You yourself stated, you're a centralized point of failure for this entire business. And there is, no matter how impossibly driven you are, a finite amount of things any one person can actually *do*. It's not lazy, it's energy efficient.”

This got her to look back. “But it's already so precarious, others will make mistakes, they will fail.”

“Yes. Failure is part of the process. They're a strategic investment, not a tactical resource. Bumpy start, long term value.” He smiled, he was actually full-on enjoying this. He hadn't realized how much he had missed work until now.

This seemed to click in her, “And if we fail too much and it boils over?” She said hesitantly.

“You need to compartmentalize the growth. Baby steps. I've had insight into small companies that have grown too fast for potential acquisition, you don't want that chaos. Right now you know someone who should be doing more, and an aspect of the business you hate running.” He nodded to her.

Without hesitation, “Kenji. I should let him manage the others.” She nodded for a moment, then her eyes went wide, “Wait, really? I can just… do that? Won't I need to pay him more?”

“Have him back off the venue collaboration, he's probably frustrated if that's his job and he hasn't been able to do it to be honest. If you enjoy that aspect, you should do it.”

She studied him, her eyes seeming to bore into his skull, “This is just a game of chess to you, isn't it? Just casually moving people into a position to be successful. There is no way I can afford you.”

“Long term consultant. I'll form my own GK. Pick a price.”

“Why would you do that?”

“I don't want to be alone.” Hawthorn smiled. She laughed, and nodded, understanding. They sat there for a few moments in silence, Hawthorn's heart thudded in his chest. He was almost worried she would hear it.

The heart rate monitor picked up in pace again. Hawthorn turned to study it, donning a bemused grin.

“Damnit! This is unfair!” Asuna exclaimed, crossing her arms and looking away.

“For full transparency, me too.” He offered. Her eyes darted back to him, her face softening, then fading into a laugh. Hawthorn echoed the laugh, the moment seeming so absurd.

“Am I just hiring you into a sexual harassment lawsuit waiting to happen?”

“No quid pro quo and we are good. Although… *there's* a fantasy I had never expected to have…” He pretended to look off into the distance, day dreaming.

She laughed harder, “Oh please stop, ow, ow.” She breathed deeply, fighting the laughter, her hand on her stitches.

“OK. No joking. Business. Tell me about your competitors.” Hawthorn said. He sat back, soaking in joy he had not felt in a year. The last time he was in this room he was so helpless, a feeling he avidly hated. His whole life built around a commitment to support and enabling, and he had been able to do nothing for the one person who had truly mattered to him.

And here, smack dab in the very same bed, was a girl who needed exactly the right nudges that he could provide to protect her from herself. The universe indeed had a sick sense of humor.

Hours later, Nurse Ito stepped into the room to check on the patient. He was met with a curious sight. The arm of the bed had been lowered, Mr. Morissett had his head resting in the patient's legs, their hands overlappinh slightly. Both he and the patient slept peacefully with smiles on their faces.

The nurse shook his head with his own smile. The irony was not lost on him. He wasn't quite sure what transpired here tonight but he was happy to see this man, who had been through more than anyone ever should, happy.

He left silently, careful not to wake them. They both deserved a long and deep rest.
