# Act 2 - Regrets

## Anxiety

**Uni**

And for our final destination–well, final unless Lara decided to pop off into Nepal or Kenya or something–we have Tokyo.

For those unfamiliar with what is arguably the most technologically advanced city in the world, it is a never-ending barrage of digital billboards, colorful characters, and uniquely shaped buildings interspersed with meticulously planned gardens that pop with every shade of green, pink, white, and blue.

Shibuya, where we find ourselves, is vibrant with activity. It’s a section of the city that was quite decidedly taken over by younger generations. A sweeping “this, this section is ours, keep your quiet shame and fear of tattoos out.” It, admittedly, has no puma overlooking it–nittany or not–but it does have a statue of a rather devoted dog, a notorious nightlife for those who just want to escape into the absurd, and an intersection that is so packed by pedestrians that multiple cafes existed purely for the act of spectating the continuous cross-road pilgrimage.

Tucked to the far corner of this electric-infused city sit residential buildings that gave up on quite reaching the same height as their more boisterous cousins. The streets here are quiet, and nights had a tendency to sweep in as a blanket to protect the residents from the eternal glow of the rest of the city.

Nestled into the bottom of one of these rather unassuming buildings is a laundromat. Clean, linoleum floors, lined with several vending machines displaying detergents, canned coffee, or canned soups, and several small wooden benches.

And in the corner of that laundromat, we find Asuna.

**Asuna**

Asuna let out a string of curses as her phone died.

The whirr of the dryer in the artificially lit laundromat was almost unnerving against the otherwise completely silent, still air. The darkness of night pressed up against the glass, shrouding the streets of Shibuya from sight.

Asuna’s arms were crossed, eyes narrowed at the long wooden bench in the center of the room. Her amber wig itched the corners of her scalp; it had been a long day. She pushed back the desire to rip it off, though. She knew the anxiety of having it off would be worse than the itch.

The stillness of the room cowered from her tense shoulders; it didn't dare intrude on the turbulent waterfall of endless planning that streamed through her mind. It had been a decade-long journey to the Tokyo Dome, and she had every intent of making it count.

Less than a month.

Shit.

She stared at the blank phone.

She could just run upstairs to get a phone charger, she supposed. No one saw her come in, and she was in disguise. There’d *probably* be no panty raiding for Mimi’s-

The door opened.

Asuna forced her shoulders down and softened her face into a smile.

Naomi. You are Naomi. Quiet college student.

She registered the corner of her vision.

Suit. *Cheap* suit. Bowl cut. Glasses. Low thirties? Hard to tell. Definitely within her target demographic.

“The sakura festivals were good this year, weren’t they?” he offered.

“Oh, yes, indeed. Quite nice. I wish they held them for longer. The blossoms are still so pretty!” Asuna beamed.

Let him keep digging. Did he want something, or was this small talk? She definitely was not convinced he had anything she wanted.

“I managed to make it out to Kyoto for a day. Quite beautiful. You would never guess who had a surprise performance the other week!” He noted.

What weird way to phrase that.

The answer was her, of course. Or rather, Mimi. Charity event. Cultural preservation of temples. She expected the invested resources to pay back ten-fold, and if some ancient statues got some funding in the process, well, that was just efficient for everybody.

Regardless, she had a fan in the room with her. Could be good feedback. When life gives you lemons, you slap on some cute cartoon girls for branding and find a market for the seeds and zest.

Time to squeeze the juice.

“No, who? Were they any good? How was the sound?” Asuna gave a precisely measured head tilt and the widest innocent eyes she could muster.

“Oh, you don’t need to play coy with me, Mimi,” the man replied with a smile.

Okay.

Not the first time a fan recognized her in disguise. Let him think he won something.

“So how was the sound?” She repeated, returning his smile.

“Oh, you always sound perfect. Your voice is that of an angel’s. I always stand in the front row so I can hear you. Sometimes I can smell you,” he stated.

Maybe this was a rotten lemon. Not the weirdest interaction with a fan she’d had.

“Smell me?” Asuna brought a finger to her lips.

“Yes, Mimi. Your sweat. Your perfume. The way your breath sometimes carries down into the front row as your skirt whips around.”

Asuna groaned somewhere in the back of her mind, but Mimi kept smiling.

Still not the weirdest interaction she’d had.

“Oh, you know how worked up I get for you all! I just lose myself in the music and the sound of your energy!” She winked and raised a peace sign.

To her satisfaction, the man clapped enthusiastically. Her eyes scanned desperately out the window for anyone passing by. It was night and remote, but it was still Shibuya. *Someone* had to pass by. Right?

“Yes! That’s why I followed you! It took a long time to map you here. You’re so sneaky.

Mimi’s smile faltered; Asuna barely kept her pitch. “Map?”

“Yes! Getting an AirTag in your backpack was-”

"What the fuck do you want?" Asuna snapped loudly in her natural lower tone.

He simply laughed, and the grin broadened to the edges of his face.

“Language, Mimi. You don’t remember me?” He said.

Asuna stared.

Granted, she was bad with faces. And the man didn’t have a memorable hairstyle. But did he really expect her to remember every fan?

“No?” Asuna said. Maybe she should have lied.

He paused, and his smile broke. “I’ve been around since day one. I was your first autograph.”

She should definitely lie. Until she could get the police. That’s the move.

“I don’t remember that.” She admitted.

Shit.

She watched his face go through several emotions, only one or two of which she could name. She used the time to take inventory of her surroundings. If she made it outside, she could definitely run faster than him. She was fit. He likely was not. Most of her fans were not.

Just buy time. Someone will come in.

“I’m sorry, I could-” she started.

“Well. I suppose I was prepared for this,” he said.

And a knife came out.

Asuna's blood froze, eyes locked on the silvery blade.

Shit.

“I've been there since day one, and now I get to be here on the last day. *Romantic*, isn't it? Poetry that writes itself."

Shit shit shit shit.

The blade reflected her eyes back at her: brown. She wouldn't even die as herself.

The thought filled her with an anxiety beyond simply fear of an end.

Her entire life had been spent alone. They knew “Mimi”, they knew “Asuna, head of Project Galaxy”, but no one knew Asuna Morita.

Did *she* know herself?

Her whole existence was maintenance of a legacy. When would she have had time to *actually* live, *actually* connect? Even now, her mind was frantically trying to comprehend the impact of a knife attack on insurance premiums.

*Insurance premiums*.

**Now, I'm not one for spoilers.**

**Wait, yes, I am.**

**Spoilers: Asuna doesn't die in this timeline. Probably. Though those squeamish about knife wounds may want to close their eyes.**

“Help,” she said. It didn’t come out as a word but rather as a squeak.

That didn’t sound like her. She was… she was stronger than that. Right?

The knife-wielding man’s smile deepened, and light glinted across his glasses.

His tongue slid across his lips.

“Help,” Asuna attempted again.

He advanced.

Asuna flattened against the humming machine. Her mind flitted through a thousand options, none of which felt good.

Her eyes landed just outside the window, where a tall and broad-shouldered foreigner slid out of the darkness towards the laundromat door.

Long black trench coat? Scruffy beard? Tired eyes?

Clearly had the makings of a vigilante. Perfect.

Asuna screamed, and the knife-wielding man lunged.

**Hawthorn**

A scream?

Unusual for a laundromat.

"Take his fucking knife!"

Hawthorn froze and dropped his laundry basket. He took in the scene in front of him, though he wasn't quite sure what he was ready to do.

A small girl, her back to Hawthorn, had managed to get a knife-wielding man into a full nelson. He growled, wriggled, and cursed with arms held straight up with a gleaming knife in his hand.

"What the *fuck* are you waiting for?” The girl growled. “Take the-"

The man lurched and overwhelmed the girl. She stumbled back several steps.

"Mimi, you-" The man turned and saw Hawthorn. The crazed smile faltered as he took in the large American.

Hawthorn shook his head, snapping himself out of the momentary daze.

He took several assertive steps forward and squared himself up. He could at least *look* imposing. "Drop the knife,” he said in Japanese. “Now. Or I'll-"

The girl lunged with another screaming growl, colliding headfirst with the man. He gasped, and the knife clattered to the floor. The girl promptly connected a fist to his face with a loud *smack.*

Hawthorn flinched.

The man slumped to the ground.

For a moment, everything was still. Hawthorn remained mid-step, hand reaching forward as if he had *anything* prepared for this moment. His heart *pounded.*

The girl turned and squeezed her eyes shut. "Security detail.” she said in English.“ I need a security detail. Fucking always the last priority on the-"

Security detail?

A thought for later.

Hawthorn finally spoke, in English, "Y'all OK? Injuries?"

The girl opened her eyes. To Hawthorn's shock, she *smiled*, and her hands folded over her waist in what he assumed was an attempt to look innocent and shy.

She switched to Japanese. "Oh! No! Thank you for your help! I can take care of it from here!"

**Now, Hawthorn was quite used to Japanese pleasantries. It was one of his favorite aspects of the country, in fact. The societal contract was clean and simple, and expectations were quite clear-cut, with very obvious, easy-to-repeat phrases.**

**He, however, couldn't** ***quite*** **shake the feeling that this was** ***not*** **the best time for said pleasantries.**

The man lunged out and grabbed the girl's ankle. She yelped and stumbled forward. Hawthorn reflexively caught her.

"No. No no no no. This isn't how it goes. Mimi, it was all-" The man said. He grabbed the knife and slowly stood.

"What... what is he *doing*?" Hawthorn asked, glancing around and expecting to find a film crew.

"I think he's monologuing. Too much Anime," the girl said in English. The word "anime" caused the attacker's eyes to narrow.

"Anime? You think this is about *Anime*? This is about *love*, *devotion*, this is about-"

Hawthorn shook his head, "Goddamn. He's *actually* monologuing."

"He's even trying to do the crazy eyes. See the glint in his glasses? He's doing that on purpose!" The girl laughed. Hawthorn wasn’t quite sure if that was hysteria or amusement.

“The glint?” Hawthorn asked. The three of them kept each other in their peripheral vision.

“Gendo Ikari?”

“No clue.”

“Shou Tucker?” She offered. The name clearly struck something in the creep. His mouth opened to protest.

“Don’t compare me to-” he started.

“Alexander Anderson? Enishi Yukishiro? Szayelaporro Granz? Sugou Nobuyuki?”

“Negative,” Hawthorn stated flatly.

The girl groaned.

The creep found his dumb grin again. “It’s funny you mention Sugou, Asuna. I-”

“Shut it, neet. Adults are talking.” The girl–Asuna, apparently–turned to Hawthorn and crossed her arms. The creep was left gawking. “Okay. Light Yagami. No glasses, but he has the vibe.”

Hawthorn simply stared.

Asuna stared back.

Hawthorn did *not* have the mental bandwidth for this shit.

The creep faltered. He held his knife out at his side, and his gaze bounced between Hawthorn and Asuna. His mouth opened a few times but seemed to fail at finding words for the moment.

Asuna gestured towards the knife. "Are you going to *do* something about him, or do you want to keep revealing your ignorance of otaku culture while I run out of ways to keep him distracted?"

Oh.

"Fair." Hawthorn pulled out his phone and dialed 119.

"You're *calling* someone? I meant-"

The creep lunged again, this time at Hawthorn.

The girl growled again, and before Hawthorn could register what happened, she was on the ground, pinning the man's wrists to the linoleum floor. The knife skittered across the floor, leaving a thin line of red along its path.

Hawthorn stepped over and kicked out at the creep's head. His foot connected with a *crack* that filled the room, and he went still.

Hawthorn flipped the phone to speaker.

"I repeat. Is it a fire or a medical emergency?" The voice came through in Japanese. The girl slowly stood and took a few steps back, eyes narrowed.

"Attempted murder. Attacker appears unconscious. Nakamoto building." Hawthorn responded in Japanese.

"Injuries?"

"No." Hawthorn paused, glancing at the girl. "Wait."

Asuna was looking down, with a palm on her stomach. Blotches of red soaked through the cloth around her hand.

"Yes, injuries. Knife wound. Applying pressure." Hawthorn did not hesitate this time; he placed the phone on a table and dashed to his laundry basket, grabbing a white shirt.

"Mother *fucker*." Asuna exclaimed in English. When he turned around, he found her slumped on the floor against the vending machine.

"Hey, hey, eyes on me." Hawthorn knelt next to her then lifted her shirt enough to expose the wound. It didn't *seem* bad, but he wasn't quite sure that a decade or two of ER dramas was the best source of information for him to act on.

He pressed the shirt against the wound and applied pressure.

The girl didn't even flinch.

"I do *not* have time for this. It's only one month until-" The girl cut off as she looked up and met his eyes. She blinked, and her lips spread into that "innocent" and "polite" smile.

Hawthorn groaned internally, but remained focused on the wound.

"Emergency vehicles are en route." The voice on the phone said.

"I'm Naomi, by the way!" The girl–Mimi, Asuna, or Naomi, whoever she was–said, in a joyful pitch clearly designed to sell.

Hawthorn glanced up at the forced smile. Then looked down at both of their hands covered in blood.

She was masking.

While bleeding out on a laundromat floor.

Hawthorn didn't care. This girl's business was her own.

"A pleasure, I'm sure, Naomi. Hawthorn." He pressed further down on the wound, making sure to keep it shut.

"Are you American?" she said in English. Her eyes glazed over briefly before refocusing. Was that shock?

"Depends on who you ask. Definition of ‘American’ is up in the air lately. But yes, from Texas. Keep talking. Talk to me about Naomi."

"Naomi Yomimoto. Nineteen years old. Studying architecture at The University of Tokyo."

Not even a good lie. Granted, in over a decade of audits and acquisitions, he had heard worse.

"Favorite food?" Emergency sirens approached. The room filled with strobing red.

"They… They can't find out… insurance…" Her eyes began to fade again.

"Commit insurance fraud later. Just need you awake. Come on." A smack to the face, and she was back.

"Naomi. I'm Naomi." She smiled faintly, her fingers landing lightly on Hawthorn's bloodied hand.

"So I've heard." He smiled back, hoping the look came across as comforting. He was quite aware of his "resting brood face". Vanessa used to tease him about it almost every day.

Naomi’s eyes went wide and flickered between the paramedics and police as the room filled. The whirr of the dryer was drowned out by the efficient patter of shoes on tile, amid a volley of communications spoken into radios.

The scumbag on the floor was promptly handcuffed.

Hawthorn released pressure on the ruined shirt as more experienced hands took over.

Hawthorn exhaled.

His part was done.

He could do his laundry, then go back upstairs and return to soaking in misery. Back to routine, and-

The girl's eyes grabbed onto his with desperation as she was placed on a stretcher. "No, Asuna! I'm Asuna Morita! Please find me, Hawthorn! I'll tell them you're family!"

Fuck.

He held her gaze until she disappeared on a stretcher into the back of an ambulance just outside.

"I liked that shirt…" he muttered.

The police approached him.

They questioned him, and he answered directly, giving them his old business card. He explained his visa would expire soon, so he may be difficult to contact.

That girl, though. She lied about her name… it was a prepared lie. But then she gave him her real one? Was she still in danger?

He should do his laundry and go home.

He should do his laundry and go home.

The familiarity of zoning out on a couch in front of rolling stock tickers called to him like a siren.

He should-

Fuck.

The image of the girl, alone, in the world's lowliest of prison cells: a hospital room, was too much.

Vanessa would have never let him hear the end of it.

"Excuse me," Hawthorn said in Japanese. The closest police woman turned. "Where is the closest emergency room?"

"They will be taking her to Tokyo Medical, but they won't allow anyone but immediate family until visiting hours." She turned and left with the others.

"Apparently..." He grimaced. "... I'm family."

Tokyo Medical. Of course. What fucking games was the universe playing with him now?

**Just for the record,** ***technically*****, depending on your definition of “interfering”, I have played no games with Hawthorn.**

A little more than a year ago, he would have thought this to be some sign that he had a fated role to play in the complex, interconnected fabric that composed a holistically driven universe.

He carried no such delusions anymore.

**It’s an amusing occurrence in pattern seekers. To see the statistical rhythms in circumstantial impossibilities. The average mind is incapable of processing the cyclomatic complexity that feeds into “just how close” something came to happening or not happening. But the impossibilities are notable nonetheless, so they weave a tapestry of the unknown.**

**Occam’s razor is put to the test: Is it simpler to believe there is a larger pattern–a god, a blueprint, a simulation, a basilisk at the end of time, or any unknowable force–that is simply beyond our comprehension? Or does Occam’s simplicity come from accepting anarchy and chaos as a fact of reality?**

**I cannot speak for powers that are or are not involved in your realm.**

**All I can say is that I, personally, maintain a strict policy of non-intervention.**

**Well. Sort of.**

**Anyways.**

The dryer buzzed.

Hawthorn scratched at his chin beneath an overgrown beard. He looked down at his own long-overdue laundry sitting expectantly in the white basket. He could practically feel the grime in his last button-up and *jeans*.

He was down to *jeans.* He hadn't even known that he owned jeans before today.

Hawthorn looked out into the darkness that had been filled with blaring lights and sounds only moments earlier.

*FUCK*.

At the very least, he needed to deliver her clothes. He couldn't, in good faith, leave them.

So it was that he found himself two hours later waiting in a familiar hospital lounge, holding some "teenager's” bag of clothes.

He was sure the nurses would recognize him from last year. Even if they didn't, was he really going to pretend a tall, broad-shouldered gaijin was the family of a small Japanese girl?

Hawthorn scrolled through his professional contacts to fill the time.

The idea of returning to a country that felt like it was on the cusp of revoking his citizenship didn’t feel great. But he couldn’t stay here without a job.

A name stood out: "Cale Drab". Memories of shared whiskey and tears filled Hawthorn, and just the slightest hint of a smile formed at the corner of his mouth.

Los Angeles.

He could do LA.

Cale’s company was what… marketing, CRMs, bunch of web tech that changed every year. He would have *something* in the department. Hawthorn’s last stretch was security-focused, but his specialty was accountability, not a specific technical skill. Everyone needs Product Owners and Project Managers, right?

Could he manage a project that he didn’t control? He could certainly consult. But the market wasn’t starved of consultants. Everyone wanted an opinion without owning the solution. Having an opinion was easy.

And Hawthorn didn’t want to consult people who wouldn’t listen. Another thing the market was starved of.

It all came back to accountability.

Hawthorn exhaled a drawn-out breath. Whatever Cale had open had to be better than C-Suite had been.

He was done with that life.

The double doors opened, and a nurse he did not recognize entered, blinking at the giant American in a black trench coat sitting on the couch.

"Mister…" she began in English, dripping with doubt.

"Morita. Hawthorn Morita." He fought back a wince. He had told worse, he supposed. He adopted his smile, the same smile every person in the entire country seemed to share. The contract.

The nurse returned the smile and bowed.

"She told us to expect you. An adopted brother."

Sure.

"Thank you. Yes, I would appreciate seeing my sister," Hawthorn stood and returned the bow.

The nurse paused only briefly. She turned, and Hawthorn followed with laundry bag in hand. She led him through a maze of sterile hallways. The unbearable scent of sterilization only seemed to get worse the further they walked.

His heart sank as they approached the far corner of the building.

Of course.

The universe was clearly fucking with him.

Mocking him.

Rubbing salt into an injury.

Kicking the fucking horse while it was already twitching on the fucking ground.

**In my defense, I have never once kicked a horse.**

It was the same room.

Hawthorn gritted his teeth as the door opened, further enveloping him in the overwhelming assault of sterilization.

Granted, it was a nice room. Single bed. Several furnishings were hardwood, including the headboard and foot of the bed itself, and the window overlooked a small garden that had been subject to countless hours of Hawthorn’s contemplative stare.

But none of that competed with the tidal wave of memories that smashed into Hawthorn as he stepped in.

Vanessa lying on that bed, with a mischievous and coy smile that defied the somber drips and monitors, flashed before him.

It was not Vanessa, though.

It was the damned girl.

Asuna's short, amber hair fell just above her shoulders. The phone cast a glow of rapidly shifting colors across her face in the dimly lit room.

Something about the posture was familiar. It carried a distinctive air of... efficiency that Hawthorn *almost* admired.

But the way she had lied and masked, as if she wore a second skin, was enough to tell him to make no assumptions.

"Miss Morita?" the nurse said.

Asuna practically threw her phone; it landed on the cotton sheets. The look of focus evaporated, replaced with a bright smile that didn't quite match the eyes.

"Brother!" she exclaimed, throwing her arms ou

Hawthorn slapped on his own fake smile. "Sister! I came as soon as you texted. Are you OK?"

Apparently they would both be lying poorly today.

Hawthorn walked up, dropped the laundry by the bed, and gave her the most awkward shoulder hug in the history of mankind.

"Some creep tried to stab me! It was the craziest thing!" she said, with a pronounced fake pout.

The nurse bowed, rightfully choosing *not* to engage in this dumb bout of blatant bullshit. "Please let us know if you need anything. My shift ends soon. Another nurse will be in sometime later to check vitals."

She bowed again, turned, and left.

Both Hawthorn and Asuna watched her go.

As soon as the door shut with a light thud, he backed up from the bed, maintaining his hollow smile. If this girl wanted to mask, he could mask.

"How can this lone ranger be of assistance?"

The girl looked away. "I just… didn't want to be alone."

"You'll find I can be pretty good at existing in a space if that's all you need; been told I'm annoyingly good at it." A line directly stolen from Cale.

It earned a chuckle. The noise put a tiny chip in the darkness that consumed him.

*No.*

She met his eyes. "It means a lot. I don't really have anyone else I can call right now… And for some reason, facing your death makes you desperately want to not be alone."

Was that vulnerability real or forced?

"Family? Friends? Work?" he asked.

"Passed away or uninvolved. Who has time for those? And… it's complicated," she responded, turning away.

Hawthorn grunted with genuine sympathy. "Same. Loss at your age... Sorry."

"It was hard. But it's always about the next step. No regrets." She beamed. He managed to hold eye contact for about two seconds before looking away.

Were they really going to sit here and have their facades exchange small talk? Who was this benefiting? What did she want from him? Just company?

If they were going to chat, he much preferred the woman who had mocked a knife-wielding threat over a girl who spits out practiced lines.

Or was *he* the new knife-wielding threat? Was that mask she wore sitting over a doe, or was he being circled by a tiger?

He glanced over. Her smile had fallen off, but was quickly replaced.

What happens when a tiger realizes it found a hibernating bear?

"Wise beyond your years." He stood up and took his coat off, concluding that he would be here for a while.

Hawthorn walked directly to the coat closet, hung up the coat, then grabbed pillows from another closet and turned the couch into a bed. He could feel Asuna’s focused gaze follow him. Every motion he made was being analyzed.

Let her prowl.

The monitor kept a steady rhythm to his motion through the room.

"You are very familiar with this hospital."

"She and I go way back. Story for a different time. You? You mentioned college?"

Hawthorn lay back on the pseudo-bed, catching up with his old friend: the crack in the corner of the ceiling. His feet hung off the end.

"Oh! Yes! College! I'm studying Architecture! I'm a big fan of Kenzo Tange; his blend of modernism with Japanese traditions is truly amazing!"

If she was going to lie, he expected there at least to be effort put into it.

"Is that what Naomi thinks or Asuna?" he asked.

Hawthorn shot her a pointed glance; she was still smiling as if he hadn't just called her out on her bullshit.

However, the heart monitor briefly spiked.

"I… shit." Her voice dropped in pitch, losing some of its loftiness but still maintaining the crisp tone.

It was a few moments before shein said anything, and when she did, her voice was back to the elevated pitch.

"I can at least get out of this dumb thing. I guess there's no reason to hide here."

Hawthorn turned his head as she pulled a wig off, revealing long black hair.

He cocked an eyebrow.

She, of course, was still lying by omission. One doesn't just take off a wig without explaining why they needed it. She was covering up "young attractive girl" with "young attractive girl".

He had to admit he was at least curious, even slightly amused.

Hawthorn sat up and leaned forward. He did his best to tune out the sensory overload of the room's various beeping. "Y'all still in danger? Get the feeling this was not a random attack."

He let his own fake smile fade.

"No. I had never met that creep before in my life," she stated flatly.

She either had a perfect poker face or was telling the truth; the monitor continued without breaking pace.

"Why... why do you ask? If it was random," the girl finally asked.

"Seemed to know you, is all."

Her mask slipped, zeroing focus on Hawthorn, as if he were a puzzle to solve.

"Am *I* supposed to know you?" he asked.

Her mouth opened, but she didn't respond.

OK. Maybe a middle ground. Light banter.

Hawthorn narrowed his eyes. "Yakuza?"

"What are you..." she started, then held up a hand to cover a smile. Brown eyes followed Hawthorn as he stood up and began to pace along the foot of her bed.

Hawthorn went with another. "Would be hard to picture Tokyo's greatest luchadora to be so caught off guard."

"Luchadora? Your next jump was a masked Mexican wrestler?" She snickered.

The laugh egged him on; it felt honest. Besides, it lightened her mood, which was, in fact, satisfying.

"Nah, you're right, that would be an insult to Japan's top secret weapons designer," he said.

Her smile widened, and her voice dropped to the same tone as the woman who put a man twice her size into a headlock, "You know I'm not going to confirm if you get this right, right?"

"Implication being there's something to be got?" he said with a knowing glance over his shoulder.

He pulled up another one. "Would be statistically unlikely for anyone to guess that they had saved an heir to the long-lost Japanese Empire, come to steer the country into a bountiful golden era."

A full-blown laugh, cut short as she winced from the stitches. "You are ridiculous."

"Not as ridiculous as the idea that I just happened to bump into *the* biggest international J-Pop sensation."

Hawthorn himself finally laughed, a noise that almost startled him in unfamiliarity.

The heart rate monitor's beeping significantly increased in pace. The moment hung, and it took a few seconds for him to realize what had just happened.

"Shit," she muttered.

The door opened, and a familiar nurse entered.

"Mr. Morisset?" Nurse Ito said.

"Shit," Hawthorn muttered.

"Are you-" the nurse began.

"He's my brother. Adopted." Asuna returned to her lighter voice, although it lacked the mock enthusiasm.

The nurse paused; he was likely *very* confused. Hawthorn had shown him pictures of all six of his siblings, and this girl was not in them.

"It's complicated," Hawthorn offered, without looking back.

Technically, not a lie.

"In any case, I hope you are doing well. The loss of a wife, I'm sure, never fully heals."

Hawthorn clouded with recollections of the final moments...

Those memories were his; that sorrow was *his*. He could not, and would not, share it.

This was dumb.

Why was Hawthorn doing this?

Suffocating under the memories this room filled him with. And suffocating under this oppressive *fucking* aura of *fucking* disinfectants and antiseptics.

"Miss Morita, are you wearing colored contacts? Your eye colors don't match our records, and I don't see any indicator of corrective prescriptions. Could you deposit those in here for the night?"

Hawthorn turned his head in curiosity.

Asuna hesitantly removed two contacts, revealing light grey eyes. Now *those* were unique and beyond stunning.

Their eyes met.

Ten heartbeats they remained frozen, seeing significantly deeper into one another than either had planned to allow that day.

The moment was broken mutually and simultaneously.

Asuna turned to her phone, which Hawthorn took note was not lit, and he started a staring contest with the whiteboard.

It was several minutes after the nurse left that Asuna spoke up. She cautiously turned to Hawthorn. "I'm so sorry. Could you tell me about her?"

He contemplated the question.
