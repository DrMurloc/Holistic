**Lara**

Kenji fumbled through papers in a folder, sitting across his soft-gloss oak desk from Lara.

The room was otherwise silent.

Hawthorn stood by the far window, arms crossed and staring out over the city. Miss Morita sat nearby, buried in her phone, as was typical when not performing or helping on stage.

Lara was unsure whom to focus on: her direct manager, the domineering American, or her personal role model, Miss Morita. So her eyes flickered around the room, attempting to piece together what was going on.

"Did he kiss you again?" Lara cautiously asked in Japanese; she couldn't think of why else she would have been called in.

The folder slipped from Kenji's hand and fell to the floor.

Miss Morita burst into laughter, and even Hawthorn gave a snort, which was the most mirth that Lara had seen from the man when he wasn't coaching or coordinating.

"Yes, once or twice, but that's not why you're here. We're past the point where Mimi's image matters; Harin's team has the focus now." Miss Morita put her phone away as she shifted forward, finally giving Lara someone to focus on.

Kenji was still fumbling with papers; his face was quite red.

"Tell me about Spanish culture, Lara. We're talking youth," Miss Morita started, buying Kenji time.

Lara contemplated the question; it was a rather large ask. Just cover everything she knew about a culture that spanned more than a tenth of the globe?

"Do you mean Spain, Mexico, the Caribbean, or South America? Are we accounting for the large number of Spanish speakers in North America? What part of culture? Entertainment? Idols?"

This was not quite what Lara had expected, and coming up with an answer on the fly while piecing together Japanese would be difficult. She wasn't sure she was getting all the words quite right.

Hawthorn gave a grunt of approval at the question, though.

Miss Morita nodded with a smile and asked, surprisingly in slightly off Spanish, "South and North America, entertainment, streaming, videos."

She held up a phone, which appeared to be live-translating whatever Lara said.

Lara paused briefly, continuing in Spanish, her words starting slow, "They're cultures that are built on community and passion..."

Should she... info dump? Would it be too much?

A nod from Miss Morita was the encouragement Lara needed, and she started off, slowly, "Both Latin America and North America are big on digital communities around shared passions or interests. I'm far from an expert, but I think most of the entertainment industry is built around capitalizing on those communities and driving them..."

Enthusiasm built, and her words shifted to rapid-fire, hoping the translator could keep up. "It's actually fascinating! It's a culture that lends itself to free marketing if you can find the right angle and reach the right people. Especially if you tap into meme culture, which spreads information and brand recognition like wildfire. I..."

Lara realized she was probably info-dumping too much and stopped herself.

Hawthorn looked over his shoulder with a raised eyebrow, though she wasn't sure he understood anything that had been said.

Did she scare them off?

Was that too much?

Her heart filled with the icy tendrils of dread, hoping she hadn't just ruined some opportunity with her tendency to talk fast.

Miss Morita read the translation out to Hawthorn in Japanese; from what Lara understood, it was close enough. It got another grunt of approval from Hawthorn, and he turned back to the window.

Kenji was back at the desk, flipping through papers.

"Kenji, do you need help?" Miss Morita asked, standing up and moving beside him.

Lara was confused.

What had just happened?

Were there not going to be further questions?

"No! I have it right here, sorry." Kenji laid out several papers in front of Lara.

Lara was still not fluent in reading Japanese; kanji, in particular, was annoying, so she pulled out her phone translator and began skimming the poorly translated Spanish.

"I'll save you some effort. It's an offer of... promotion," Miss Morita offered.

"What? I'm contracted." Lara shifted back into Japanese.

"And we want to change that."

Lara was confused; this job had never been intended to have a career path attached to it.

"Miss Morita, I-"

"Please, call me Asuna," Miss Mori- Asuna said.

The implication of that offer was not lost on Lara; Japan almost exclusively used family names in professional contexts. Asuna had basically just said, "I want to be friends".

Lara's eyes skimmed back down to the translator; the words "streamer", "talent", "voice-acting" stood out.

"Talent?

"Are you familiar with V-Tubers?" Asuna asked, standing.

Kenji realized he was not driving this conversation and vacated his seat for the woman.

"Yes?" Lara had stumbled upon a few of them; she had her favorites —she liked the translated clips of the red-headed rat in particular —but she didn't necessarily avidly subscribe to any.

From a cultural perspective, they were interesting.

Some saw only the parasocial and toxic portion of what they provided. But Lara found that the global sense of unity that came from people laughing at the same stream across the world to be touching.

Asuna continued, "It's a vertical we're interested in. We need... hands-on experience. And you, we found out, have a history of content creation and cultural exploration."

With that statement, Asuna gave the most supportive smile Lara had ever seen.

For the briefest moment, Lara smiled back, as if the offer that had been placed on the table wasn't the most absurd leap of logic ever made.

As her mind finally caught up to the moment, her jaw dropped, and she fell back to Spanish, "What?"

Asuna didn't budge, though, and simply nodded to the paper in front of Lara, so Lara looked down.

She found a number.

Lara swallowed.

She wasn't quite sure why she swallowed; her mouth had gone quite dry.

It was far from an extravagant number, but it was leagues ahead of what she could ever expect from her typical short-term jobs. If she were making this... it could easily cover travel expenses indefinitely. Her remaining funds would become savings...

"I... I don't know that my voice is built for this..." Lara found herself saying.

"Your voice? Why?"

Lara looked up, tilting her head.

"You heard, I... ramble. I say... too much."

Asuna looked confused by the assertion, "Lara, that wasn't rambling. That was *passion*."

Hawthorn grunted in agreement.

Passion?

That?

It... no? That wasn't right. Her mom always said...

Asuna continued, "Besides, you created all those videos, right?"

Lara shook her head. "It usually requires five or six takes to make sure I get the right pace. If you pay attention, you can hear the clips where I stitch together different takes to make sure it's *just* right. It's really cool, actually, I-"

Lara stopped herself from going on a tangent about the nuance of video editing.

"That sounds exhausting," Asuna stated.

"It... is." Lara nodded, keeping herself back from several further tangents.

"I don't think that matters for streaming. I promise you, the passion, the heat, that you just spoke to us with? People will eat it up," Asuna stated.

Passion... heat...

Maybe...?

Lara would need to contemplate that. Somehow, the assertion coming from Asuna was enough to at least contend with two decades of being trained to control her voice.

So, she should take the job, right? If Asuna had faith in her, then maybe...

Wait...

"Could I travel?" Lara asked, waiting to hear the catch. She held back the tides of hope with a floodgate of skepticism.

"I knew you were going to ask that." Asuna shifted in her seat, looking back at Hawthorn. "Mr. Tall, Broad, and Broody over there thinks it would be a poor decision to allow remote work at our current size. Extra paperwork, tax implications, and legal requirements of remote countries. Fortunately for you, consultants don't make decisions."

Lara felt the tide smash through the barrier, flooding her with relief and excitement at the freedom to access the open seas.

"Did you call me tall, broad, and broody?" Hawthorn asked without looking back.

Lara's phone was now on the table, live translating to supplement any words she didn't understand.

"Am I wrong?" Asuna laughed. Hawthorn only snorted in response.

Kenji chimed in, "You *are* extra broody today, sir. I'm sure the city is well protected with your watchful gaze."

Hawthorn shook his head, but continued to maintain his posture. "You too, Kenji? Do you want to be audited again? I never got those organization charts."

"Don't threaten Kenji with more paperwork; his heart can't take it." Asuna snickered with a grin that Lara had never seen on the woman.

"How's that report to insurance from April going?" Hawthorn followed up, cutting off Asuna's laugh.

"It... hasn't come up. I've been busy."

This was not quite what Lara expected from these three. On stage, they were focused and efficient. Sho and Lara had, on multiple occasions, used them as examples of "responsible adults that have life figured out".

"Insurance?" Lara asked, trying to keep up poorly.

"Oh, don't worry about that. Hawthorn is just being silly." Asuna returned to her supportive smile, focusing on Lara again.

"Deflecting, Asuna?" Lara could see Hawthorn's body shake with a silent laugh.

"Yes," she said without so much as blinking.

"I'm shocked. *Shocked.*"

Lara got the feeling that Hawthorn wasn't that shocked.

"Anyways." Asuna's tone made it clear they were back to business. "Yes. Get me a list of countries you're interested in. There are a few you'll need to use time off for, but most we should be able to allow for a few weeks at a time. No working from Germany, for instance."

This was a lot.

Not the Germany part, though that was slightly disappointing.

This wasn't just a job offer; Lara felt like she was being welcomed into an inner circle.

How had she possibly earned that?

"I don't understand, why?" Her eyes flickered between Kenji, Asuna, and the back of Hawthorn.

Asuna contemplated her response.

"Kenji, can we have the room, please?"

"Ma'am? Oh, uh. Y-yes. Of course," Kenji stuttered, then rose to his feet and shuffled out the door. Lara caught a brief glimpse of Sho sitting in the small lobby outside. He looked nervous.

Why is he here?

The realization that Lara was still anchored hit her.

They would figure it out.

She would need to be careful about how to break the news to Sho, though. He still had high anxiety that he was "holding her back".

Their eyes met, and Lara shot him the smile she knew he liked briefly before the door closed.

"Lara, you remind me of me." Asuna leaned forward on the desk. Hawthorn grunted, seemingly in agreement.

Lara blinked, not quite sure she had understood that sentence correctly. The phone confirmed what she had heard.

"I'm sorry... What?"

"The way you apply yourself to everything you do. The speed at which you learn. Who knows, maybe I'm just trying to find myself in some young girl that I can help." Asuna shrugged.

"I learn fast?"

"Lara, how long ago did you start learning Japanese?"

"About two months?" Lara was unsure of how the question was relevant. It got a whistle from Hawthorn.

"It took me two years to get to the confidence I have in speaking English, with a dedicated tutor," Asuna stated.

"Three years in Japanese," Hawthorn noted without turning.

Lara didn't know what to do with that information. "I have to apply myself, I have to learn. That's... the point of me being out here."

They were giving her too much credit.

She didn't feel that "I had to learn, I like talking to the boy that I love" was super pertinent to the conversation. No one in the workplace knew that Sho and Lara were dating; anyway, they had done a good job of hiding it.

Asuna laughed. "Obligations to the family?"

Lara cocked her head. "How did you know?"

"Call it a hunch. It's beside the point. The fact that I overlooked —something that took Broody McBroodface over here to call out, because I most certainly would not have acknowledged it on my own —was that others *don't* apply themselves like that. I want you to think of every person you've met and tell me honestly how many of them look to leave every encounter having learned or gained something?"

This legitimately confused Lara, though the back of her mind registered some truth in Asuna's assertion.

"What else would I do?"

Hawthorn looked back over his shoulder and gave a smirk that Lara hadn't known possible from the man. She apparently had said something he found amusing.

Asuna simply smiled, as if Lara had answered her own question.

Lara looked back down at the number on the paper. It *did* feel like a very freeing number... But this felt like an investment on their part.

She would be at the center of an entire new "product". *That* felt constricting.

"What if you're wrong? I've never done this before... I was never sure if the videos were what I really wanted to do for the rest of my life..."

"For the rest of your life? You're what? Nineteen? Let's start with the next year." Asuna laughed, Hawthorn shook his head, turning back out the window.

"Twenty now... Didn't you start younger?"

"Don't use me as a role model. I had no clue what I wanted to do until roughly five months ago, and even now, I'm still figuring it out. Everything up to that point was stumbling forward without knowing what direction I was even moving."

That was a hard image for Lara to picture. She was not sure she had ever seen this woman wear a look of uncertainty. Had her image of "responsible adult" been off?

Or was Lara's take on "responsibility" wrong?

"Would I just... start streaming tomorrow? What do you expect from me?" Lara asked.

The question got yet another grunt of approval from Hawthorn.

They were acting like Lara was asking the most professionally refined questions, but this was just basic common sense, right?

"We're planning for six months of spin-up time, and even that is flexible. Time to research and prepare. Half your job is market research. We can have Harin work with the design team to get you a model."

Lara was going to get paid to learn.

She was going to get money... in exchange for... doing what she already did?

That felt wrong.

There had to be a catch, right?

As if reading her mind, Asuna stated, "Jobs are non-binding, Lara. There's a reason laws exist to protect your right to quit if it's not a good fit."

Lara signed the papers.

**Sho**

The door opened, causing Sho to straighten up.

Lara bowed deeply on her way out. Sho was impressed by how good she had gotten at that.

Their eyes met as she left, and she flashed him yet another smile. He would need to follow up with her later, though, as Miss Morita waved him inside.

Sho found himself sitting on the other side of a desk from Miss Morita, who was digging through what appeared to be Kenji's folder. His eyes flickered between her and Hawthorn, who stood by the window staring out over the city.

Why would they have him in here?

What happened?

They had talked to Lara too...

"Did... did he kiss you again?" Sho asked cautiously.

Sho had never heard Hawthorn laugh; it was a powerful noise.

Miss Morita buried her face in her palms with a sigh. "No. Well, yes. But, we're here for you."

Sho attempted to think of anything he had done wrong. This had to be about one of the newer hires getting some of the costumes mixed up. Sho hadn't spent enough time teaching them; it was his fault.

He was about to bow and apologize when a paper slipped in front of him.

"Stage Manager?" He read through the terms.

It was a salary job, Kenji's old job before he took over for Miss Morita.

An official leadership position, not just a contract.

Sho immediately filled with pride. He was sure his parents did too.

"This is an honor, thank you, Miss Morita." Sho stood and bowed.

He could barely think; this was so unexpected.

"Not me. As much as I'd *love* to take credit, I'm signing papers. Our dark vigilante over there has been the one keeping an eye on your progress." Miss Morita nodded towards Hawthorn.

Now that Miss Morita mentioned it, with that coat, the man *did* look like he was missing an oversized broadsword as he overlooked a gruesome battlefield.

The corner of Hawthorn's mouth curled up, seeming proud at Miss Morita's assertion that this was credited to him.

But something felt off.

"Don't... stage managers... talk?" He pieced together, slowly.

"Yes? Of course."

"But... I don't," he stated.

"Of course you do! You-" Miss Morita started.

Hawthorn interjected. "Your words are *efficient*, Sho, not *abundant*. It's powerful."

Sho studied the man for a moment, the way he didn't even turn back to make the assertion, like it wasn't a debate.

If that's what Hawthorn felt... The implications...

Sho never repeated what others had said, wouldn't waste time explaining what couldn't be explained, and made sure anything he *did* say wouldn't need to be repeated...

He supposed that was "efficient".

Huh.

That would take some thought.

Sho quickly sat back down and read through the rest of the paper, noting a significant, even if not massive, number.

The pen was in his hand, but he stopped just short of signing.

"I couldn't travel," he stated.

This got Hawthorn to turn, exchanging a look with Miss Morita.

Hawthorn nodded. "Yes, it's an on-site job. Fifteen to twenty days paid time off. We might be able to swing some extra time between shows, but those are only getting more and more regular."

Sho's heart thudded as he pushed the paper away as if it carried forbidden magic.

The room remained silent.

This was a mistake.

He was being irrational.

This was his future; he owed this to his parents, right? This was everything they had been raising him for: to get a job *exactly* like this. Hell, he owed it to *Hawthorn* for all the effort he had put in coaching Sho.

"Sho?" Hawthorn stepped over.

Miss Morita vacated the seat for the American to take over.

Both looked shocked —new expressions Sho had never seen on those faces.

"I am conflicted," Sho stated, not wanting to burden them with the details.

Hawthorn seemed to understand more than Sho said, though, and Sho watched as gears turned behind the man's eyes.

"Remind me, Asuna, you have other candidates lined up, right?" Hawthorn's eyes remained locked on Sho's.

"Well, we... oh, yes, of course," Miss Morita stammered. Her shock gave way to a smile as she seemed to pick up on something Hawthorn was implying.

"So it would not inconvenience us if our contracted stagehand remained on contract, perhaps with a further bump in pay to reflect his recent increase in responsibilities, right?"

Hawthorn was smiling, which felt distinctly unnerving on the man.

Sho shrank ever so slightly.

"No, no inconvenience whatsoever," she continued to smile.

That was a lot of smiling.

Should Sho smile?

They had just told him he was getting a raise.

That was good, right?

Sho smiled.

It felt wrong.

Sho stopped smiling.

Miss Morita continued, "Sho, we're going to need Kenji back in to help whip up some new paperwork. Could you send him in?"

Sho looked back down at the paper.

This was most definitely a mistake.

Sho would regret this.

He knew it.

Would he really throw away the opportunity that just threw itself into his lap, simply to entertain some fantasy of traveling with the first girl to sleep with him?

Sho's hand tightened around the pen before placing it down.

Yes.

Sho did not sign the papers.

**Hawthorn**

The door shut as Sho left the room.

Hawthorn's hands immediately moved to his temples, rubbing with eyes squeezed shut. He could hear Asuna break into a rapid circular pacing motion.

"Fuck," Asuna stated.

"Fuck," Hawthorn agreed.

They, in fact, had no contingency plan.

It was one of the most confident promotions that Hawthorn had ever planned. Sho would both accept the position with honor and then fill it like a glove.

"Did you know they were dating?" Asuna asked.

"No!" Hawthorn was almost impressed at how professional the two had been in hindsight. It only furthered his wish that Sho would sign on.

"Also, did you know Kenji and Harin are dating?"

"Is that what that is?"

Those two were weird together.

As if hearing his name, Kenji entered and shut the door behind him.

"Sho didn't sign," Asuna stated.

"Damn." Kenji finally retook his own chair.

"Language!"

"Oh, my apologies, Miss Morita." Kenji switched back to his standard formal dialect.

"I'm fucking with you, Kenji." Asuna moved over to the window, crossing her arms.

Several moments passed while Hawthorn and Kenji gave Asuna the space to process. Kenji's promotion was a bit more complicated without Sho locked in. Not impossible, just... messier. Kenji, of course, had no clue.

Hawthorn's eyes fell on a distant digital billboard featuring Harin's "Kira Void" persona, walking with attitude across a stage, a raised microphone in hand.

"What's the call, boss?" Hawthorn finally asked.

In the reflection of the window, Hawthorn could see Asuna's head rock back and forth in contemplation.

"Kenji, whip up a proposition for Haruki. Let's put a public posting too, see what bites we get in the next... month."

"Yes, ma'am." Kenji got back into his seat and moved towards the door.

"And Kenji, add Director of Operations to the organization chart you owe Hawthorn. Put your name under it."

"I... Yes, ma'am? Uh, what is the salary range for that?"

Hawthorn stepped up next to Asuna, mirroring her cross-armed and focused posture.

Asuna pretended to think, finally responding, "I don't know, get a listing for an HR position going. I think we're overdue. They can probably help you on that."

Hawthorn had to hold back a laugh.

Asuna, of course, had numbers and the plans for an HR hire already laid out. Kenji would most certainly undershoot himself on whatever number he came back with, and Asuna would adjust it on the formal offer to the real number.

"I... Yes, ma'am."

Before Kenji could leave, Hawthorn added, "Kenji, the first task for that HR hire should probably be a sexual harassment training. Make sure Harin takes it."

There was a pause, "I... yes. Understood, sir."

And with that, Kenji left, seeming to forget, as usual, that this was *his* office now.

Hawthorn and Asuna remained standing in front of the large window with arms crossed, attempting to keep a straight face.

"Broody McBroodface?" Hawthorn finally broke into a laugh.

Asuna followed suit and shot him that beautiful grin, "What? Like the boat!"

"Boat?"

"Have you *not* been on the internet? Do you *know* what industry you're in? You need to touch digital grass more."

"I don't know what that means." Hawthorn lifted her up to eye level, and she draped her arms over his shoulder.

"No, you wouldn't. It's ok, I still love you even if you're a stuffy boomer."

"You know I'm half the age required to be eligible for such a title, right?"

"Boomer's more of a state of mind, and you're a boomer at heart."

"Beginning to regret buying you the time to research what the 'youths' are into nowadays." Hawthorn still wasn't quite sure what a "skibidi toilet" was, and at this point, he was too afraid to ask.

They kissed, and it washed away any and all stress from Sho's rejection.

"Anyway, this boomer needs beer." Hawthorn lowered her, looking back out the window at the bar he had been eyeing down the street.

"Did Cale ever get back to you on that favor he and Astrid were going to call in?"

Hawthorn grunted. "New York. November. Sounds like they want Asuna Starlight, not Mimi."

"Oh, good. My knees are getting tired of Mimi." Asuna began to move towards the exit.

Hawthorn laughed. "You're not even thirty yet."

"You spend ten years endlessly bouncing on a stage, and then you can lecture me on knee lifespan."

"Fair."

The two emerged into the office; the bustle of activity was slowing as the workday drew to a close. It was hard to call sixty-three people "bustling", but compared to the skeleton crew that Hawthorn had joined, it was pretty lively.

The progress had speed bumps, of course; growth was hard.

Redefining boundaries, defining new ones, rebuilding communication lines and processes. An eternal balance between exploration and exploitation... But they had the right people solving the right problems.

And the right problems getting solved made Hawthorn happy.

It would be a year or two until the mixture of bank loans and angel investors sorted out into anything that resembled *true* green, but there was momentum.

And momentum made Hawthorn happy.

And most importantly, above all else, Asuna was happy.

And when Asuna was happy, Hawthorn was happy.
