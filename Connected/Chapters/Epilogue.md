**Cale**

Cale looked into the beautiful, icy-blue eyes of the most captivating girl in the world.

The clearing in the garden, their gathered friends and family, the perfectly temperate air, the quiet sobs from several onlookers, none of that mattered.

What mattered stood in front of him.

What mattered was the way she had woven the blue ribbon between her hair in a braid. It was a subtle, yet grand, sign of affection that meant nothing to anyone else but everything to him.

What mattered was the tear that rolled down her perfect cheek, a tear neither of them did anything about. Cale knew she liked the feeling it left. And he knew that she loved that he knew that.

What mattered was that, despite years of tormenting one another, of him poking and prodding at her emotions like a plaything, she still smiled like he had given her the world.

But everyone else was waiting for him.

He had promises to make.

Promises Astrid didn't need to hear, but promises that would make her happy, and that was all that mattered.

He looked at his cue cards, not actually focusing on them. Nothing written on them mattered. There was only one person here who mattered.

The cards were promptly thrown aside, falling to the ground, ignored.

"Astrid, I've loved you for eleven years now. I'll love you for another seventy. When they inevitably unlock immortality, I will love you until the end of even that. A year ago, I was faced with one of the hardest decisions of my life, and it feels so silly in hindsight. It was never a hard decision. If you gave me a trillion lives, I would take you to that park in every single one of them. I love you. I love that I get to say those words. And I love being your idiot."

**Cale**

The back of the reception room led out into the hedge garden; they had the privacy they so desperately craved.
