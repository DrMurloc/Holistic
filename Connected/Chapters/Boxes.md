**Astrid**

Astrid sat on the metro, head leaning against the window, eyes focused on nothing in particular. The air hummed with silent anticipation. The city lights flashed by, a colorful mirage of energy and potential.

Cale sprawled on the seat next to her, their hands overlapping on her leg.

There was no banter.

No bickering.

Just stillness.

For the first time in their life, they simply soaked in one another's presence.

A stop was announced, though Cale made no move to get up. He seemed to be lost in his own thoughts. The doors slid shut, and their vessel continued its rumbling progress.

Astrid stole a glance at Cale, noting his mask was off.

Her mind had begun to draw a divide in how she viewed her boyfriend. Gone was "old Cale": the brash, opinionated, and relentless boy from high school and early college. "New Cale" carried charm, charisma, and calculated restraint.

She had, at some point, fallen in love with the old Cale, but the new Cale was the one who won her.

Did she love the old Cale or the new Cale?

Even the word still felt foreign to her.

The car shook lightly as a train passed on the opposing track, and her eyes drifted back out to the passing city.

He clearly had more layers than she could ever have comprehended. He most certainly enjoyed the smirking, the projected confidence, the joking; his laughs were genuine, she could tell that.

But seeing him now, with tired, yet focused, eyes that seemed to mirror Astrid's own internal state...

Had they done that to each other? Or was this who they were?

Or was she overthinking it?

It had been an emotionally exhausting day, and they weren't even done. Cale had worked his charm all day, and Astrid had been charmed all day.

The ebbs and flows of realizing how compatible they actually were had been a ride more thrilling than any coaster could achieve.

Cale patted her knee as the next stop was announced.

Astrid sprang to her feet; the excess ambition in the movement drew a chuckle from Cale, though he didn't comment.

She steadied herself on the strap hangers, and their eyes met.

He smiled.

Not a smirk, not a grin.

Just a smile.

Somehow, that simplest, most benign, yet affectionate of actions reinvigorated her. Reality of what she was here to do crashed in on her; a reality that she would have labeled preposterous merely twelve hours earlier.

Cale stood, and she took his arm. They began a steady, yet purposeful, march out of the station and along the sidewalk towards an apartment complex.

New Cale or Old Cale, she knew one thing: she would *not* let this man out of her life again.

And she had *every* intent to stake that claim.

He opened the door to the complex, and they anxiously waited for the elevator doors to open.

Astrid studied her nails, wondering what other girls actually did when they did this. Did anyone *ever* seriously study their nails, or was it well established that this was solely a move of nonchalance?

What felt like a fifth wind of energy flowed through Astrid's veins as she listened to the hum of the approaching elevator.

Cale's voice finally broke the silence, "How cliche would it-"

Astrid stopped him dead in his tracks, "Don't. You'll ruin it."

She found her foot tapping, a sign of anxiety that likely came off as impatience.

Out of the corner of her eye, she could see that he was studying her again; it sent her mind into a frenzy. What images were flashing through his mind?

The doors opened, and they entered.

Cale pressed one of the buttons, and Astrid took satisfaction in noting it was a higher floor.

This elevator door was impossibly slow.

It had to start closing any moment now, right?

Was it broken?

*Fuck it.*

As the door finally started to close - agonizingly slow - Astrid's hands found Cale's shoulders and pressed him into the wall with a soft *thud*.

Cale had clearly been ready, and his arms wrapped tight around her.

Lips met.

Tongues engaged.

For the first time that day, they found skin contact, with hands greedily finding purchase under shirts. The way his back arched in reaction only further filled her mind with hunger, and the way his hand slid up to her side caused the contact to burn in *all* the right ways.

A chorus of noises—loosely categorizable as "moans", "grunts", and "whimpers"—filled the elevator from both parties.

After several moments, Astrid's eyes flickered up to the floor counter, afraid of what she would find.

To her horror, at that moment, the door opened, and a small, grey-haired lady stood, gawking with wide eyes behind thick glasses.

To say Astrid launched herself back would likely be an understatement.

She jolted to the other side, the air seeming to "pop" with the vacuum left behind. Her face burned bright red from a combination of passion, alcohol, embarrassment, and the friction of the air from such a movement.

"Miss Pearl." Cale nodded, attempting to play it cool as he grabbed Astrid's hand.

The woman, still gawking and wide-eyed, finally spoke, "Cale! Is that Astrid?"

Astrid stumbled as she was dragged down the hallway.

"Yes, Miss Pearl," Cale exclaimed as they passed.

"She's gorgeous, as you said! Go get her, Cale!" the lady yelled down the hall.

Astrid attempted to look back over her shoulder at the old lady, momentarily stunned by the brief exchange.

"Thank you, Miss Pearl," Cale yelled back, already fumbling with keys.

Astrid did *not* know where to begin with this question, "Could you… Could you explain why a random old lady knows me? And seems to be interested in your sex life?"

"I have no comment on the latter. But as for the former… well. That's Miss Pearl. Her husband passed away two years ago. I uh… treat her to dinner every now and then."

Astrid looked back again as a door opened, and a middle-aged man popped his head out. "Cale got the girl?"

Astrid's mouth fell open. How many people had he talked to about her?

"Cale-" she started, but he quickly pulled her in and flipped on the lights.

"OK, so one or two people I've talked to about you. A lot. Some of us don't have a brother to confide in." Cale latched the door closed.

The words never connected with Astrid.

Boxes.

The room was lined with boxes.

Stacked boxes, lined up neatly along the walls.

It was so... hollow.

Cale had almost left.

The *objective* reality crashed into her.

Her hunger to claim, her urgency to make it unmistakably, *painfully,* *and excruciatingly clear* that Cale was hers, that had not come from simple bodily lust.

Multiple times tonight, she had repressed the thought that she very nearly lost Cale for good. That *she* had almost pushed him away for good.

What would she have done if she hadn't been able to verbally manipulate him into confessing on that coaster? What would she have done if he had left? Would she chase him? Would she beg him to come back? Would she have pretended like she didn't care while crying herself to sleep every night?

She didn't know, and that scared her.

"Self-sabotaging tendencies" was a phrase her therapist had spent years trying to get into her head, and right now she felt it.

But no, the reality wasn't that she was here to stake some physical claim.

This was not her redeeming a prize.

The reality was that those boxes were *ready*.

Cale had been *ready*.

Ready to make a move that sounded like he *genuinely* thought would lead to a happier her.

She felt Cale's hand find her back, though he offered no words to fix things.

She loved that he didn't.

Her legs wobbled. She did nothing to stop the collapse onto her knees, nor the flood of tears that followed. This was a dam that had filled for some time.

Her body shook, and her face fell forward as if hiding accomplished anything.

A word managed to surface through the noise: Why?

She attempted to pick up the shattered pieces of rationality that lay around her. Anything. Absolutely any logic that could explain...

Her gaze turned back up to the boxes, causing a new surge of sobs and shakes.

"I did this. This is my fault." The words flowed with the tears, uninhibited.

Cale finally spoke, with *conviction*, "*Absolutely* not."

The authority in his tone caused Astrid's breath to catch, holding back the current for long enough for her to grab a branch.

He took a seat next to her, and she felt the warmth of his arm embrace her. His own staggered breaths joined her own.

She shook her head, "Don't lie to me, Cale. I'm the one who pushes away, not you."

"No, Astrid. *We* did this; it has always been a *we,* from day one. And today, *we* fixed it. Together."

A sob escaped. Then another.

Wait, that second one wasn't hers...

She looked over and found Cale's own dam had broken, though it ran beneath eyes that held firm and unwavering.

Astrid was unsure if she had ever seen him cry, even in the earlier days when their fights carried venom. Was this part of the new Cale or a piece of the old one she had never been exposed to?

"No crying, idiot," she said, wiping her own eyes, "You're apparently the strong one now."

They both laughed, a broken sound with multiple layers of mixed emotions.

"No. Not strong. I... learned to build my own armor from someone I care deeply about." Cale took a deep breath, regaining a piece of stability.

Astrid took a moment to mirror his breath, finding a center in his regaining composure.

Seconds passed, possibly even minutes, serenading the boxes with a duet of synchronized breaths, sniffles, and lingering sobs.

When she spoke again, her voice had partly cleared up, "You just… You set this all up. You took the initiative, while I spent all day being my stubborn old self."

Cale laughed weakly, "Oh man. You have no *idea* how many people it took to convince me to talk to you before I left. I was ready to ditch and hate myself forever for your sake."

Something in that sentence grounded Astrid...

With a slightly-less-than-dignified sniffle, the tears dried, letting her focus.

She cocked her head, "Cale, how many people have you talked to about us?"

He ignored the question, "Ultimately, it was Miss Pearl. She guilt-tripped me. Told me to do it in honor of her dead husband. Clever old coot… She even came up with the whole amusement park plan."

Astrid decided to let the "how many people" question drop, for now.

"That old lady? I'll have to express my gratitude somehow, I suppose."

"Make her some of your cheese bread. But without the pockets of hot sauce, you so lovingly surprised me with that one time. It's one of her favorite stories."

Cale smiled, moving his arm from her back and down to her hand. Almost intuitively, she leaned against him, and her head fell to his shoulder.

It was warm, comforting in ways she couldn't have expected.

He continued, "I think you undersell yourself, though. I have put an *obsessive* amount of time into understanding your limits, and I could tell the effort you brought to the table. Astrid, you could have held that confession over my head for *years*. But you kissed me."

The thought of using that confession as ammunition had never even crossed her mind. The relief she had felt, the pressure that had been released on that coaster, had been *overwhelming*.

"I wanted it to end. I wanted you," she admitted.

Cale nudged his shoulder into her and squeezed her hand, "See? This has always been a two-player game."

She worked through that assertion. Were they doing it again, now? Crafting a new game that would lock them in place? It seemed like they were, but maybe the place that locked them in was better.

“This… us… We *will* work, right?" she said cautiously, almost afraid of any answer he would provide.

She expected a witty remark, a deflection, or a downplay of the concern.

But he contemplated, as if it were a concern of his too.

He took a deep breath, "No clue... But I think we both know we need to try."

It wasn't an answer she had expected, but the honesty alleviated significantly more of her concern than a "yes".

*They* were in this *together*.

For better or worse.

When she didn't respond, he followed up, "A wise bartender once told me that the best relationships are less focused on permanence. They are a celebration of connection. The permanence then becomes a byproduct that holds strong as long as the connection remains."

A celebration of connection… that's what today had been.

It had been all the best parts of their lives, but shedding the worst. It didn't matter if this new Cale was different from the one she knew before, though she still had a lot to mentally work through on that topic.

That connection was still there.

Astrid slowly stood up, pulling him up with her.

They walked down the hall, into the bare bedroom.

The anticipation and passion from before had long since passed. They would have time for all that later.

What she craved now was to feel him there, with her.

So they lay on the bed, curled into each other, fully clothed above the sheets. The room was silent except for their calm breaths.

Nothing more was said.

They had used up their emotional fifth wind on something much more important than a physical claim.

They kissed gently for a while, but it wasn't long before their all-encompassing exhaustion took them both into a deep and contented slumber.
