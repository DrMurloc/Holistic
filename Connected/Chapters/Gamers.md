**Leo**

Leo poked at the tablecloth with his fork, the busy steakhouse bustling around them. His mind mulled over the revelations of the last three days. Cale had clearly overstepped the intentions he had communicated. Astrid hadn't come home since that day… They were moving too fast.

A soft hand stilled his fidgeting, he took a deep breath.

“I know, I know…” He said, looking over into May's dark brown eyes. She was all knowing, no thought of his escaped her inner eye.

Her black hair was tied back in a braid and curled onto her shoulder just the way he liked. She wore the purple dress that perfectly complimented her dark skin, just for him. She clearly had put much more thought into his own birthday than he had, and he loved her for it.

“It's your birthday, this is about you. They're on their own journey.” She said, right as always.

“I just… she's my sister, and he was my best friend for a decade… I want to see them happy. They're rushing it, right?” He turned his hand to hold hers.

“Maybe. That's for them to know. Your mother will dig into them enough.” May squeezed his hand and landed a soft kiss on his cheek. Her job as an elementary school teacher left her way too understanding.

“You're right. Ok. Ok. This isn't about them. I can do that.” Leo took a deep breath. He looked out the window, and exhaled sharply seeing the couple approaching.

“What did you say Cale's job was?” May asked, following his gaze.

“Junior Engineer?”

“Those are six hundred dollar shoes.” May observed. Leo looked down, they looked like just very nice sneakers to him. They were a very elegant colored brown, definitely designed to satisfy multiple levels on the casual to formal scale.

“How would you know that?” Leo looked back at her.

“Father owns three pairs from the same brand. He calls them his ‘casual strollers’.” May laughed softly. Leo constantly forgot her father was a CEO. She seemed so far removed from that lifestyle and mentality.

“See! They're holding hands!” Leo pointed as the two exchanged several words before coming in.

“Leo, you were just worrying about them having sex. You saw a picture of them kissing. Hand holding I think is expected.” He felt her hand move to his back, scratching softly. He smiled, leaning back into it.

“Today is about me. You're right. I'm sorry.”

He watched the two enter, the host gestured to the table Leo and May sat at. Leo stood up, taking another deep breath. He loved these two with his whole heart, he could be supportive.

“Leo!” Cale grinned, running over to his friend. They hugged, and Cale whispered “Thank you so much.” Leo pat him on the back and smiled.

May and Astrid exchange their own hugs briefly, and Astrid followed up with a hug of her own for Leo. “Thank you.” she also whispered.

Leo felt a rush, he *had* done this, and they did seem happy. Three days and they weren't murdering each other. Maybe May was right. He and her had been talking about motivating Astrid to move out anyway, maybe the jump to staying with Cale wasn't such a bad thing.

“I see that Meredith isn't here yet.” Cale said, eyeing the remaining empty seats.

May laughed softly, Leo raised an eyebrow, “You know you are going to be dealing with a dragon protecting her treasure, right?”

“Very prepared. Well, prepared to be unprepared.” Cale smiled and leaned back.

“Prepared for an ass whoopin’.” Astrid grinned. Was she… excited for Cale to get torn apart by an angry mother? Actually, that checked out. It was good to know they still had a dynamic. For some reason that calmed Leo's concerns.

“The only ass whoopin’...” Cale started.

“Cale, no!” Astrid threw a hand over his mouth.

“See! See!” Leo stood up and pointed, looking back at May.

“No! Leo, we haven't even…” Astrid tried to get in.

May was laughing audibly behind a hand that failed to muffle the noise.

“I was going to make a very, very bad steak pun.” Cale finally said, moving Astrid's hand away. The table went silent save for May's snickering.

“Wait, you two haven't?” Leo sat back.

“No! Not that it's any of your business.” Astrid sat back, her hand returning to Cale's lap.

“You've been gone for three days, what have you been doing?” Leo asked. He felt May's hand return to scratching his back and he relaxed again.

Astrid and Cale glanced at each other briefly, then said at the same time “Redecorating.” Their eyes flickered back with a hint of surprise.

“What… what does that mean?” Leo asked, severely confused.

“What Leo means to say, is that we are happy you two have found something beautiful.” May interjected. Leo sighed and leaned back, placing a hand on her lap.

“Thank you, Leo.” Astrid said pointedly.

And then the dragon entered. The middle aged woman wore a fashionable white romper, sunglasses that seemed to glare, with a designer pink purse slung over her shoulder. Behind her trailed their father, a walking stereotype of an 80s poindexter: bow tie, glasses, cardigan and all.

The table went silent, Leo stole a look at Cale who simply wore a confident smile. What in the hell was that confidence? He was about to get ripped in two.

The hostess attempted to greet the woman, but their mother ignored her, taking in the room through those angry sunglasses. Her eyes fell on Leo and smiled, she walked over leaving the hostess mid sentence.

“And there is the birthday boy!” She said, Leo stood to receive the hug with a smile. His dad followed up for a second hug and a pat on the back.

The woman greeted May with a half shoulder hug, then proceeded to Astrid who stood up for a full hug. The entire table went quiet, focusing on Cale and their mother.

“Meredith.” Cale nodded, his smile never wavering.

She raised her sunglasses, her piercing blue eyes drilled into Cale. The entire restaurant seemed to hold their breath.

“Well?” She asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Mom…” Astrid started, a sharp raised finger silenced her, and Cale placed a hand lightly on her arm.

Cale grinned and stood up, extending his arms wide and approaching the woman. She let out a light squeal of excitement and ran forward to accept the hug. Leo’s father leaned back with a knowing smile, the rest of the table simply stared in shock.

“What?” Astrid said, speaking for everyone.

“Oh honey you're the one dating him not me.” The woman said, pulling back from the hug but holding Cale’s hands with excitement.

“But… you hate him!” Astrid almost seemed upset at this turn of events.

Leo’s father spoke up, “We never hated him. We hated what you two put each other through.”

Astrid looked exasperated, “Why did literally everyone but me seem to know we were into each other?”

A manager approached with a bottle and six glasses as Meredith and Cale took their seats. “A bottle of the house champagne, to celebrate the new couple.”

The whole table stared in confusion, except for Cale who looked up at the ceiling avoiding eye contact with anyone, the manager began distributing glasses.

“Excuse me?” Leo asked.

“Now Leo, we never turn down free bubbly.” Mother excitedly held up her glass.

“Mister Drab is a friend of the staff. We are excited to see him escorted by the wonderful Miss Astrid.” The manager poured each of them a glass. All eyes turned to Cale who continued to look at the ceiling.

“Cale, the other night when you said you had many people you talked to…” Astrid's eyes narrowed.

Leo leaned back to May, “This is still about me, right?”

May squeezed his hand, “It appears I underestimated the amount of drama and attention those two would soak up.”

“Understatement of the year.” Leo whispered back.

“Don't worry,” she leaned in and whispered in his ear, “I'll make sure you get all the attention you deserve tonight.” He sat up straight, looking over at her. She opened her mouth very slightly and made a subtle biting motion. Leo moved his hand to her leg and squeezed, shifting his legs slightly while trying to tune back into the conversation.

“OK but how many bars, Cale? Don’t you ignore me!” Astrid was demanding. Leo’s parents both were laughing, the manager had left. The moment was interrupted by a server looking to take their order.

As the dinner progressed Leo found himself steadily coming to terms with things. His concerns had been for naught. He had always been third wheel to those two, this was a return to normal, a normal he hadn't felt for years.

“You're right.” He leaned over to May as the steaks were dropped off.

“I mean, yes. But what about?” He could hear her smile.

“It's their journey. Same way I have my own and we have our own.”

“Are you saying you'll take it?”

“I'm not sure.” Leo had a big job offer open for a UX position that was many steps ahead of his simple web design job. But it would mean giving up a lot of bandwidth he had for maintaining community tooling that Cale and he had built out years earlier.

The project of course was self sustaining at this point. But Leo had ambitions for it, for the arcade community that he and Cale grew up in, that he met May in. Accepting the job felt like abandoning something that meant so much to him.

May knew the value it provided, she used the tool almost every day. But she constantly insisted that Leo needed to spend more focus on himself and not just supporting others. He knew she wasn't wrong, but that didn't make it easier.

He slowly turned his attention back to the ongoing conversation, somewhat distracted.

“-her name is Mimi Galaxy.” Their father was saying. He was holding up a phone where a pink haired girl danced and sang on the stage, a vast sea of glowsticks waved in unison before her.

“I'm pretty sure you two collectively have more years of life than that entire stadium combined.” Astrid stated.

Leo's mother chimed in “Well your father and I were at this very nice brunch with crossdressers-”

“They are called Drag Queens. They ‘slayed’ us.” Father explained, beaming at the youthful knowledge.

“I don't think that's how that word is used.” Cale pointed out. This got a look from Astrid.

“Yes. Well, one of the… queens? Told us about this Asian tradition where millions of unemployed young adults use all of their parents' money to worship, and I kid you not they actually call them this, ‘Idols’.” Mother explained. She wore a look of amusement.

“You know, I'm not sure that's the best descriptor of the J Pop scene, but it is scarily close to accurate.” Cale had his own look of amusement he wore, Astrid just looked shocked at her parent's new interests.

“Anyways. Your father and I are going in five weeks.”

“Japan is the hip place to be right now, I was reading about this thing called a ‘maid cafe’. They cast love spells on your drinks and say ‘moe moe cute’!” Their father beamed again.

May and Cale chuckled, Astrid's mouth only dropped further open. Leo himself just let the energy play out, soaking it in. It seemed everyone at the table had their own life changes happening.

“Wait, so you two are going to Japan too?” Astrid said. Everyone's eyes fell on her.

“When you say ‘too’...?” Leo's mother leaned in scrupulously.

“We may have tickets to go next week. As a celebration for Astrid's grand entrance into the Legal world.” Cale admitted. Leo cocked his head at this, leaning forward over his now empty plate.

“Cale, can you afford a two person trip?” Leo asked.

“Please.” Mother's eyes rolled, “Leo, sweety, the combined cost of this man's outfit is well over three thousand dollars. And he brought her to Harmony of all places. Whether he won the lottery, became a successful drug lord, or engineer is simply paying more than I had thought, I do not care. Astrid is his problem now.” Mother sat back with a satisfied grin, her hand resting on Father’s.

“Mom!”

“Just know you're picking up her therapy costs. They're your fault anyways.” Mother continued.

“*Mom*!” Astrid got out of her seat, glaring at the woman. Cale laughed and Leo could hear May's trademark silent chuckle. Even father laughed at this.

Almost as if to make a point, Cale smoothly passed his card off to the nearby server. “Director of Engineering, since the cat is out of the bag.” He said simply. This got a very approving nod from Mother.

Leo blank his surprise away and returned to sitting back as the four discussed travel. May's hand gently took his again.

“See? They'll be fine. People can take care of themselves, you don't need to be the solution to everybody's problems.” He heard.

She was probably right. But it wasn't that easy. He couldn't describe it. The sense of purpose he felt… It was addicting. He couldn't picture a corporate job replacing that.

Everyone slowly went their separate ways. Cale and Astrid followed Leo and May back to the house for a short while to pick up more clothes and several other items of Astrid's. It wasn't long before Leo and May were left alone.

“Now, Birthday boy, where do we begin?” Astrid stepped forward, running a hand down her dress. Leo took a step forward with a grin, but felt his phone vibrate. He pulled it out, a message from one of his users…

“Leo, really?” May crossed her arms and glared.

“The site is down. Hold on.” Leo turned to go to the basement.

“Leo! It can wait! They're just sweaty gamers!” May stomped her foot.

Leo paused, looking between her and the door to the basement, “It will just be a minute, I think I just need to kick it.”

“No, I'm not doing this. That project isn't healthy for you.”

“Isn't healthy for me?” Leo turned from the door, facing her. “It got me my job! It's how I met you! How is that not healthy?”

“Am I a side effect or the goal?” May asked, her eyes were sharpened. She was forcing this to be a decision point. She didn't understand though, he could have both. Forcing a choice wasn't necessary.

“That's not… look, in the amount of time we've been talking I could have hit the restart button.” Leo opened the door. She didn't say anything, simply watched with a frown.

He hurried downstairs, pulled open the command tool and typed a few things in. The VM began to reboot. He watched for a few moments as it spun up, saw the green “connected”, and ran back upstairs.

May was gone. Her car was missing in the driveway.

It began to dawn on Leo just how much he had screwed up. Had he… had he really just picked fixing a tool that maybe twenty people would use tonight over a night of passion with the woman he loved?

He checked his phone, no messages from May. He started typing out something to her, an apology, an explanation, his side of the argument. But he couldn't. He knew the points she would make back. And he didn't have answers for them. She was right, and that meant he was wrong. She was always right. Why would you argue with someone who was always right?

He pulled a chair up to the window, and sat waiting for her to come home. He sat there for hours, his mind circling on how much he had fundamentally messed up. Three years. He threw three years out for a server restart.

They had gone through this before, it was a cycle. They would be happy for months, he would do something dumb. She would leave. Four days later they would find themselves going at it in an arcade bathroom, continuing like nothing happened. He didn't know why she kept coming back, he didn't deserve her.

She didn't come back that night.

Leo couldn't even find the energy to cry. He just sat, watching the empty parking space, thinking through every decision he had made that had led him to this catastrophic failure.
