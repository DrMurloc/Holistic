He pulled it out, knowing that many messages at once could only come from the community Discord.

"The site is down," He stated.

May groaned.

After several taps, he continued, "Hold that thought... for like... five minutes."

Leo took a step towards the basement door. It would probably be a VM restart; this happens every few months. Standard pain points of self-hosting.

"Leo." May's voice shifted to a thick mass of incredulity, and Leo's blood froze.

He knew that voice.

Anger came after that voice.

And there was no stopping it.

His eyes turned up to meet May's; her arms were already crossed, leaning back slightly.

The whole conversation played out in Leo's head.

He knew *every single word* that was about to leave their lips.

It was up to May on whether they needed to say them, which would lead to her leaving, or if the silent argument that played out now was enough.

Leo waited, breath held, hand on the door to the basement.

"They're only sweaty gamers, Leo," May started, beginning to pace.

*Fuck*. *Fuck. Fuck. Fuck. Fuck. Fuck.*

Her voice definitely held back anger; she was *trying* to control herself.

Maybe Leo had a shot at managing this?

He tried for a line that had worked in the past, "You're one of the sweaty gamers, May, the most important one. It will literally be a minute, I just need to kick it."

She shook her head and dug in, "I'm just a sweaty gamer to you?"

*Fuck. Apologize? What does she want?*

"That's not what... I'm sorry. I-"

"Don't apologize. Leo, for fuck's sake."

*Fuck*. *Ask directly?*

"What do you want then?"

"I want you to prioritize you, me, us, for one fucking day!"

"I already lost one family today, I don't want to lose another."

"What the fuck does that even mean? You *lost* a family? What the fuck am I, Leo?"

*Fuck*. *Why can't I get any of these right? Should I just concede? But she'll just get mad that I caved.*

"May, you... That's not... Fuck, you know what I mean!"

"Do I? Because you clearly are prioritizing concerns of Astrid, Cale, and a collection of antisocial nerds over *us* right now."

*Fuck. What do I use when Astrid digs in her heels...*

"May, are you venting or problem solving right-"

"Don't pull that bullshit on me, Leo. I'm not Astrid or Cale, I don't need a fucking mediator. I need you to man the fuck up."

*Fuck*. *She's not going to accept meaningful conversation; she's clearly venting. I need to defer.*

"What does that... May, this clearly isn't the best time for us-"

May's pacing stopped, and her glare darted at him. "Oh, you want to 'talk about it later'. When the fuck is later, Leo? Should we put it on the fucking calendar? Is this like how you always want to talk about redistributing Cale's portion of the mortgage *later*? Like how you want to talk about that bitch ex of yours that *still* bothers you *later*? Like how you want to talk about *kids* *later*?"

The room went silent.

The emptiness of the house allowed that last, most deterministic assertion to echo, reverberating with the implied finality.

Leo knew there was no answer he had for her.

She was right.

He solved the problems he could and avoided the ones that didn't have clear answers.

He was weak, always had been.

He built communities and families, supported friends, to hide that weakness, but it was still there.

And May saw it, because May knew him.

May was *always* right.

May's hands went to her temples, and her eyes squeezed shut.

For a brief moment, silence hung. The house itself seemed to avert its eyes and seal its lips in an attempt to defuse the situation.

"Leo, I can't... I can't right now."

*Fuck*.

There they were. The final trigger words.

It wasn't impossible... She merely needed a minute to cool down. They were always more productive in the riddens of an argument.

It would only take Leo a minute.

"May, in the amount of time we just spent talking about this, I could have restarted the VM, and you would already be bent over the kitchen counter."

Leo attempted a smile to disrupt her.

She didn't budge an inch; her eyes remained squeezed shut.

Leo sighed and opened the door, practically leaping the entire flight of stairs down.

As he expected, the command window had a series of red network failures. In a few keystrokes, the window filled with a series of scrolling text.

After a mere thirty seconds, it lit green, and he confirmed the site was up.

He returned to the first floor, hoping so desperately to find her in the armchair by the door.

But he knew better.

It was no surprise to him when he found the room empty.

It was no surprise to him to see her car turning the corner at the end of the street.

It was no surprise to him that she didn't return that night.

The only things different today were that there was no Astrid to comfort him, and he didn't get a message from Mother stating that May was staying at her place.

The house was empty.

It was only Leo.

He didn't cry. What would it solve?

He fell asleep on the couch, desperately hoping to hear the door open as literally *anyone* returned with some semblance of regret for having left.

It didn't.

Even the soft hum of the air conditioning unit cut out, plunging the house into absolute silence.

Leo was left alone.
