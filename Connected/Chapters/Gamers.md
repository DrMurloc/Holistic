**May**

Arrows cascaded, and pride flowed through May's veins like fine soju.

May was immersed in methodical madness conducted by the electric-charged rhythm of techno. Blue "perfects" pulsed in sync with the machine-gun clacking of shoes against acrylic and metal.

The arcade around here was a non-existent backdrop; only the screen, the pads, and her body existed.

**Now, I'd like to take a moment to explain exactly what is going on, as it is typically** ***quite*** **important to a few members of our cast.**

**Some portion of readers are thinking, "May is playing DDR, what is there to explain?"**

**"Um, actually, it said the perfects are** ***blue,*** **not** ***yellow.*** **May is most certainly playing the superior game made by the Korean company 'Andamiro' called 'Pump it Up'."**

**They would likely then launch into a five-minute speech about why a game with diagonal arrows instead of orthogonal was superior, including a significant quantity of terms you don't understand like "doubles", "timing windows", "chart quality", and "skill cap".**

**If you are wondering which one is actually "superior", I will just say that people have a tendency to get rather specific with trying to rationalize their personal preference when the real deciding factor was "which game did you have better access to in an Arcade".**

**For those not familiar with DDR, Pump It Up, Stepmaniax, In the Groove, or the concept of rhythm games as a whole, the games are simple in concept.**

**Arrows go to the top of the screen, and you hit them with your feet.**

**In May's case, you do this** ***very*** **fast.**

"Push, less than ten seconds left, *push*." Leo's voice came from behind May.

Her body all but teleported from one pad to another, foot stretching for the beginning of the last pattern.

She... just... needed to...
