Cale smiled; it was his turn to look away. He placed one of the pieces of fish in his mouth, and it was delicious, but his mind was elsewhere.

“Gochujang burrata with charred scallions, served with a soy-balsamic reduction.”

Astrid pulled her phone out, “Hold on, Leo is asking how it’s going, I swear I’m not being rude. ” Her fingers began to tap away at the screen.

“On that note… ” he pulled his phone out again and fired off the picture to his cousin. It would make her day.

“I mean… that camping trip… I think we almost did. ” Astrid blushed.

Their plates were swapped: “Soondubu ravioli in an anchovy dashi brown butter sauce”.

Cale cocked his head, “How so? You mean when we were stuck in the tent with pouring rain and spent an hour fighting about whether brown or black bears were native to North America? The funny thing is, we had our phones right there. Could have just looked it up.”

“I almost kissed you just to end the argument,” Astrid admitted.

Cale’s eyes widened with a grin. “I had a very similar thought myself, to be honest. Man, so close but so far. Not sure we would have worked well back then, though. ” He chuckled, popping a ravioli into his mouth.

“Absolutely would not have. We acted like eight-year-olds until we were twenty-two. And then honestly, I think we just saw each other less, so it gave us time to simmer. ” Nostalgia played across her face.

They basked in this moment, eyes drinking in each other like a limited resource. It was a shame the table was between them; Cale could think of nothing he wanted more than to return to that silly swan boat.

His phone vibrated and he stole a glance. Leo had responded, “You are NOT allowed to ruin this! (Congratulations!!!!!!!!!!1111!!!1one!!)”

As he watched, another message appeared, “Wait, that place looks REALLY nice. No making moves on the first date! ” Followed by one more, “Wait, this is my sister, no making moves at all! What the fuck are you doing? This wasn’t part of the agreement!”

“What’s so funny? ” She asked.

“Leo is being dumb.”

Her own phone vibrated and she checked it. A splash of red filled her face as she put the phone away, “I’m going to just leave that on silent. ”

The server interrupted the awkward moment, “Black cod alla jjigae”.

Cale, thankful for a distraction, eyed the bowl placed in front of him, then looked up at the server, “Now, would you consider this a soup or a stew? ” Astrid hadn’t even recovered from the previous blush; her hand promptly went up to cover her face.

“Jjigae is literally translated as ‘stew ’ as I understand it. ” The server responded.

The server looked between Cale and Astrid for a brief moment. A glaring eye peeked between two of Astrid’s fingers.

“They would be wrong, sir.” The server said flatly, and he carried just the faintest smile of understanding.

“Thank you! A rather appreciated insight into international culture, you are of great help! ” Cale exclaimed. The server nodded and removed himself from their silly antics.

“Idiot,” Astrid said once the man was out of earshot, although she was clearly repressing laughter.

Should he? He held a card that could only be used once. It had been in his pocket for years.

“If you really want to own the trope, you can just call me baka. ” The card was thrown on the table with a wink. Oh, this better be worth it.

Her face burned brighter red, a raw mixture of fury and repressed laughter. For a moment, it seemed she was unclear on which she wanted to commit to. A hand moved to her mouth to cover the smile. Here it comes.

She mumbled something.

“I’m sorry, I legitimately didn’t catch that. ” Cale leaned forward.

“It’s not like I like you or anything, baka. ” Her hand moved from her face while she spoke, narrowing her eyes and giving a very convincing pout.

The hand was promptly returned to her mouth as she managed to turn an even brighter red. It was Cale’s turn to burst out into laughter. It was infinitely better than he ever dreamed.
