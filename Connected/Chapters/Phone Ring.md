## Phone Ring

**Cale**

Cale's phone rang; it was an unfamiliar number.

Given his current task, he probably should have ignored it.

But he was feeling good today.

It was a good day.

Cale was in a good mood.

Why *not* make a telemarketer's day with some casual chit chat? He was sure they were bored, and he wanted everyone to feel good today.

Because, as established, it was a good day.

He picked up the phone and held up a finger to the woman who had been helping him. "Go for Cale."

"Uh, hi." A soft, feminine voice.

Good opening pitch, so far.

"Hi!" he repeated back with no deficit of cheerfulness.

"I'm sorry, give me a moment; I hadn't actually expected you to pick up," the terrible salesperson responded.

It must be their first day. Cale wondered whether the call was being recorded for quality assurance purposes.

"Nothing important going on here,” Cale lied. “Take your time. I have to say these scam calls are getting more and more convincing.”

Cale winked at the woman behind the counter. She smiled and nodded.

"No! Not a scam! It's about Zoey," she said.

Cale raised an eyebrow.

Well, there's a good sales pitch.

He excused himself to the harsh outside. The humidity and sun pelted him with ferocity as cars and people passed.

"Listen, if you have that girl hostage, I highly recommend you let her go for your own sake. She *will* have you watching cheesy romance movies in a matter of hours, and you will hate that you *love* it."

Cale managed to find a spot of shade behind a bus stop shelter, turning to face into the store.

The girl on the other side of the line laughed, "Too late, fortunately."

"Fortunately?" Cale nodded slowly. "Could I get the name of my cousin's mystery kidnapper?"

Cale knew who it was without asking, of course. Zoey talked endlessly about this girl.

Naina responded, "Naina."

"And how can I help you with your Zoey situation, Naina?"

Cale held up a hand, signaling five minutes to the woman who peered curiously out the plate-glass window. There were not many things that would take priority on this: the goodest of days. Zoey's happiness was one of them.

"That's the thing, I don't know if it's even a situation," Naina responded.

"Is she OK?" Cale said with no real concern. "You didn't feed her shellfish, did you?"

"Yes! Wait, no. Which answer means she's OK? Also, she's allergic to shellfish? Good to know."

"No, she just hates it. Puked one time when she found out there was shrimp in an egg roll." Cale's eyes trailed a bus as it pulled up. Several people got off and walked by.

More laughter. "No… You know what, let me be direct. Does Zoey like girls?"

Cale supposed Zoey liked a lot of people.

Of course she would like–

Oh, wait.

"Oh, Naina, do you have any idea what you are getting involved in?" Cale cast the fishing line out.

"Hyperactive bundle of joy with the romantic ideals of a fourteen-year-old who just discovered young adult books? Complete with a smattering of insecurities about self-worth. The combination basically leads her to use others' love lives as a mechanism to deflect and mask her perceived inadequacies. She masks her intelligence, and I suspect she has dyslexia but I doubt she would seek accommodation for it unless someone close to her seeded the thought. She– oh, wait. Was that a rhetorical question?"

Cale took a half step back as if struck over the phone.

Oh boy.

Cale reeled in a whale.

Okay.

Goals:

Make sure Zoey is doing okay through an invested third party

Get Naina to trust him so he could use her to check up on Zoey more often

Get Naina what she needs to make Zoey feel loved

Easy.

Step one: lower her guard.

“No questions are rhetorical if you’re brave enough, Naina.”

Cale slipped on his grin for no one and let the flow of the script take over.

“I don’t know what that means,” Naina responded flatly.

“I don’t either! That’s why it’s rhetorical.”

A satisfying pause passed while Cale shot a finger gun at a passing roller blader. They did not return it.

“What?” Naina finally said.

“Exactly. Listen, Naina, you said ‘hyperactive’. Good word. So Zoey is energetic?”

Another pause.

“Yes? Well, most days. Some days she just… isn’t. And… um. I mean, with dopamine dys- with… Some days she’s just tired,” Naina stumbled through the words.

The words “just tired” slipped through Cale’s head, and for a moment his grin slipped off.

“Felt.” He muttered.

“What?” Naina said.

“Nothing.”

Zoey let Naina see her when she was tired, though; that wasn’t nothing. By most definitions, it was something. A pretty big something.

Cale opened his mouth with some leading question about schoolwork.

“She wasn’t always energetic?” Naina asked before he could start.

The mental image of Zoey in her young teens at a dinner table, physically restraining herself from bouncing with an almost painful-looking grimace, made Cale wince internally.

“No,” Cale pivoted. “She grew up with a dipshi- er… strict father. Military father. Everything was solvable with *his* routine because it worked for him. Never mind that his daughter was *not* him and that his obsession with control and–” Cale cut himself off and realized his knuckles had gone white against the phone.

He took a deep breath and let the white noise of the city wash over him and push the thought out.

“Hello?” Naina asked.

No. That daughter-beating piece of shit wasn’t the point. Zoey was.

“No, Naina. Zoey was quiet growing up,” Cale said.

“What changed?”

“What changed,” Cale repeated slowly as he mentally shuffled through some cards. How much should he say? He supposed Naina had gone out of her way to call an important cousin before making a move on a fragile girl.

She had his respect.

“The second she turned eighteen, on her birthday, she moved in with me. I showed up at her place in Pittsburg, helped her pack, and brought her back to LA. She-”

“In the middle of the school-year?” Naina interjected. “Wait, sorry. Go on.”

“No, please. You’re fine. But yeah, it was near the end of the year,” Cale said.

“I mean, that’s… Huh. And she did okay?”

Cale thought back to two months of getting Zoey to school and helping with homework while juggling career growth and trying to wrap his head around Astrid over late night talks with Diego. *He* had been the one struggling those months. And Zoey made sure her homework was done so she could get to bed on time, wake up, and make him pancakes with little dog faces on them.

Those pancakes got Cale through every day of be accountable for a team when he hadn’t even known how to be accountable for himself.

Cale took a deep breath and realized he was smiling.

Where did that come from?

He let it linger.

“Yeah. Zoey did well. I was fresh out of a weird housing situation and needed the company, anyways,” he said. “I think Zoey does better when she’s oriented around someone other than herself.” Cale thought for a moment, “Honestly? Same.”

“I mean, I’ve been sort of discovering that effect. It’s just hard to find the line on when it turns into fawning instead of interdependence.”

Cale turned the word over in his head a few times. “Fawning?” he finally asked.

“An automatic response to people please as a mechanism to maximize safety and avoid conflicts,” Naina said. “Common in neurodivergence, especially those with a history of trauma.”

Huh. That had a name.

“Doesn’t everyone do that?” he found himself asking.

**Wait, is he actually going to figure it out here? This is so much earlier than normal.**

“No. Well, to some extent maybe. But typically you label it when it becomes damaging to the self. Loss of boundaries, hard time saying no, people pleasing. Basically ‘I’m just a conduit to make everyone else’s lives better’.”

She just described his entire career.

That was just how you got ahead, right? Astrid had also called him out for catering his communication to the audience too much, but how does that compute against how successful he’d been?

**Come on, Cale, you’re so close.**

She said common in neurodivergence? He wasn’t familiar with the term.

**You’re** ***right there*****.**

Probably means just people with weird brains. Maybe he should ask? Couldn’t hurt to learn.

**Do it!**

Wait, Zoey. Focus.

**No!**

“You said interdependence. Good word.” Cale said.

“Yeah, like symbiotic. As opposed to codependent, which can be fragile and often turns parasitic.”

“Yeah, symbiotic. Like the fish.”

The bus station he paced next to emptied onto a bus and Cale plopped down on the bus stop bench beneath the shade. The bus hissed as it straightened up from the curb then drove off.

“I mean, there’s a lot more than fish that are– not the point. It was really nice for you to do that for Zoey. To just take her in.”

Cale nodded at nothing in particular. “She would have done the same for this idiot, if our roles were reversed. I just got lucky with the right job at the right time.”

“But you said it helped… Um… what did you do? To help?”

“We were idiots,” Cale offered with another nostalgic smile. “It was a complicated time for both of us. Re-evaluating relationships with people and family we loved. And I had money. So we spent that money. Because money might not buy happiness, but it sure can buy distractions.”

The pause Naina gave almost made Cale laugh. Like she was actually contemplating what he just said as serious.

“A lot of clothes shopping,” Cale jumped in before she spent too much time on it. ”You’ve seen her wardrobe, I assume. We made it to almost every thrift store in LA. You know Dr. Frank-N-Furter?”

A brief pause.

Niche movie, he supposed. “He’s–”

“Oh. No. I’m very aware of who he is. I was trying to find the right level of enthusiasm to put behind the word ‘yes’.”

Cale chuckled.

“Fair. Well, Zoey found this pimp suit. Faux fur, cane, hat, the whole thing, and right next to it was a full-blown Dr. Frank-N-Furter costume. Corset, garter belt, the gloves, and all. So, naturally, an hour later we were parading through a street festival. Zoey was the most aggressive pimp in the city, and I found out that fishnets are actually quite comfortable.”

There was a pause. Then a satisfying laugh. Cale’s chest shook with his own silent laugh.

“She’s been getting me to wear fishnet too. I think there’s a correlation. The longer you spend around Zoey, the more likely you are to look like you walked out of Hot Topic.”

“Gotta get her around Astrid…” Cale tried to picture Astrid in full-blown goth. Should he be scared or aroused by that image? Probably both.

“Um… Did you get any offers? From your… pimping.”

Cale nodded. “Three. The third one, we kind of panicked and ran when the guy actually started pulling out cash. Zoey haggled him up to $500. Honestly, in hindsight, I’m taking it as validation that it got that high.”

More laughter.

Cale stood as a few others arrived at the stop and stepped back out into the glaring sun. His leg bounced with energy as he looked in through the glass window.

Naina finally settled herself down and responded, “I mean, using shame-attacking exercises as a mechanism to help cope or de-mask from trauma-induced rejection sensitivity is kind of brilliant. You more or less provided Bakhtin’s Carnivalesque, a space to flip the script upside down. You just want to make sure the clown training doesn’t leave you with a fresh mask of painted makeup.”

Cale blinked.

A fresh clown mask.

Something about that hit much deeper than he was ready for today.

**Ask about it! Just say the words “what do you mean by that?”**

“Attacked the shit out of that shame, yeah,” Cale managed.

**No!**

An annoyance of teenagers on scooters passed by, temporarily forcing him to step out into the sun.

"Hold on, to clarify, are you a potential partner or her therapist?"

It was Naina's turn to pause. "Potentially both?"

How strangely fitting.

Cale nodded slowly while contemplating a bus ad displaying "discount catheters." He couldn’t quite process the words, which somehow snapped his brain out of picturing himself with a red clown nose.

**Damn you, Marley’s Medical Surplus!**

Back to the good day.

Astrid hated clowns anyway.

Cale finally mustered his thoughts. "Going back to your question. Zoey's never dated. Always seemed more interested in my dating life than her own. But you mentioned romantic ideals of a fourteen-year-old earlier? Lean into those. You’ll know real quick if she’s interested. She’s not necessarily subtle."

Another long pause, Cale shifted back into the shade.

"Oh."

Oh?

He had expected more... enthusiasm. "Oh? Sorry if I was misunderstood. Yes, Zoey likely can be into girls."

"That's not the part I'm worried about anymore," Naina responded.

The romantic ideals were a problem?

Cale paced in his tiny spot of solar protection, thinking through what Zoey had told him of this girl.

Zoey, who could spot soul mates from miles away before they had even met, was legitimately convinced that Naina wasn't romantically interested. And Naina was clearly romantically interested.

Would it simplify things to just say, “Zoey likes you, trust me, don’t overcomplicate it, you are going to make her very happy just by being there for her?”

**Yes, Cale.**

How would that play out? Zoey just accepts it, the two kiss, then they live happily ever after? Happy Zoey, happy Naina, happy Cale?

**That is** ***exactly*** **what happens if you just say the words “Tell Zoey you like her” right here, and right now. They will be cuddling under a sensory-friendly blanket with ice cream, watching Love Actually while Naina rambles off a bunch of fun facts about Alan Rickman and Mr. Bean.**

Could it be that simple?

**Please. Cale. I don’t have a table to flip nor hands to flip it with right now.**

No, Zoey deserved something big.

**<screams in the unintelligible language of the omniverse>**

"I don't want to speak for her, but I doubt you need to be eternally the living embodiment of Romeo and Juliet. Give her something grand on occasion, and she will be *thrilled.*"

“I feel like Romeo and Juliet should not be role models in this situation.”

Fair.

“Cyrano,” Cale offered.

“Try again,” Naina said.

“Phantom?” Cale tried.

“Book or movie?” Naina asked.

“There’s a difference?”

Silence. He’d need to ask Astrid about that.

“Great Gatsby? Atonement? Orpheus?” Cale paused. “Holy Grail?”

“Those are terrible– Holy Grail?”

“Lancelot! With the- and huge tracks of-” Cale paused. Where *had* he been going with that one? “The Notebook.” Cale pivoted.

“I mean, better but still- Wait, hold on. We’re on a tangent. Is there something she's always dreamed of doing? I think I got the picture."

A cruise? No. Paris? She’d love that. An entire orchestra to play an instrumental cover of Titanic? Maybe-

The woman's motion at the storefront window pulled him out of the thought again.

Oh right.

Cale was on his own tangent.

He held up a finger, and she disappeared with a smile that said, “God, this idiot better buy something expensive.”

"New York,” Cale concluded. “She loves Broadway but has never been. Follow a show with a nice dinner, then end in a nice bar with a live singer —probably mocktails. The setting is more important than the drinks. We're talking dim lights, wood; just search for the word 'speakeasy'."

"That sounds expensive," Naina stated.

Cale reminded himself that his budget was just a tiny bit higher than the average college girl's.

The threadstrings of his brain were already being tugged back inside, though.

"It is. Honestly, it might not be necessary; just be interested in her cheesy movies, and you'll win her heart. But in case you go the New York route, I'll send you some hotel and restaurant options that will find you a good balance on cost."

Naina paused again. "I… I would appreciate that. She's right about you. You are a nice guy."

"Nah, don't listen to her—I'm awful. I'm about to hang up on you. When I said I had nothing important going on, that was a lie."

"Oh! I'm so sorry. Thank you so much!" Naina stammered.

"Glad to help. Good luck with Zoey. You'll need it."

He hung up and quickly added the number to his contacts.

He would follow up later to figure out where she landed.

But for now...

Cale sauntered back inside the jewelry store, returning to his prior spot in front of a display case.

"I'm so sorry about that. Love waits for no one. Speaking of which, where were we? You said you could custom-make these with sapphire?"

He pointed down at one of the elaborate rings on the counter.

**Naina**

Naina hung up the phone and let her head fall forward into her folded arms. The silence of an empty campus filled in, easing the passive tension of important conversations with a stranger. Her little grated table sat off to the side of the quad, tucked away under several overlapping trees.

New York.

The word bounced around in her head.

Big buildings. Cars. And people.

Many, many people.

A brief breeze blew through and cooled her shoulders and arms, reminding her of just how exposed she was through the fishnet of her new shirt. Feeling the exposure against her legs under the long green skirt was also a new experience.

Honestly?

It was kind of comfortable.

Naina looked at her phone. A text came in from Cale: *“Tell me if you need help. I have a ‘Zoey’ budget.”*

She typed back, *“She bought me a bunch of clothes. She didn’t have an answer when I asked how she could afford them. I suspect I’ve already tapped into your ‘Zoey’ budget.”*

A quick response, *“Well spent. Take care of Zoey, Naina.”*

Take care of Zoey.

New York.

People.

Her head fell back into her folded arms.

Cale.

Distract yourself by analyzing someone new.

Surface area of many things but lacking depth on most of them. Knew how to play off of his mouth saying things before his brain processed them. The way he talked, like everything had to be his own original idea… Definitely an addiction to novelty.

It definitely felt like “my brain has picked a goal, and anything that deviates will get deflected from.”

Did *Cale* know he had ADHD?
