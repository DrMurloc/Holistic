## Octopus

**Asuna**

Asuna was surrounded by meat.

Signs for different forms of fried octopus lined the street behind countless food stalls.

Each and every stall had rows of the golden delectable treat sprinkled with delicate fish flakes that danced under heat lamps to various songs of "takoyaki", or some form of wagyu, advertised in a way that screamed, "Who are you kidding, you want this meat in your mouth?"

The signs were not wrong.

Asuna practically drooled.

She did want that meat in her mouth.

She did a brief wig and contact check in a passing window. Amber and brown. Pleated skirt. Sweater with “UTokyo” splashed across it. Naomi was in place.

“Y’all don’t need to check every minute. Ya look more conspicuous the more you check,” Hawthorn noted.

“Insecure girls check themselves in mirrors all the time,” Asuna sad flatly with her light smile. She pivoted her hips to pretend checking out the back of her skirt. She used the motion to glance over her shoulder.

That one couple with the tornado potatoes had been roughly on the same block as Asuna for the last thirty minutes. That one foreigner in the hawaiian shirt seemed familiar, but maybe she just saw a similar shirt earlier.

“You *are* an insecure girl,” Hawthorn said.

“Exactly,” Asuna said.

What did she say exactly to? Oh well.

She really shouldn’t be this paranoid. Honestly she probably could have been out as Asuna. For some weird, illogical reason, though, she wasn’t quite able to shake off the thought of “hey, you were almost murdered by someone who recognized Mimi. Maybe you should not have that happen again.”

Also being Asuna meant she needed to smile for the rare fan who recognized her. Which was so much more draining than Naomi’s tiny polite smiles.

The crispy scent of octopus and dough grabbed Asuna by the stomach and dragged her towards her stalwart bodyguard. Her eyes widened as they fell on the box of six delectable fried balls Hawthorn held.

And in his other hand…

*Wagyu skewers*.

Asuna growled.

And pounced.

The takoyaki stood no chance.

Two fried balls disappeared down her throat in a barrage of savory sweetness that threatened to burn her mouth. The first wagyu skewer followed suit. Three, maybe four chews. One more takoyaki ball and–

Asuna became very aware of a powerful gaze on her.

She looked up and popped the third golden pocket of umami into her mouth.

“What?” She said as she chewed.

Hawthorn stared.

Asuna stared back.

Oh.

Wait.

“You can have some too,” Asuna said.

“Y’all definitely were an only child,” Hawthorn noted.

Asuna reached for a fourth ball. “Seems hardly relevant.”

Hawthorn tapped the back of her hand and offered the second of the wagyu skewers instead. She took it with and slide all three pieces into her mouth at once.

“Besides.” Asuna swallowed. “I was basically alone after seventeen. Not like anyone was gonna give me handouts. If you want something, gotta get it yourself.”

Hawthorn’s chewing slowed as he contemplated the assertion.

How should she be standing right now? Normally she would just stick to the walls and get from point A to B. This whole “lingering” without the intent of *going* somewhere was nerve wracking.

Asuna glanced around to study how other people stood.

That girl. The high school girl.

Hands over the front of the skirt, slight lean onto a foot. Shoulders high but not too high… and…

“Y’all’re ridiculous.”

Asuna’s shoulders slumped as she lost the pose and glared at Hawthorn. “I almost had it. What do you mean I’m ridiculous?”

“Practicing standing?”

“Yeah?”

“Y’all can just stand.”

“Standing is a thing. You need to *do* it. I’m gonna do it right.”

“Standing is a *nothing*. There’s nothing to do..”

Asuna groaned, “You keep saying that. How can you *do* nothing. *Doing* nothing is *doing* something.”

Hawthorn took another slow bite. His jaw moved so slow that she swore she could hear it creak.

He was definitely mocking her.

“Do you want me to chew your food for you?” Asuna said. She slapped the high school girl’s smile back on and tried to soften her shoulders again.

“You *really* hate staying still,” Hawthorn noted.

“Yes,” Asuna said, practicing her polite voice. “It’s pointless. I have too many things to do, and not doing them makes my brain itch.” She finished the statement with a giggle and hand raised to her lips.

Not quite right. The giggle needed to be softer.

She’ll try it again later.

“Y’all know doing the high school naive girl routine standing next to a full grown man makes you stand out. People are gonna think you’re an escort.”

“It would be much easier if you didn’t walk around in a *trench coat* looking like you’re out to hunt demons or perform hits on shopkeepers who are behind on rent.” She tried the giggle again.

Better.

“It’s comfortable. Good weight. Was a graduation present.”

Asuna tilted her head with a finger on her lips, letting her hair dip sideways. “You’ve worn the same coat for sixteen years?”

“Affirmative.”

“Gross,” Asuna said and straightened back up.

“I *clean* it.”

“Sure.” The itch was too much. She needed to move. She needed to *do* something. She couldn’t pace out here, there wasn’t good space. Hawthorn still had her phone.

She shook her hands in the air briefly, then shifted to checking her nails to cover the motion.

“Seriously, y’all doin’ okay?” Hawthorn asked.

Asuna contemplated the question. Other than the fact that doing nothing was objectively the hardest thing she’d ever done… She felt fine. Anxiety. But that was to be expected, right? They had been cooped up in Hawthorn’s apartment for a week or so with nothing to do but video games. And now they were out in a different city.

And the event… the Tokyo Dome was coming up…

The itch burned. Should she ask for her phone back? They had so much to get done. But Hawthorn said Kenji was on top of things?

"The person at the corner to our right keeps looking at me," Asuna finally said.

Hawthorn looked in the direction she had indicated and grunted. "That's a toddler."

"That's what he wants you to think. Deal with him."

Damn kids, not even old enough to buy Mimi merch yet.

"Not kicking a toddler." Hawthorn took his second octopus ball.

"What if he had a knife?" Asuna raised an eyebrow and glanced at Hawthorn.

Hawthorn shook his head and snorted. "Then *you* take the knife from him."

"It would have to be me. You *clearly* don't know how to do that."

"That was the joke."

"It's not funny when you explain it, Hawthorn."

Another snort. "Y’all're insufferable."

"And *you're* a lazy bodyguard.”

“Y’all deflected. You doin’ okay?”

Finally, Hawthorn closed the box and handed it back to the vendor to throw away. He began to walk again, and Asuna fell into step next to him. The crowd parted slightly for them as they moved. The motion felt like it brought with it a fresh wave of oxygen to her central processing units.

“Better,” Asuna finally responded.

“Better is good.”

“Got that itch. Unsolved answers, ya know.”

She knew.

She was quite aware of that itch.

Asuna swung her arms a bit wider, any excuse to pump in more motion.

“Vannessa?” She asked.

“Yup.”

“Anything I can help with?” Asuna looked up briefly to find that stone gaze out across the crowd. She was never quite sure if he could hear her when he was like that.

“Maybe…” He said. “Maybe later…”

“I don’t know.” Asuna said. She let her mouth run just to say things. “I keep going back to that first question you asked.”

"Who's the target demographic for a gacha machine that's full of tiny vending machines on key chains?"

Asuna shook her head. "No, but, adults, twenty, thirtyish. Meta-novelty is the category. They're ironic? I meant the question from the hospital. I think you so elegantly put it as 'Who the fuck are you?'"

Hawthorn grunted. “That’s important. Can’t do everything.”

She still wasn’t quite sure he was listening. The way his jaw clenched and unclenched. Maybe she was overthinking it.

“Okay, but then what *do* I do? I can't even remember if I like being Mimi. I'm beginning to question if I even like running the business. Who knows, maybe I'm meant to race horses."

This earned a glance from Hawthorn. "Horses?"

Asuna shrugged. "Deeply afraid of them. But who knows? Who the fuck am I?"

Hawthorn stopped, his eyes settling on something in the distance. "Passion is the core of quality. Ya need to find your 'painting'. Something you can pour your heart into. Good work life boundaries..." his words felt distant, not quite there.

Passion? She was passionate about Mimi.

And organizing spreadsheets.

And helping the stage hands.

And doing market research.

How do you find good work-life boundaries when work *was* life? She enjoyed it all. Was she not supposed to?

"I don’t know. Maybe I’m still in burnout recovery. Trying to throw myself back into the fire. How much of my life was stumbling forward, and how much was passion? Was it all just overcompensation? I legitimately enjoy the business. I-"

Hawthorn came to a stop, and Asuna stumbled a few further steps before looking back.

His gaze was definitely out on something that wasn’t her.

Maybe the thought hadn’t been interesting enough.

She was overcomplicating it. The rest of the world had that whole “work life balance” thing solved, right? And the rest of the world were morons who spent money on gacha games.

She would figure it out.

Hawthorn muttered, "Goddamn."

"--Shibuya, they *absolutely* were on the left side." A sharp voice in English sliced through the crowd like a blade. Asuna stood on her toes to catch a glimpse.

"You can say that, but they were on the right side at the Namba station." A foreigner with black curly hair and glasses came into sight, walking next to a blonde with a blue ribbon.

"I. Have. *Pictures,* Cale!" the blonde said.

Cale?

Asuna's ears perked at the name.

It wouldn't be, though. Statistically impossible.

"I'm not saying you don't, Astrid. I'm simply stating facts."

Asuna almost stumbled while standing perfectly still.

Astrid? It was literally unbelievable.

Inconceivable, even.

The two began to pass by, and Hawthorn's head followed them with a broad smile.

Did he read the blog, too?

Hawthorn fell into step behind them, speaking in English, "Y'all know the side of the escalator you stand on changes from city to city in Japan."

The black-haired man, Cale, stopped and cocked his head before turning to face Hawthorn. A wide grin erupted on his face, and then he ran forward into an embrace.

"What?" Asuna and Astrid spoke simultaneously.

Hawthorn broke free from Cale's hug but kept an arm around his shoulder. "Drinking buddy from LA. His company was looking to acquire us. This is the man who convinced me to quit."

Cale did an exaggerated double-take. "You *quit*? I thought I convinced you to take a vacation."

"Yeah. Eleven months of vacation." Hawthorn gave the confident laugh and smile he used for deflecting in public. "This must be the one and only Astrid. An absolute pleasure. Just as beautiful as he described."

Hawthorn knew *celebrities*?

Well, technically, he was "it's complicated-ing” a celebrity. But he knew *other* celebrities?

"Cale. We. Are. In. A. *Different* country! How *far* does this go?"

Cale laughed.

This was clearly one of their ongoing... "fights"? Spats? Asuna didn't really know what to call them. They were more entertaining in person, regardless.

Asuna’s focus landed on Cales smirk.

Oh, that was a good smirk.

She found her hand rising to the back of her neck to mirror his posture. She forced it back down.

Later.

For now she needed to figure out what value she had to squeeze out of these lemons.

Wait. Blog.

She was behind if they were together.

She froze with her hand halfway into Hawthorn’s pocket as he glanced down at her.

She waited for him to tell her no.

To shake his head.

He just looked away and launched into an explanation of a poorly executed overseas acquisition. It sounded like Cale's American CEO didn't go drinking with Hawthorn's old Japanese CEO. Rookie mistake.

Text scrolled across the window while Asuna skimmed dates.

Yup, there it was.

Late April, Cale and Astrid were *together*.

Asuna didn't really believe in romance; it was inefficient. But something about those two making things work filled her with warmth. Small moments between sets, restroom breaks, shorter train rides, these two had been filler for Asuna’s life for some time.

She had to say something. Right?

"Congratulations, by the way." It came out stuck somewhere between “shy college student” and "I've never had friends in my life and don't know how to talk to people" businesswoman.

All heads turned to Asuna.

"Naomi, you know them?" Hawthorn asked.

Cale's eyes went wide. As if he was receiving a present for a birthday he forgot was coming up.

Astrid, on the other hand, appeared to have stopped breathing.

**Now, Asuna wasn't one to shrink in a social situation; she was already relatively small, even by Japanese standards. What would shrinking really do? Shrinking was a retreat, anyway, and like everything else, Asuna only did tactical retreats.**

**Under the sharp, narrowed glare that Astrid donned, one that bored into Asuna like a drill, a glare that almost made Hawthorn's harshest interrogation seem affectionate, she had no choice.**

Asuna *shrank*.

"It's... Is it *Cale* and *Astrid*? Right? I mean... The blog-"

"*Blog*?" Astrid's death stare, thankfully, turned to Cale.

Cale did *not* shrink.

Cale *basked.*

Oh.

The confidence he wore in that bask. She needed that one. She was not sure for what. But that hardly mattered.

Asuna remained perfectly still while she flipped to her camera app and took a picture.

"That's the word she said, yes." Cale nodded, head tilted back in pride.

Hawthorn took an amused step back, leaving the two idiots in their own little battlefield.

"Cale?" Astrid's voice sweetened, a very false smile playing across her lips.

Oh. That was a good smile.

Asuna snapped another photo.

"Yes. I'm Cale." Cale nodded.

"When she says the word blog...?"

"Typically, the word refers to a periodically updated article or post. Often of the informal or more casual nature, and following a central topic or idea." Cale nodded again.

Had he memorized that?

Astrid nodded, "Mhm." Her tone dripped with weaponized calm.

It felt like she was mentally perusing an armory of blades, making sure to get *just* the right one.

"Cale?"

"That's my name." Cale nodded..

"How many people would you say read this regular publication?"

"Oh, it's hard to tell." His hand went to the back of his neck, as if he were thinking.

And one more picture.

Wait, he had winked at Asuna. Was she part of this?

She flipped back to the blog.

"The last update four days ago had three hundred thousand reads."

Astrid blinked in shock. "Did you say *'hundred thousand'*?"

Cale let the grin play through, standing with pride, seeming to wait for the payoff from this grand play.

Hawthorn whistled. "You could monetize ad revenue at that volume."

Astrid's stare turned back to Cale, and her mouth opened.

"You are-"

Cale's grin widened as she started, but she cut herself off with wide eyes.

"Wait, four days ago? Time out. Cale! You didn't!?" Astrid turned and snatched the phone right out of Asuna's hands. Cale’s grin vanished.

Asuna was left staring at empty space.

What did she have on her phone? Well, other than pictures of them now. Which would be awkward. But the other tabs on her phone were…

Oh.

Project galaxy spreadsheets.

"Wait-" Asuna reached out.

"Astrid, no, it wasn't that!" Cale started, arms going up defensively.

"Then what could you possibly-" Astrid was interrupted by Hawthorn plucking the phone straight out of her hands.

"It was a post about going to Japan." Cale pulled his own phone out and passed it to Astrid. “I told you we needed to talk about it, then we left, and we’ve been busy and jet lag and–”

“We’ll talk about it later,” Astrid said.

“Astrid, I’m sorry. I–”

“I’m not mad, Cale.”

The few moments that followed were a mix of silence, apologies, and “not now, we’re in the middle of the street”s.

It was quite awkward.

For them.

Asuna didn’t care.

She reached for her phone, which still dangled from Hawthorn’s hand. Once again he glanced over, but didn’t stop her.

Having her connection to the infinite bottomless pit of “stuff to do” alleviated “the itch” considerably.

That should be wrong, though, right?

What would Hawthorn want her to do?

Asuna fired off a single message before putting the phone back in her pocket, to Kenji: *"I'm back, keep working with Hawthorn. Anything urgent for me?"*

Good enough for now.

Dip her toes. No need to dive back into the deep end.

"OK, ladies,” Hawthorn said, causing all heads to turn up to him. “I'm going to steal Cale here for some guy talk. Naomi, could you grab a table with our fabulous new friend, Astrid here?"

Asuna's head perked up.

Friend? Asuna had a friend?

That she would be alone with?

A chill ran down her spine. Which face did she use for “friend”?

**Hawthorn**

Hawthorn fired off a quick message to Asuna: *"Let me know if you need me to come back".*

The response was immediate: *"k".*

"She's cute," Cale stated as the two carved their way into the crowds, no real destination in mind.

Hawthorn grunted, pocketing the phone.

She was, in fact, cute. She was also a palm-sized tiger.

"So are you two...?" Cale fished.

"Complicated. Naomi is amazing, but Vanessa's still in there. The same damn grin."

"A wise bartender once said: oof." Cale bumped his shoulder into Hawthorn's arm.

Hawthorn extended an arm over Cale’s shoulders; Cale put his own around Hawthorn’s back. A distant but familiar position, one with significantly fewer tears this time.

“Y’all really didn’t tell her about a blog that had her name in it?”

Cale’s shoulders shrank under the weight of Hawthorn’s arm.

“Okay, in my defense–”

“Y’all’re gonna need a real good defense.”

“I actually don’t have one,” Cale admitted. “I didn’t have a single reader for years. Forgot about it for a month or two, and when I came back it… sort of had just… exploded. So I just… kept going?”

“With both of your names in it,” Hawthorn noted.

“First names aren’t PII,” Cale countered.

*That* was his defense?

Hawthorn couldn’t hold back the laugh. It shook both of their bodies. “Y’all think ‘Astrid’ and ‘Cale’ together doesn’t combine to be PII? What other persons could that information identify? Tell me you didn’t include the ribbon. ‘No one reads it, it’ll be fine’ is definitively security by obscurity.”

Cale groaned, “You know I suck at the security stuff. I have a team for that.”

“Ain't cybersecurity. It’s common sense,” Hawthorn squeezed the man’s shoulders.

“Yeah. Well. I apparently never came with that. I have… uncommon… sense. Rare sense. Or no sense. Nonsense. Definitely that.”

“Y’all’re an idiot,” Hawthorn noted.

“Hey, that’s Astrid’s word,” Cale bumped his shoulder into Hawthorn’s side.

The two turned off the road and onto a pedestrian bridge leading into an open-air shopping center.

A series of buskers performed along the bridge. The majority of the crowd swarmed around a beatboxing duo. Hawthorn and Cale stopped, shrouding their conversation in the noise of the crowd.

Hawthorn considered his friend. Cale was typing up some sort of extended apology on his phone. Hawthorn sure hoped the fool was included a note to follow up later in person. But that was none of his business.

As hopeless as he was, Cale knew how to listen. Would latch onto every single word and twist them around in an attempt to reframe as if his life depended on it. Was sort of what Hawthorn needed, to be honest.

And Cale already knew about Vanessa.

Very convenient that he was here right now.

A bit too convenient.

Unlikely.

Inconceivable, even.

“We’re working on *boundaries*,” Cale said as he wrapped up the text. “Mine. Apparently I had none. Surprise!” He slid his phone away and the two returned to their shoulder embrace.

“Not a surprise,” Hawthorn noted.

“What? You knew? And you never said anything?”

Hawthorn thought back to those two weeks in LA. Lot of whisky. Probably too much. How had that gone?

“I said, ‘y’all need to draw your line and stick to it’. Ya then turned it back on me instead of hearin’ it for yourself.”

“Huh. Did it help?”

“Spent a year erasin’ my own lines. Told myself I was goin’ back to basics,” Hawthorn said. “Build my life around a girl and work. Lost one and found out the other was killing me. What lines are there left to draw when you lost everything to draw ‘em through?”

“Hawthorn the poet,” Cale snickered. Hawthorn didn’t reward him with a reaction. Cale was just filling the gap while he processed. Hawthorn used to do the same thing.

Instead he let the kicks and snares of the beat boxers fill the gap. A mid-aged woman brushed into Hawthorn with brief “sumimasen”, which he returned with flash of a smile before letting his neutral face return.

“Naomi’s helping, though?” Cale asked.

Hawthorn grunted. “She’s got me playin’ video games.”

“No.” Cale pulled away and looked Hawthorn up and down. “Who are you?” He whispered with a conspiratorial glare.

Hawthorn snorted. “It’s been nice. She needed to slow down, I needed to speed up. Get my brain workin’ again without real consequences.”

“Wait, which games? Are you ‘playing’ or *playing*? Don’t tell me you’re just playing Call of Duty and qualifying it as ‘gaming’.”

Hawthorn shook his head. “No more Call of Duty. I banned that. Naomi started cussin’ out teenagers online.”

“That’s the entire point! Those kids are terrible and they need to know it! May agrees!”

“They’re just learnin’. Cale, they’re kids.”

“And how are they going to learn if *we* don’t tell them how shit they are?” Cale grinned.

Hawthorn raised an eyebrow. “Is that how *you* learned?”

Cale’s grin vanished. His hand rose to the back of his neck and scratched with a deeply averted gaze.

Fuck.

“BlazBlue,” Hawthorn said, extending the olive branch. “Been practicin’ Iron Tager. Naomi plays the prick Jin.”

“Ice car spam?” Cale asked.

“Is that what it’s called? I call it fucking annoying. I have to lean into Spark Bolt, but she’s learning to jump-cancel on blocks. Keeps punishing me on over-commits. Sometimes I just stay out of range. Makes her mad. Makes her make mistakes. She doesn’t think she’s playing the game unless she’s pressing a button. Easy to punish if I just wait.”

Cale gawked. “Okay,” He said slowly. “Plan. We are getting you to Gamfest. January. You’re gonna do that whole ‘grumpy old man’ look–” Hawthorn stared blankly. “--Yeah. That one,” Cale continued. “I’m gonna take out some bets. And you’re gonna sweep. Easy money.”

Hawthorn stared.

Cale grinned back.

“No.” Hawthorn said.

“Hawthorn! It’s not about the money, it’s about the fun of *winning* the money!”

“No.” Hawthorn repeated. “I don’t know what Gamfest is but there is *no* force in this world that will get me into a building full of sweaty freeloading video gamers.”

“Hawthorn. Hawthorn. My friend. My buddy. My rugged and well-built nighttime visitor of fantasies,” Cale slid his arm back around Hawthorn’s back. Hawthorn refused to react. “Right now? You’re the sweaty freeloading video gamer.”

Hawthorn continued to simply stare ahead.

Don’t give it to him.

Don’t react.

He doesn’t deserve it.

He–

“Did you say visitor of fantasies?”

Fuck.

Cale chuckled.

Fuck.

He had a point, though. Even if he adamantly chose to make it in the worst, most ‘Cale’, way possible.

Hawthorn had admitted he lost momentum. He was a fallen giant. His “vacation” had long overstayed and turned into something closer to *actual* non-productive… brooding.

Was Asuna his way out of that? Should he build a new routine around a new woman? Or was that just setting himself up for another fall? *Could* he build a new routine around a new woman?

He had no illusion that continued involvement with Asuna would suck him into her own journey. In many ways, it would scratch that itch that every person in leadership had for “what if I took what I knew to a smaller company and did it right from the start”. Both for her business growth and for her own.

He could see the future down that route. He knew it.

He would lose himself in her. Because like Asuna, like Cale, Hawthorn didn’t half-ass. It was all or nothing.

“You’re brooding,” Cale noted.

“I know I am,” Hawthorn replied.

“Just checkin’. Keep going. It’s kind of hot.”

Hawthorn squeezed his eyes shut, and he let the passive noise of passing chatter be his focus. Don’t reward him. Don’t do it.

Why was he excited to hang out with this man again?

“Was it your first failure?” Cale asked.

The word. “Failure”. It sank somewhere deep.

“No. I–”

“No, not like, ‘oops I got a bunch of people laid off’ failure.”

“That’s hardly an ‘oops’,” Hawthorn said. He knew what Cale meant, though. A failure that Hawthorn could never take back. A failure he didn’t see a path forward from. There was no going back to the time he had with Vanessa. There was no going back to how things had been at work.

They were closed dead ends.

“Yes,” Hawthorn admitted.

“High school? College? You aced everything?”

Hawthorn snorted, “Family could never afford college. Not for six kids, not supporting the rest of the family over the border. Not even for one kid.”

Cale went silent and absorbed the statement.

Hawthorn leaned forward and threw a few bills into the busker’s basket before turning them further down the path into the shopping center. He needed to move. Bottles of sake, ornate ceramic plates and bowls, anime figurines, graphic T-shirts with unintelligible English all passed as Cale and Hawthorn picked their way through the crowd.

“Had a counselor that worked with me in high school. Came up with a game plan. Stuck to it. Adapted it. I worked a few jobs, earned whatever cheap certifications I could get my hands on. Entry-level positions don’t need a degree; they need to show you are driven. Everything else you self-teach. College is just a system to package all that.”

Cale nodded, but by some miracle of the universe, remained quiet and simply listened.

“But no. Every step in the plan was meticulous. And I stuck to it. Then I found Vanessa alone in a coffee shop one evening. Doodling. She was beautiful, even with the tear stains.”

Hawthorn paused, giving Cale a moment to interject with a quip.

He didn’t.

This was why Hawthorn was excited to see Cale.

“Plan didn’t change. Just grew. I could do it all. Family, Vanessa, Business, Gym, keep up with Football. Easy. Just find the right routine. Make the right plan. Follow through. Iterate. Adapt. So yes. Vanessa was my first failure. Because I never gave her enough of that plan. And now I can’t. Can’t iterate on dead.”

The sentence felt like someone slid ten more pounds onto the bar Hawthorn had been holding for years.

“I think I’ve had a few of those. Things I’ve done or said that I can’t undo,” Cale noted. “I’ve spent so much of my life getting back on my feet I barely even notice the fall anymore.”

“Y’all’re sayin’ I need to fail more?”

Cale laughed, “Sort of. Yeah. Well, no. I don’t really know what I’m saying. Sometimes words just spill out. I think I’m just having a hard time picturing your perspective because it seems so foreign. My entire thing is ‘fail forward’. ‘No regrets.’”

“Naomi does the ‘no regrets’ thing too,” Hawthorn noted.

“Highly recommend it,” Cale said. Then, after a pause, “Just, remember to have healthy retrospectives.”

Healthy retrospectives.

Hawthorn snorted.

He definitely had the retrospectives covered. But how do you define actionable takeaways when there was no strategic direction?

They turned back towards the distant main street of Dotonburi. He hadn’t realized how deep they had made it, he could barely see the bridge past the sea of capitalism that filled the open-air mall.

"What do you think those two are talking about?" Cale asked.

Hawthorn grunted. "Girl talk."

**Asuna**

"Right, so a subsidiary would be a separate entity altogether,” Astrid said. “So it shields the parent company from liability and positions you better for independent scalability. You're eating more organizational complexity, though."

She popped a piece of grilled squid into her mouth and threw back her shot of sake. Hunkered in a side booth, Asuna and Astrid were serenaded by the glorious sounds of sizzling meats and shouted orders in the open kitchen.

Asuna refilled Asrid’s glass.

Asuna had made a friend.

"What if we were to start as a branch and evolve into a subsidiary once we reach an appropriate level of structure? Test the American market without fully committing substantial resources."

She might have also gone a *tiny* bit into the deep end.

The first few minutes were awkward. It seemed neither of them had any real interest in “small talk”. Then Asuna remembered that the blog had mentioned Astrid had a Master's in Corporate Law.

Asuna didn't really know how to do "friends", but she had been learning how to do "consultant" from Hawthorn. And the two ideas were pretty much the same.

Right?

Astrid stroked her lips in thought while looking to the ceiling. "If you just want to test the waters, find a trustworthy overseas partner and set up a joint venture. Depending on the product, you could start export-only. The foreign entity doesn't need local representation to export products."

Did this qualify as bad work-life boundaries?

Asuna was enjoying herself.
