**Astrid**

Astrid clung to the impossibly perfect man while he awkwardly attempted to get keys out of his left pocket with his right hand.

She could help him, or at least let go of his left arm, but it was funny to watch him struggle.

The perfectly slight smile he wore meant he knew what she was doing, and he rolled with it.

Because he was perfect.

It had been a perfect two weeks. Cale had worked remotely, which meant Astrid had spent the perfect amount of time in his presence: all the time.

Every barrage of kisses between meetings: perfect.

Every moment of banter while they returned his condo to a livable state: perfect.

Every night falling asleep in one another's arms: perfect.

Every date night, which was every night, was perfect.

It was all so. Damn. Perfect.

And that had scared her.

At what point did Cale go from the overopinionated loudmouth to this man who knew *exactly* what she wanted and how to deliver it?

Lola was right, it wasn't... a problem. But that didn't stop her from having a fight-or-flight reflex every time Cale found *just* the *right* way to call her beautiful.

Cale finally got the door open, and Astrid let go of his arm now that he no longer needed it. The subtle tease was not lost on him and earned her a brief glance back with that charming smirk, which filled her with joy.

Cale called them "micro-teases", and he loved them.

They entered the refurnished room. Astrid's plants sat interspersed between wooden bookshelves and leather furniture.

The idea of this warmly lit space being the hollow city of boxes from a few weeks earlier felt foreign.

It was already home.

A large dry bar and wine rack lined a wall, though no bottles sat in it. Cale had, at some point, decided he didn't like drinking at home. It sounded like pacing himself was a problem when silly things like "social convention" didn't exist to slow him down.

Astrid's eyes flickered to the hallway that led to the bedroom, then over to her perfect idiot.

A mixture of fear and anticipation caused her heart to thud.

She had unfinished business from that first night. And Lola had been the last push she needed.

Tonight, she would fully claim Cale.

Astrid went on a light offensive. "How long did you say you knew Lola?"

"I didn't say."

They moved past two large, packed suitcases to a deep love seat that had quickly turned into Astrid's favorite place in the condo.

Cale fell into it, leaving space for her.

Astrid remained standing with crossed arms, holding herself hostage until she got what she wanted.

"Cale."

He grinned. It didn't take long for him to cave.

"I knew Diego for four years. The day I turned twenty-one. Lola didn't really exist back then. Well, not like she does now."

Astrid thought back. "Twenty-one... Oh."

An image flashed in Astrid's mind of Cale's face dripping in tequila.

She couldn't remember what he had said, but she remembered the anger and sadness, both at him and herself. And she remembered storming off. Leo told her that Cale had, himself, left shortly after.

She caused him to abandon his own twenty-first birthday party.

Cale sighed. "Yeah... It was... probably my... lowest point. Six years of self-sabotage did not have me in a good mental state. I was... uh... broken."

She finally fell into the space next to Cale. "I cried myself to sleep that night. For what it's worth. Not that that was unusual back then."

There was a brief standoff to determine who would hold whom; they settled on Astrid being held, and she curled up into his slender, comforting arms.

Cale nodded. "Leo would tell me when you did."

"Traitor," Astrid said with affection.

Astrid felt his cheek rest against the back of her head as he continued. "It's funny, I visited Zoey a little bit before that time, you know what she said?"

"That you're a super spy?"

Astrid felt Cale's head shake gently. "Nah, that was before the spy thing. I was a mad scientist developing the world's first love potion back then."

"Why would she think that?"

"Because I told her it."

"You two are weird."

Astrid knew that Zoey needed the weird. Cale kept a tight mouth about it, but Astrid had picked up on the times when Cale would fly out to Pittsburgh with little notice. He once came back with a black eye, which he wore with pride but refused to acknowledge.

Cale continued, "But, no. She said our costumes matched too closely. That they clashed."

Astrid nodded and shifted in the seat, resting her legs over Cale's lap and off the side of the couch so she could watch Cale's face. This was definitely honing in on her concerns.

Seeing that he had her attention, he continued, "It had me thinking... And then... well, tequila to the face. I... think I called you a bitch again or something. I tried to make it funny, but... well..."

Astrid was given a very rare glimpse of regret with downcast eyes.

She helped him move past the moment. "And then Diego?"

"And then Diego." He nodded, regret replaced with a smile.

Cale had called Lola his "therapist" earlier.

Astrid tried to picture a broken Cale at a bar, reeking of tequila. She didn't like the image.

"It was at Diego's old bar. It was basically a hole-in-the-wall— sticky floors, broken taps, and all. Carmen had caught him putting on lipstick and took Lara to a hotel, threatening divorce and full custody. It was a right-place, right-time moment for both of us."

"And you... What, used your wit and charm to cheer him up and motivate him?"

Cale laughed. "Fuck no. Astrid, I had no fucking people skills, you know that. Those early days were just Diego and me venting. Eventually, he and I began to help build one another into who we are now. But *you* were the one who knew how to read people and figure out what they wanted or needed, not me."

This was the part that confused Astrid.

"Cale, the other day in the course of one elevator ride, you convinced someone to quit their job and pursue their love for writing."

"Yeah, that was fun." Cale laughed again. She could see pride behind those eyes.

"But it's like people are just a game to you now?"

Cale widened his smile, an unspoken "Yeah, and?".

"Where the hell did that come from?"

"Talking to people. Everywhere. Everyone. How else was I going to learn to talk to *you*?"

Astrid laughed. The assertion was absurd. "You're saying you spent years *training* for... this? For us? For me?"

"Of course."

Astrid's lips parted in shock, eyes desperately looking for any sign of deceit. Cale only lied by omission, though; he would not have said those words unless he truly believed them.

She needed to dig more. She knew this man had loved her, but investing so much effort in something that had felt so toxic felt crazy.

"How long?"

His renewed grin indicated she was asking the right questions; he was *proud* of this. "Four years. It was that same day. The one thing Diego and I came out with on that first night was an agreement that we needed to change. And change we did."

"You just... what, talked to people? Bar hopped and socialized?"

"And flirted. A *lot* of flirting. All catch and release, of course. Needed to learn what did and didn't work. I'm sorry to say you're not the only girl, or boy for that matter, who's thrown a drink in my face."

Astrid blinked a few times. "You are telling me you would get girls... and guys... interested in you, just to let them down?"

"I mean, when you put it that way... In hindsight... Yes?"

Astrid punched him in the leg, though she wore a smile.

"Idiot."

She should have been horrified, but the idea of this ex-video gamer grinding out charisma like a character skill was deeply amusing. The fact that he had done it for her was the real source of the smile, though.

"Of course, none of them were you. That was always the tricky part. Diego and Lola helped quite a bit with understanding you, but I needed to make sure to see you just often enough for-"

Cale cut himself off, lips sealing shut.

Just often enough?

He grimaced. "Can I get a mulligan on that sentence?"

Astrid shook her head. "Abso-fucking-lutely not. Cale, I got you monologuing about plans like a villain, and you just admitted you intentionally stopped seeing me to... what? That first month you disappeared, I thought you hated me."

"Astrid, we were hurting each other. And it was objectively clear that the more often we interacted, the worse we got."

"I... wait."

Cale was *very* intentional with his word choice; something about it stood out to Astrid.

It took her a moment, but she got it.

"Objectively? What, like you measured it?"

Cale shrugged. "Well, I have notes."

Astrid gawked. "You took *notes* on our interactions?"

"Yes. I can show you them; it's not like they have anything creepy written down."

Astrid laughed and shook her head. "I think their existence is the creepy part, Cale."

Even as she said it, she knew that statement wasn't how she felt. It was how she *should* feel, probably?

Cale's eyes turned down, and his smile faded.

Astrid interjected before he got too far. "No. Cale. I'm sorry, I didn't mean that. Lingering habit. Pushing away things that make me feel... well..."

"Loved?"

Cale's eyes turned back up to her.

"Yeah. That."

It was a *very* challenging word for her to say; she hadn't been quite able to form the sounds of it herself.

"Well, you are. I love you."

Cale, however, had no difficulties with the word. It seemed that he had sat with it longer than she had.

Astrid's entire body filled with warmth at the assertion. Her mouth opened, but her other three words came out: "You're an idiot."

It got a smile out of him, nowhere close to the breadth of the ones she had gotten at the park.

Why couldn't she say it?

She knew she felt it.

She supposed that if the answer to that were so clear, they wouldn't have been in this mess in the first place. Probably insecurities and vulnerabilities, self-sabotage... all those fun words therapists love.

She had just found out this man had spent four years working on himself for her, though.

What had she done?

Did she deserve his love?

He had comforted her that theirs had always been a two-person dance on that first night by the boxes, but she wasn't sure she felt that still. It had felt like he led, and she dragged her feet the whole way.

Four years.

"What were you waiting for, then? Four years is a long time."

Cale's mouth opened and closed several times; she apparently had asked the right question.

He finally answered, looking slightly past Astrid, "I think I was scared. I have all sorts of notes and rules listed out that state that I was waiting for *you* to end it. But I'm not so sure anymore. I'm generally good about handling failure. But... with you... It was almost like the idea of trying and failing, closing a door, was infinitely worse than walking away with the door still open."

"You and Zoey, such romantics. Going to go starve yourself to death next?"

This got a brief glance from Cale. "I want to be surprised that you know that reference, but I'm really not."

Astrid was pretty surprised that *he* knew the reference.

"What does that mean?"

Wuthering Heights was a great piece of literature. She read it often. It sat right next to A Farewell to Arms and Anna Karenina on her desk.

"Nothing." His eyes turned away. Astrid let it drop; it wasn't relevant. Cale was being dumb.

Astrid thought through his earlier statement, and she found her hand playing lightly on the back of his neck. His cheek nuzzled into her palm, spreading a faint smile to both of their faces.

She needed to ask the question out loud. It was the last, and probably the most important one, she had been circling it ever since Harmony.

"Cale, tell me honestly, why me?"

It got a raised eyebrow. "Originally, or now?"

"Originally."

Said with no hesitation, "No fucking clue."

It got a snort from Astrid. "You *are* a romantic to the core."

Cale winked, but continued, "Now though? Astrid, you care about me. You spent that entire roller coaster line attempting to calm me down because you didn't want me to soak in fear. You've built a whole language of lovingly crafted insults just for me. I have never once heard you insult anyone else."

"I have ten more ready for whenever you earn them, by the way." Astrid joked, using the words to push back a welling of tears in her throat.

Cale's eyes latched onto those words for a moment, and she could see cogs turning, but he quickly snapped himself back to the current discussion.

"You've always made me feel seen. As twisted as that sounds. Until Leo found me, I was literally no one. My parents never wanted me. The only other family I had was... well... Zoey. I had the social skills of a weasel. But you always took time for me, whether helping in school or insulting me, it didn't matter. You saw me. And you cared."

This felt backwards; he was the one who was always watching *her*. Had she undervalued what she had viewed as minor moments of sympathy?

Astrid was struggling really hard with those tears. "I think you have our lines mixed up."

Cale shrugged. "I don't know what to tell you."

The idiot turned his head and kissed her hand.

The feeling distracted Astrid long enough for several tears to escape, dripping down over smiling lips. Cale's eyes trailed them down her face, but he didn't move to wipe them away. She was grateful; she liked the feeling they left behind.

It was time.

Astrid shifted on the seat, bringing herself up to straddle his legs, and lowered her lips to his for a kiss. She felt his arms rise behind her, holding her firmly. She pressed the kiss further with a very soft moan to make it clear there was more to this.

When she pulled her face back, she found that smirk.

"Take that off," she said, shaking her head.

His shirt was off in a moment, and she laughed.

"You know what I meant."

Astrid watched as the smirk faded, leaving behind a significantly more subtle smile. She was going to need to make a rule about "no armor for real emotional moments".

It hadn't been the first time she'd seen him topless.

Or naked, for that matter. It took him a few days to get used to the whole "there's a girl living with me" thing, and apparently, he liked showering with the door open.

Of course, it wouldn't be the first time he would see her naked, either.

Why *wouldn't* she join him in those showers? It was a simple matter of water efficiency.

Plus, the look on his face the first time she entered, and the rather loud "holy shit", had very much been worth it. It was rare that she got to throw Cale off his game so dramatically.

But, despite the number of times that Cale found himself dirty enough to need to shower, which was up to twice a day, skin-to-skin embraces had really been the most intimate they had ever been.

But Astrid was ready; it was time for her to give him all of her.

"Cale..." She started, grabbing his eyes.

His head tilted ever so slightly, picking up that there was more to this. He had to have picked up the implications; he was clever.

She procrastinated for a moment. "I really want to thank you. These last weeks have been truly amazing. I still don't think I deserve it, but you make me at least feel like I do."

His smile grew. It wasn't armor, it was authentic. It was what Astrid needed.

She could do this.

She found more words to push off what she knew had to come next. Part of her was still scared; it would be a significant moment.

"I only hope that I'm able to give you even a tenth of the affection and attention you provide me. Because..."

Those damn tears caught in her throat.

"Oh, come on," Astrid said out loud to herself.

Cale's lips parted in anticipation.

"Because..."

It's just three words.

What was wrong with her?

Her eyes focused on his, which glistened. He had his own tears coming.

She said it.

"Because I love you."

Astrid's breath hitched in her throat. Her heart *pounded* as heat flooded into her skin like a fever.

Cale's eyes went wide, and his mouth appeared to form some word that never escaped.

Astrid didn't look away.

She didn't use a kiss to dampen the impact of the moment.

She didn't close her eyes.

She didn't stop the tears.

She stared deep into this perfect man's perfectly watering eyes.

And she *said it again*.

"I love you."

It had been years. At least three of them — she suspected it had been ten — of holding this release back.

And it was a *rush*.

The words rang through her whole body, filling her with the emotional climax she had denied herself time and time again.

Astrid breathed in and gave a trembling exhale.

"Astrid..." Cale's voice broke, and his chest stuttered on a breath.

She found *every* excuse to use the new, most passionate word of her entire life.

"I don't know how long I've loved you. I don't really care. All that matters is that I love you now. And I'm going to keep loving you."

Cale's smile grew, his mouth still hung open, tears dripping freely off his lips. Astrid's tongue ached to taste them. To drink in and savor his own emotional breaking point.

But she held back.

"You're going to *regret* how much I love you. This is not a love you can control."

Every nerve in her body lit, burning to feel his skin against hers.

She placed her hands down on his thighs to support herself as she leaned forward, grabbing for more words.

"Bards are going to make a comeback just to sing tales of how much I *strangled* you with my love."

Cale found words, even if simple ones, and gave a tear-broken laugh. "Fuck, Astrid."

His own hands made it up under her shirt and onto her waist. Her skin bristled at the touch, sending a new jolt of electricity that branched through her body.

"You're going to *wish* you had died next to my ghost with how inescapable my love will be."

The initial eruption of exhilaration had begun to subside, but she craved more.

Astrid reached for more words.

"You are *my* fucking idiot, and... and..."

The words were losing their efficacy; it was time to find a different way for her to show her deep, infinite passion for this perfect man.

She said it one last time.

"And I love you."

Astrid had given him all of her, and she knew that she had all of him.

She craved more.

Her entire body was a conduit for emotional dialogue, and she was ready to use every inch of it to further cement her claim.
