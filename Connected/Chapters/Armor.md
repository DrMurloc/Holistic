**Astrid**

Astrid sat on the metro, head leaning against the window, eyes focused on nothing in particular. An air of wistfulness hung like a fog. The city flashed by as a confusing mess of colors and shapes.

Her phone dangled in her hand, displaying the message from Leo: *"Amusement Park, it's urgent."*

Leo was not, by any definition, "dumb", but his cleverness had always been a tad lacking, especially in the area of subtlety.

There was no doubt in Astrid's mind that at the top of the metro escalator, it wasn't only Leo who would be waiting for her.

The thought filled her with... emotions.

Yes.

Those were most certainly emotions.

Astrid was not particularly prone to the plague of "feelings". When it came to Cale, however... well, he had always been particularly contagious to her.

She turned her gaze to her phone and found her own vacant eyes staring back.

With several taps, the phone opened to her photo gallery, obscuring the reflection. She scrolled through a series of selfies.

Each and every one had those curls in the background. It was not lost on Astrid that a significant number of the pictures also had the brown eyes focused on her, behind charming, round glasses.

It was one of the greatest thrills in life: catching Cale watching.

It wasn't just the attention; it was like he would *intentionally* get caught, like he *knew* it was the safest way to let her know he could *see* her past... well, whatever was wrong with her.

Her eyes fell to the timestamps on the photos, enveloping her in a haze of sorrow. It was not uncommon to go even a month without being privileged by that raven hair that she ached to run her hands through...

The sorrow was forcefully shoved aside by the image of her lightly grabbing those curls and pulling him into a kiss.

The image *almost* brought a smile to her otherwise expressionless lips.

She had been so close so many times, as if she got some sick pleasure out of keeping herself on the precipice of happiness, but never taking the plunge.

Emotional edging.

*What is wrong with me?*

The thought was a familiar one.

She scrolled down to a series of interval shots, playing out a scene where Cale got a *micrometer* too close and earned a punch in the shoulder. His face bore a broad grin; maybe she wasn't the only masochist.

Why was it that she could get close to him, but he wasn't allowed to get close to her? Who started that rule?

It was like they were stuck in a game, one that she suspected they had built for themselves over ten years. And, well, she wasn't good at games, and Cale was.

Was she losing or winning?

The more distant he grew, the more she felt like the answer was "losing".

The metro jostled beneath her, shaking her out of the line of thought.

She scrolled further back, and the pictures evolved from over-the-shoulder shots to photos of family trips from high school. A small laugh, barely a snort, managed to escape as she landed on a picture from the zoo, taken by her brother.

She and Cale were red-faced in the image, pointing at a snake behind a glass. She couldn't remember who argued it was a python and who claimed it was a cobra, but the photo clearly showed the sign read, in large print, "mamba".

She was at least glad they were out of those early years in high school, where the fights were with sharp swords and not wooden ones.

The fact that all three of them, Leo, Astrid, and Cale, had remained close friends through *all* of that was some twisted mix of miracle, raw stubbornness, and the unrelenting efforts of Leo to mediate.

She sighed, her breath carrying away the addictive dregs of sentiments she couldn't quite name.

Her eyes turned up as the city buildings began to fade and a roller-coaster came into view - a dive coaster, "The Breaking Point". It was one of her favorites. The stop right before the first drop had such a stunning view, and the suspense it built for the descent was deeply gratifying.

The faintest wink of a smile played at the corner of her mouth.

She loved the park.

Regardless of how things went, she would make sure to enjoy herself.

A day with Cale, though...

Not merely a cameo at game night, but a full day... It had likely been a year since they had an extended interaction beyond sitting slightly too close to each other on the couch, all the while May and Leo pretended to not notice their overlapped pinkies.

She was partly horrified at the unknowns. However, with Leo there to mediate, things shouldn't get *too* bad.

Who knew? Maybe they could get along. They were adults, right?

The thought caused an audible laugh to escape her lips, causing several other metro riders to turn to study her in concern.

Astrid and Cale: *not* fighting.

Like that'd ever fucking happen.

**Cale**

Cale leaned back on his foot, doing *absolutely* nothing to stop the broad grin that spread across his face as blonde ponytail and blue ribbon came into view.

There she was.

The most captivating woman that had, did, and would ever exist.

Every step: graceful and precise.

Poised and clever beyond comprehension.

Icy, blue eyes sat over a refined tongue that carried a love language of custom-crafted insults reserved for Cale, and Cale alone.

And the ribbon.

The blue ribbon that Cale knew was there as a symbol of affection, *specifically* for him.

It had been one of the first signs of good faith he had ever earned from her. They both knew what it meant, though it was taboo to mention it: “I’m interested. Keep looking.”

She sported high-waisted jean shorts and a long-sleeved white blouse, perfectly practical for a warm late April day.

Behind him, the air vibrated with the occasional clatter of a wooden coaster or the release of gas from the nearby power tower, accompanied by screams that varied from fear to joy. The lightly salted air of the Los Angeles coast seemed an appropriate accompaniment to the park.

“You sure you’ll be OK, bro?" Leo placed a hand on Cale’s shoulder.

Cale shook his head, betraying none of his inner concern. “Pfft. Yeah. What’s the worst that could happen? Punch to the arm?”

"That's... the least of my concerns."

"We've been doing this for ten years. What's one more round?"

"But... Alone?"

Cale didn't respond.

He hadn't *technically* lied. "What's one more round?" had been the mantra that got him out of the condo this morning.

But...

Cale's phone burned against his leg as Astrid neared, his pants practically sagging with the weight of decision.

*Today,* it would end.

One way or another.

Astrid snatched the two tickets out of Leo’s hands as she passed by, very intentionally not meeting Cale’s following gaze, though she knew he was watching.

Cale heard her feet come to a stop a few steps behind him.

His smirk was locked in place as he watched her over his shoulder. He pictured the spinning wheels on her face as she realized there were, in fact, two, not three, tickets.

Leo mocked out their unspoken pleasantries, “‘Oh, thank you, Leo’, ‘Oh, absolutely, Astrid, I know you love the park so much’, ‘Anything for my sister and best friend’.”

He sighed and began to walk back towards the escalators.

Cale snickered. “Thank you, Leo.”

Leo's slow headshake was not missed.

Astrid called out, with a smidgen of concern, “Leo?”

"Good luck. Love you, Astrid!" Leo raised a hand and kept walking away.

"*Leo!*" Astrid yelled with command this time, but got no response.

"Love you, too, Leo!" Cale called back in a poor Astrid impression. Leo's head kept shaking, his gaze fixated on the ground.

Cale finally turned to face Astrid and placed his hands in his pockets.

She studied the tickets as if they carried philosophical answers to the universe, deliberately avoiding Cale's gaze.

Her armor wasn’t up yet, but Cale rarely took off his own anymore.

While hers was grown, a layer of icy plate, his was *crafted* and full of leathery padding*.*

A myriad custom-made smirks and grins, a casual gaze, hands in pockets, a slight lean, all built to say “come on, Astrid, hit me with your best, you can't break what's already broken”.

Astrid's lips parted, and a light breeze rustled both tickets and ponytail.

Was that apprehension? Concern? Anticipation?

Cale's heart raced, though he let no indication of such slip.

He had endless cards ready to play, but they were all reactive. Astrid would set the pace; he had her on her turf by design.

Cale's veins thrummed with unbound energy as Astrid's eyes darted up to meet his.

Was... was that a smile? It was so faint...

It was gone as fast as it appeared.

And the armor flashed into place.

The narrowed eyes, the crossed arms, squared shoulders, the tapping foot that screamed “why do I put up with you”, and a very clenched jaw, all acted as her greaves, breastplate, gauntlets, and visor.

He missed the pout.

He had made the mistake of poking *too* much fun at the pout, and it had gone. An error that had not been repeated. The cycle of analyze and iterate was prone to opportunity cost, and he never looked back.

No regrets.

To no surprise, she turned on her heel and began her purposeful progression towards the gate without saying a word.

Cale had let himself hope for a moment, but... it was clear that Astrid wanted nothing to do with him one-on-one.

Cale shook his head, taking a deep breath, his smirk fading, turning away to save himself from the image of defeat.

It was an anticipated defeat, but it hurt nonetheless.

The phone was out in an instant, and he began writing the first of two deterministic messages.

At least it was finally-

“Are you going to stand around playing your dumb gacha games all day, or are you going to come and get this over with?” Astrid's voice cut across the tense air like a longsword, trickling with the venom of authority.

Cale froze, finger hovering over the send button, feeling the tip of her blade against the back of his throat.

He raised his eyes and turned, surprised to hear the Astrid equivalent of “I miss you, please come hang out with me”.

Her hands were on her hips now, foot continuing to tap. A rare posture, it was the closest to “vulnerable and open” that she would ever intentionally show Cale.

The phone returned to the pocket, the message left as a draft, and Cale lowered his handcrafted visor as the wide smirk returned.

The game resumed as Cale moved to catch up with her.

"Don’t knock ’em ’til you try ’em. I’ll have you know these overpriced simp-magnets can be quite fulfilling.” Cale, of course, did not play gacha games: he did not gamble.

Astrid’s eyes rolled, her arms returning to a crossed position, “That smile... Please. Don’t tell me you think this is a *date*.”

“Wouldn’t dream of it” —a big fat lie. Dates were *far* from the only dreams he had about her. “I had merely thought you were leaving me stranded without a ticket.”

“I would never,” she snapped.

They fell in step with one another, with their standard spacing of “could smell each other’s shampoo”. Astrid used lavender today, Cale’s favorite. He was sure that she picked up on the peach he switched to.

“Astrid, you literally once told me to meet you and my friends in the monkey house at the zoo. Just for me to realize my human friends weren’t actually at the zoo that day.” Cale grinned.

It had been clever, even if it took Cale a few weeks to get the joke.

He caught the faintest glimmer that might be describable as a smile on her face.

“To be fair, when Leo and I eventually showed up, you seemed to be enjoying yourself, making faces with your primate relatives.” She glanced over to catch his own widened grin at the insult.

She continued, refusing to acknowledge the grin as always, “Regardless, Leo spent a lot of money on two full-day fast passes. It would be rude to let them go to waste.”

Good, Leo didn’t let that one slip. It had, in fact, been Cale who bought them.

The bag check and ticket process was quick, and their tickets were exchanged for purple arm bands that gave off a sense of exclusivity. Of course, FastPass wouldn’t mean no lines, but it would realistically let them expect more than two rides in one day, given the relatively late start they had.

Astrid scanned the park, needing to only slightly crane her neck over the ever-shifting crowds, before turning her head down to an unfolded map.

Cale took a spot fairly close behind her, looking over her shoulder. He subtly braced for impact, expecting a punch that never came.

Her eyes flickered up briefly as he encroached on her personal space, but she didn’t otherwise object.

Her scent was intoxicating this close; a considerable part of his mind screamed at him to wrap his arms around her and kiss her slender, exposed neck. Frankly, he was partially convinced she would accept it. But it would be a gamble, one he wasn’t willing to take when they still had all day.

Her armor was still up.

He ripped his mind away from the warmth that emanated from her cheek and studied his cards.

“What’s the agenda?” he asked, lowering his voice. He noticed the way her ear twitched upon realizing *just* how close he was, but she didn’t look back. Her hands tightened on the map as if the thin piece of paper could stabilize her.

“We need at least five to make these passes worth their value by my calculations,” she speculated, then paused, waiting for him to call her out.

*How could you possibly quantify such a thing?*

“Makes sense. Do you have a priority list?”

Her eyes narrowed, then turned back to the map, “How would you prioritize rides at an amusement park? What *possible* metrics exist to quantify that?”

She was definitely fishing; these were experimental feints.

*The same metrics you used to determine fast pass value.*

Cale needed to show good faith. The idea wasn’t to eliminate the fights, as they both enjoyed them; it was to demonstrate his willingness to change for her and that *they* could evolve.

He settled on a simple, “Wise as ever.”

Astrid stepped forward and turned to face him. The map crumpled up as she crossed her arms. Her eyes seemed to scan him for alien life, any explanation at this departure from the standard.

Cale proudly held his confident posture and grinned.

The two of them acted as rocks lodged in the stream of people that passed.

Astrid met his eyes briefly and responded with the three words he had spent all morning daydreaming about.

The three most meaningful words of his entire life.

Words that had, on multiple occasions, been the sole reason Cale even got out of bed with the hopes of hearing.

They carried a confession that transcended logical rationalization.

“You’re an idiot."

They sliced through the air, aimed directly at his heart.

And he did nothing to deflect or dodge. He took the affectionate knife straight through his chest and broadened his grin even further.

He didn’t quite feel he had earned them this time, but Astrid seemed confused as to what he was doing.

She shook her head with a groan, turned while tucking the folded map away in a pocket, and began her determined gait further into the park.

She carved through the crowds with an intentional efficiency while he, merely steps behind her, found himself nearly barreling into every person they passed.

His eyes struggled to pull away from the rhythmic bounce and sway of her ponytail; it was mesmerizing.

“So what’s the first stop?” he asked, barely keeping up.

“I will *not* have you throwing up on me today, so it’s only logical that we warm up on a staple.”

“A carousel?” he offered.

“Of course it is the carousel, *idiot,*” she exclaimed. He could feel the eye roll through her voice.

A glimpse back over her shoulder betrayed her expectation for a retort. Cale had to fight it back.

*Do you expect me to read your mind*?

He simply smiled, causing her eyes to further narrow before turning forward.

He let the moment fade and hurried to find a pace next to her, playing a different card, “How have you been doing? It’s been... what, a month?”

Twenty-four days, fourteen hours, though he didn’t know the minutes. He wasn’t *that* obsessed.

“I…” The word came out sharp, but the sentence softened as she continued, and she slowed her pace, “I’ve been good. Thank you. Well, as good as one could be being stuck in a Starbucks with a Master’s in Corporate Law.”

“I’m glad to hear that. You seemed tense the last time I saw you.”

His mind flashed to touching pinkies. Strangely, one of the most significant victories he had ever had in life.

*That’s a depressing thought.*

“It didn’t help that *you* lack a basic understanding of personal space.” Astrid pointedly drifted an inch closer to him, causing their shoulders to occasionally brush.

She waited for the counterargument.

Cale gave none.

When she was left unretorted, both her eyes and arms softened.

Would *she* tell him about her new job? Leo had mentioned it as a potential icebreaker, but hadn’t clarified details.

Astrid seemed to decide against it, “It’s been quiet. Leo is always out with May at the arcade nowadays, well, when they aren’t on a... break. We still have the regular movie and game nights that you seem to never find time for. The rest of my friends are off traveling, having babies, and generally being interesting.” She glanced at Cale, “Well, with the exception of one.”

Cale grinned, “Aw, I’m a friend! When did I get a promotion?”

“It’s unfortunately a title that really puts the ’a friend of a friend’ phrase to the test, or ’a friend of a brother’ in this case. Regardless, it’s probationary at best.”

Cale chuckled. Yes, they definitely needed the back-and-forth. It was part of what made them, well, them. But he just needed to show that it could be different.

The fast-pass line for the carousel was empty, so they only had a minute-long wait.

Astrid tapped her foot as if every moment were unbearable.

“Which ones are we going for?” Cale asked, pretending to scan the available horses. He knew the answer: a white porcelain horse with blue ribbons stood out as if it had been put there for her.

“Don’t be an idiot,” she said.

“I’ll do my best, but my track record has a pretty solid streak going,” Cale noted.

A brief, sharp laugh escaped her mouth, but she managed to catch it. Cale’s heart filled with joy.

*Those*, the laughs, *they* were precious and hard-earned.

Her posture was loosening, her guard wasn’t down by *any* means, but he could likely be bolder.

Could he flirt?

Flirting had cost him their pinky time, but the punch in the shoulder and the subtle smile she had given him across the room when Leo and May weren’t looking had been a fair trade.

Besides, sometimes life called for boldness.

As much as he didn’t like risk, complacency led to stagnation.

The gate opened and Astrid charged forward, staking her claim on the target horse. Cale sprinted ahead of her and offered a hand to help her up; it was ignored, as expected.

“It’s fitting.” His gaze remained on her as he slowly circled his way to the black and red stallion next to her.

“Are you saying I look like a horse?” she growled, hands tightening on the gold bar and eyes narrowing yet again.

“I’m simply saying you look pretty today. I see you’re wearing the ribbon. It still looks good.” Cale pulled himself up and studied her response.

Was that *too* bold a play? He wanted the armor to go *down*, not return. He couldn’t make her feel emotionally pressured.

“Well, if…” She started and cut herself off.

Her eyes darted, seeming still unsure of how to react to compliments. That had clearly not been the response she had expected. Direct flirting was *very* new; neither of them had many pre-built reactions or responses for it.

The ride began, their horses rising and falling in alternating sequences.

The world beyond blurred as they sped up, wrapping them in a chaos of color and noise that wove a welcome moment of privacy.

“Thank you... I suppose. I can’t say I expected you to be so… gentlemanly?” It was said just loud enough for him to hear. The last word sounded like she was speaking a different language.

This time, it was Cale's turn to be shocked.

*Thank you?* And no backhanded comment to deflect?

That felt... weird.

Was this how she had been feeling so far with *him* acting cordial? He clearly had underestimated the capability for progress today; this was what he expected the *end* of the day would see.

She remained facing forward, and he watched her as she circled beside him. Her eyes had attempted to narrow, but ended up stuck in a look of squinting confusion.

The armor was slowly chipping away.

As if she read his mind, she added, “Of course, you could have put more effort into it, simply ‘pretty’? I'm aware that monkeys struggle with even elementary English, but surely your vocabulary has become more robust.”

Her cheeks took on a hint of a rose color.

What?

She was inviting him to further his advance.

Cale felt his stomach flutter; his heart attempted to race past their steeds.

This... this was all definitely *very* new.

He accepted the challenge, “I could have gone with ‘pristinely porcelain and angelic’, but I left my fedora at home, so sadly have nothing to tip, m’lady.”

It got a snort, and she managed to line the punch *perfectly*, despite the moving target. Her head turned away to hide any further blush.

He took his win; it was massive.

He would need many more to satisfy his goal. There weren’t really concrete key results defined; he sort of assumed he would just... know whether he passed or failed by the 7 PM cutoff.

The possibility of being left with an unclear result filled him with dread.

Letting the universe flip a coin for you backfires when the coin lands on its edge. As much as he hated to view it as such, today *was* a coin flip, even if a somewhat rigged one.

The ride began to slow, and the rest of the world became more concrete, reminding Cale and Astrid that they were, in fact, at the center of a crowded park.

*She definitely won’t open up around others. I need to get her alone.*

He slid off his horse right before the ride stopped and extended a hand to help her down.

Her horse was at the peak of its rotation.

She studied his hand for a moment, then her eyes narrowed as they met his own.

“You seem intent on making this a date.” There was no immediate movement to get down, with or without help.

Cale grinned, “I have no clue what you are referring to, I’m just trying to get you off that high horse of yours.”

The pun hung like a live grenade while he maintained his smirk.

He set it off with a wink.

It exploded in a bout of full-blown, unrestrained laughter from Astrid.

Cale closed his eyes and inhaled, absorbing Astrid’s joy. It was a more potent energy source than the sun; he could power cities with it.

The laugh faded, and as his eyes opened, he found the most charming and delicate of lingering smiles.

The helmet was off.

Astrid accepted his hand and swung her leg up and over the horse, “That was clever. Monkeys with typewriters and Shakespeare, I suppose.”

The insult was said with much more rounded edges. It appeared that Astrid had exchanged her wooden sword for a foam one.

Cale reveled in the softness and delicateness of her fingers. The urge to further explore the textures of her hand with his thumb was overwhelming.

*Not yet, wait and see.*

Once she was down and they were off the ride, Astrid studied their held hands.

She finally removed hers, seeming reluctant, her eyes making a brief stop on Cale’s before turning back to the park.

*Close, so close.*

“The priorities have changed.” She returned to her brisk pace along the crowded path. With a scurrying of feet, he managed to catch up a few steps behind her.

“Great. I thought there wasn’t a priority list?”

Cale dodged around a kid chasing a loose ball and immediately needed to duck between two giggling girls.

How was she so efficient at simply moving? He was right behind her; was she trailing him into people on purpose for her own amusement? He carried deep respect for the move, if that was the case.

“I said that there were no metrics to drive a priority list; I did not say I had no priorities. You, yourself, seem to have your own… priorities.”

She addressed his “priorities”? That was the closest she had *ever* come, in ten years, to acknowledging their “not-relationship”.

“And what is *your* new priority?” Cale questioned.

She didn’t respond, but he could see the Ferris wheel looming ahead. It appeared their goals had at least somewhat aligned: private space was the play.

The fast-pass line for the Ferris wheel was about ten minutes, according to the sign. The queue left them too crowded for him to make any real progress, so he figured it was best to focus on casual discussion again.

He played his free move card. “I heard you finally scored a career job.”

Astrid smiled, “Paralegal. I’m…” she paused, studying him briefly, as if calculating how much she wanted to say, “I’m excited and nervous. It’s a well-known large firm; there will be a lot of competition for promotions.”

Cale nodded, legitimately excited for the revelation.

“That’s amazing! I’ve found that the big companies may be the slower burn on early growth, but the truly ambitious have a much higher ceiling. I’m sure, given that context, you’ll do great.”

After the words left his mouth, Cale winced internally. She was too clever not to have caught that accidental slip.

Astrid furrowed her brow and cocked her head, “Cale, that was… actually insightful.”

Cale found Astrid’s eyes scanning him yet again to verify that he was, in fact, the same person. He wore his usual jeans and a playful but well-designed t-shirt, as always; he wasn’t sure she would pick up on the designer glasses or shoes.

Cale contemplated briefly but determined the insight was valuable to her. “Well, we get a lot of interviews from people at big companies looking to advance their careers by hopping around. It’s difficult to grow within a single company unless you’re in the top five percent or so. Granted, software is likely very different from Legal, so who knows.”

Cale looked up in thought. What *would* the nuance of the Legal industry be? It was much more established —one of the oldest professions in the book —and likely had much better-defined career paths.

Astrid continued to study him from head to toe, “I’m sorry, I... I don’t believe I ever caught what *you* do? I thought you were a junior engineer.”

They stepped forward with the line, about halfway through.

Cale looked away, hiding a raised visor as his smile faded, contemplating how much he wanted to reveal, “There’s a pretty good reason why neither you nor Leo knows what I do.”

“And what is that?”

Was he actually going to break his five years of hiding his success?

Would it help him here?

His cousin, Zoey, was the only one who knew, although she was convinced he was secretly a spy. His parents might know, but he had never gotten confirmation from either that they even received the messages.

The visor snapped back on.

“It’s because I never told you.”

Astrid gave him a flat look and punched him in the shoulder. “You’re an idiot.”

“This has been established. And be careful with the aggravated battery, Miss Legal.”

Eyes rolled, “Please. No weapons or serious injuries. Simple assault at worst. Unless you consider the damage to your ego as an injury.”

Another shuffle forward, and the two stepped aside while a crying toddler was escorted out of the queue.

For a full minute, they returned to silence.

Cale could see the cogs whirring in her head as she tried to piece together a side of him that she hadn’t known. He simply maintained his smirk.

“You’re really not going to tell me?” She finally caved to the mystery, with more than a touch of exasperation in her voice.

Cale's gaze trailed the outline of a distant coaster, watching a car circle through a loop.

Would it change things? Probably… But today was *all* in, not simply “in until you become mildly uncomfortable”.

His smirk flickered away briefly as the title left his mouth, “Director of Engineering.”

She blinked in shock, and the ponytail dipped with a tilted head.

“Your ten-person engineering team... You run that now?” It was said with respect.

Another new experience.

Respect, from Astrid.

Crazy. It was never something Cale had yearned for, but having it didn’t suck.

Cale looked away and placed a hand on the back of his neck, “You know how I said it’s difficult to grow in a large company? You know what the real power move is? When you ride the waves of a small start-up that becomes big.”

Her jaw dropped, and he took a mental snapshot of the moment.

It was perfect; he had never seen her so flabbergasted. The admission may have been worth it, just for that look.

“How many... how many now?” She asked, seeming afraid of the answer.

Cale's head bobbed back and forth with averted eyes, nails scratching the back of his neck, “If you count near-shore contractors, a branch office in Tokyo, and a subsidiary that I keep slightly involved in with an office in Paris… It’s about a hundred fifty in the department. Eight, nine hundred in the company?”

“But… you’re an idiot.” She was frantically re-evaluating him.

He tried to smile, “Once again, this has been established. Turns out I’m the right kind of idiot to subject myself to the people-problems that arise with growing pains in a company."

This was a well-practiced line he used when his rapid growth was discussed at work. The truth was, he still didn't quite understand his success, but they kept paying him more, so who was he to complain?

She studied him top to bottom yet again, confounded that her sharp and scrupulous gaze had missed something so significant.

There it was, what Cale had been explicitly avoiding.

She was analyzing a shift in power dynamics.

Cale sighed as they stepped forward, his smile fading completely, “Astrid, I didn’t tell any of you for a reason. Zoey is literally the only one who knows. I’m a very different person at work. I like keeping who I am there and who I am out here separate. Please don’t act differently with me because of this.”

She nodded slowly, “Noted, although I am more trying to figure out what other secrets you may be hiding. I underestimated your capability to not run that loud mouth.”

He smiled softly to mask another inner wince; his phone weighed his pocket down like a lead brick.

Cale looked ahead as they reached the front of the line, studying the upcoming playing field.

To help with pacing during peak hours, the attendants paired couples into the Ferris Wheel cars.

A rare frown escaped Cale’s control.

He climbed into the next car and turned to help Astrid. She accepted the hand, lifting herself in.

The couple behind them began to approach, causing Cale to exhale a sigh of resignation.

*SLAM.*

Cale jumped as he found the car's door held shut.

The girl in the couple began to protest.

Now, Cale was used to the formidable, icy, and sharp glare of Astrid. The two of them had been in an arms race of sorts their entire life. Her gaze would sharpen, and his armor would thicken.

The result of a decade of such endless iteration was a death stare so intense and unflinchingly lethal that Cale was surprised Astrid was allowed to board planes.

The poor girl on the receiving end *actually* stumbled back a few steps, and her date did nothing to protect her.

Cale and Astrid were left alone.

Cale found himself laughing, shifting into a confident sprawl on his side of the car. “You can kill with those eyes, Astrid. Be careful, the less experienced may be too weak for your overwhelming aura.”

He received no response, though, as she settled into the opposite corner.

Astrid's face softened as she folded her hands onto her lap, looking out the side as their vessel slowly began to inch upwards.

The cart swayed in a light breeze, creating a sense of instability.

They were dangling in the air, suspended only by a dozen-year-old stretch of infrastructure. Cale tried desperately not to think about that.

He instead pictured himself shifting to her side of the car and placing an arm around her, leaning his head against hers, and letting himself get drunk on her scent.

He was *so close*.

Cale settled on simply drinking in her image.

That slightest of smiles was the smallest but most rewarding window into the deep wells of emotion he knew she had.

And she was letting him see it.

The realization froze Cale, fearful that any movement might prompt her to retreat.

Her arms weren’t tense; her shoulders were soft, her sharp eyes almost seemed... blunt. If it wasn't for the very corners of her lips being turned up, he would describe the look as... hollow.

And it was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

This wasn't merely removed armor; for all intents and purposes, this was Astrid baring herself naked to him.

The ponytail swayed just perfectly as a light breeze rolled through.

For the third time today, Cale had to control his breathing, though he was sure the thudding of his heart would be a greater tell.

The park grew distant beneath them.

The city skyline sketched a structured elegance, sprawling out in one direction, and the ocean extended in another with a serene, organic beauty. Two starkly different landscapes that converged and married at this one park.

“It’s a beautiful view,” she said softly as they reached the top.

Cale's mind took a moment to catch up with the words.

What, she was just going to *give* him this one? A token of good faith?

He gave a light, airy laugh. “You’re setting me up. You can’t make it that easy.”

“Say the damn cheesy line, Cale.” Astrid growled, a direct contrast to the smile she still wore.

He took yet another moment to appreciate the picturesque perfection that lay before him.

As he watched, Astrid shifted in the seat, chin lightly cradled between a finger and a thumb, elbow resting on the side of the car. Her other hand fell lightly on the seat next to her.

She was giving him a better profile to admire.

*Baring herself, indeed.*

The curved line of her neck, in particular, was exposed in this position. Cale let the image play in his mind of her parted lips and closed eyes in reaction to a warm breath and soft kiss planted lightly on her nape.

This breath he could not control, and it escaped in jagged bursts.

Astrid's ear twitched at the noise. She did not turn, though a touch of rose splashed across her cheeks, contributing to the stunning image.

He finally broke the silence, satisfying her demand. “The view is, in fact, beautiful.”

The blush expanded, and the smile widened ever so slightly.

Neither dared break the moment; it was too immaculate.

A moment of serenity as a stark contrast to years of cacophonous fighting.

They made several more rotations.

When the cart inevitably came to a stop, Cale let himself out first and offered a hand up to the still rose-cheeked Astrid. Her composure slowly returned as they emerged from their pocket of privacy.

The grace, the poise, the steady eyes all reformed, but with none of the tension.

It was not quite armor; it was more like she donned her clothes after a one-night stand.

The smile receded, but she accepted his hand. This time, she did not let go as they ventured back into the park.

Was this it?

Were they dating now?

Was that enough?

He pushed the thought out; it was a decision for later still. Right now, he had momentum to keep.

He broke the extended silence, looking to start the next round, “Where to-”

"This still isn’t a date,” Astrid interjected.

Cale tilted his head and eyed her as he followed her towards... somewhere.

How far would she be able to keep this up?

He had to push back the image of her riding him while saying, “Don’t think this means anything, idiot”.

Honestly? Knowing her, it was not out of the question.

Physical distance had technically never been what triggered her, only the emotional distance that was difficult to decouple.

He bit, though.

He played this game for ten years; he could give it at least one more round.

“Of course not. That would be unheard of, Astrid and Cale on a *date*.”

He squeezed her hand, an implied “then what is this?”.

She gave her hand a look as if it had gone rogue.

“I don’t… I don’t know *what* this is.” She admitted.

Cale thought for a moment, “Does it need to be something?”

“Categorically, yes.” She stated matter-of-factly.

Oh, *there* was a good one. It had been going on for seven years, and he found *every* excuse to bring it up. Would Astrid engage?

He went for it, “What, like how categorically a bowl of cereal has to be a soup?”

He was *pretty* sure the people in the car at the top of the Ferris wheel several minutes back could hear the groan.

“You are really going to… *That* argument, aga-... You are so childish.”

She tried to ignore it, but ultimately stopped walking and turned to him, her hand still grasping his, “A soup is defined simply as a liquid base containing pieces of solid foods.”

Cale filled with glee; this was his favorite, “So, a cream of mushroom soup is not a soup as it has no solid foods?”

“The creamed mushroom *is* the solid food.”

Any semblance of a smile was gone. Astrid’s face screamed, “Really? Now? After what we just did?” This was, after all, to her, the emotional equivalent of making fart noises moments after sex.

And Cale found that hilarious.

Cale continued through the standard ebb and flow of the argument. “I’m pretty sure most definitions require soup to have a water or stock base.”

Her arms shot down like steel pillars, causing Cale to stumble slightly as her tight grip yanked his hand forward. “Those definitions are wrong!”

Cale raised an eyebrow, “The cube rule states…”

“The cube rule is *satire*; I do *not* accept its principles.” She stomped her foot with a challenging look.

Cale grinned and leaned into some improv, “Tell me, if I asked you to get me the soup, and a bowl of cereal was sitting next to you, would you immediately get the cereal?”

Astrid scoffed, “I would be much more likely to get it than if you said ‘get me that wrench’.”

Huh.

A very, very slight concession.

Cale nodded, thoughtfully, “But you acknowledge that there’s a functional aspect of the language, not purely the definitive one you seem to draw the line around.”

“You need *both*!” She fumed, her face turning a third shade of red he had seen on her that day.

“Agreed.” Cale nodded with finality.

The word hung in the air in much the way that a can of soup wouldn't.

It took a few moments for them to realize what had happened.

Cale’s mouth fell open slightly, and Astrid straightened herself, looking everywhere that wasn't at him. Her face slowly returned to a standard pale color.

“Did we just… agree?” *Cale* was now the one left befuddled. He had honestly thrown the card on the table, with no expectation that it would carry value.

“It all seems rather pedantic in retrospect.” Astrid offered.

Both of their gazes turned to their hands, which were still clasped with intertwined fingers.

“I’m going to miss that one. Seven years…” Cale said, his eyes drifted back up to Astrid’s.

She looked thoughtful as they continued walking their path towards anywhere.

“If you really think about it, though,” she began before pausing for further thought, “Cereal would be more of a stew than a soup.”

She was keeping it unresolved, just for him.

His grin returned, somehow discovering a whole new reason why he loved this woman.

Cale found any angle to engage the sign of good faith, “Isn’t a stew more about the stewing process? I hope you aren’t letting your cereal soak long enough to be considered stewed.”

Astrid's eyes rolled. “Apparently, Mr. Director here also has a depth of knowledge on categorical culinary nuance.”

She smiled, though, and Cale mirrored it, eyes briefly meeting over their held hands.

They continued to walk, immersed in an uncharacteristic silence.

Cale’s mind returned to planning.

What did he need to call this a success? Could he define an attainable objective at this point?

He didn't feel like the objective had been accomplished, though several key results had definitely been achieved.

What was missing?

He needed her to vocally admit an interest.

The hand-holding and pleasantries were nice, even excellent, but he concluded it wasn’t the commitment he needed. This game would not be over until *she* ended it.

Could he just tell her he loved her?

She clearly already knew... An admission itself would be more of a middle finger to the rules than a proper play. There wasn't enough precedent on ignoring the rules to start now...

“Where are we going?” He finally asked.

“I have no idea, I thought *you* were leading.” She responded.

Given the context of the day, it was a fair assumption, he supposed.

The two looked up, taking in the towering steel coaster that stood beside them. The sign read “The Breaking Point”, describing itself as the world’s tallest and longest dive coaster. Cale had no clue what that meant.

“Really? The Breaking Point? You *are* full of surprises today.” Astrid dragged him into line before he could object.

Now, Cale's confidence was not entirely built on the capability to *predict*.

It was built on the capability to *react*.

He was *great* at reacting. He had spent many years perfecting it. He knew things would go wrong; that was OK.

His life was then just a constant battle of risk analysis: right-size the level of preparation to ensure the margin of error was within acceptable parameters for whatever the topic might be, then have —or pretend to have —the confidence to "wing it" when Murphy's Law kicked in.

His not-relationship with Astrid had a meticulously calculated 0% risk tolerance.

By the transitive property, "living", which from every angle he could consider was a dependency for "his not-relationship with Astrid", similarly had a 0% risk tolerance.

Being strapped to a metal cart, supported by steel pillars which were assembled and maintained by the most error-prone of creatures: "humans", would, in fact, give him quite a bit to react to. It, however, sat at a slightly higher than 0% risk, according to his analysis.

Cale's smile faltered.

“So when it says dive coaster… this isn’t underwater or something, is it?” He asked nervously.

“No, idiot. It means it has a ninety-degree drop.” Her voice pulsed with enthusiasm.

There was no backing out here.

*Shit*.

“That sounds… pleasant?” He smiled weakly, attempting something more substantial to mask his concerns, but failing miserably.

“This one has big, wide, arching loops that give you the sense of being suspended in mid-air. The whole thing makes you question whether you’re actually strapped in or not.”

She was clearly excited for this.

“Yay?”

She turned to him, head cocked, finally picking up on his dread, “Wait, how many coasters have you ridden?”

“Including wooden ones?” He asked.

“Yes.”

Cale didn’t think they were lame. They were predictable.

He pretended to think, nodding and bobbing his head slightly from side to side. “Well, then it would probably add up to a grand total of... oh, I don't know... zero.”

A gap formed ahead of them as Astrid’s hand went to her mouth to hide a snicker.

Her eyes flashed momentarily with some realization, but it faded immediately back to amusement.

What in the world was that?

She then remembered that she didn’t care about propriety with him and burst out laughing.

Cale wasn't one to blush or shrink.

Well, that's what he told himself while he proceeded to blush and shrink. His hand moved to the back of his neck.

“I don’t do amusement parks often.” He admitted as they finally stepped ahead to catch up with the rest of the line.

She raised an eyebrow, “Do you want to go ride a kiddie coaster to get your bearings first? This is going to be intense.”

He contemplated the extended lifeline she offered, but, for reasons he was sure were tied to some piece of logic he couldn't even comprehend, decided it was more essential to poke some fun, “You mean intense like the great spork debacle of 2020, or intense like the camping trip?”

Was he really going to risk their lives for the sake of seeing her get slightly flustered?

Apparently, yes, he was.

“It was the *fork* debacle of 2020, and you know it," Astrid snapped.

He could see the fumes begin to build up, but she caught herself, cycling a deep breath.

*Damn*.

She continued, "But ‘intense’ like I’m concerned about the health of your heart. I’d ask about how your dietary nutrition is, but based on your reaction to losing that silly fighting game to May the other month, I’m assuming your sodium levels are rather high.”

Astrid smiled with pride at the insult.

It *had* been good.

Cale squeezed her hand, “Astrid! Did you just call me salty? I’m proud my gaming lingo is rubbing off on you. Now if only you’d listen to some sense on that collection of weeds you keep.”

And *there* were the fumes.

Cale's grin widened as her face reddened. Cale felt her hand tighten around his, and her free hand shot down in a clenched fist.

“Indoor gardening is a valid hobby! And…" She paused and re-centered herself mid-sentence, "You know what, no. You lost your chance to work your way up. I hope you scream to death.”

*Can you scream to death? That’s not a real thing, right?*

He held the question back as they shuffled forward again.

Cale checked his phone as an excuse to look away and keep his nerves calm.

Of course, finding the drafted message staring back at him, as well as the clock indicating he had less than two hours left, had quite the adverse effect.

The phone returned to the pocket, though a thread of his brain remained attached to it.

The rest of his attention returned to Astrid.

Cale expected her to keep pushing in an attempt to build his anxiety. Instead, he found her thumb rubbing lightly along his forefinger, as if in an effort to soothe him.

It most certainly helped.

Fear was momentarily pushed back by jolts of electricity that shot up through his arm as his own thumb rubbed back. The subtle movement of her thumb reacting playfully to his *almost* made the rest of the world vanish.

This continued for minutes.

Cale stole several glances at her, but she was inclined to keep staring ahead, maintaining a soft smile.

They came to the front of the line, and Cale eyed the elevated seats as anxiety began to win through. “Those are well tested, right?”

Astrid reluctantly let go of his hand so they could step into the next car that arrived. Their fingertips lingered.

She, of course, had made them line up for the very front.

“Sometimes. This one probably is. It runs thousands of times a day.”

“I guess that’s fair.” His anxiety was relieved somewhat. There was a logical comfort in statistics, even if not emotional comfort.

It was challenging to see Astrid from around the shoulder harness. His hand managed to find hers again, and the slow and passive game of thumb war resumed.

“Of course,” She continued, and he felt her hand squeeze his, “There are always a handful of deaths every year on coasters. Sometimes they just detach completely from the tracks. But statistically, it’s safer than crossing the street. That’s comforting, right?”

He could picture her grin, and he moved his hand back up to hold the bar on the harness as the ride lurched forward, beginning its long ascent.

“You’re making that up.” His voice betrayed uncertainty.

They couldn’t die here, right?

That was preposterous.

It would be an ironic end… all of his worrying and buildup over the years just to end up being a statistic in a headline. That potential irony unsettled him; his heart pounded.

“No, really, granted it’s practically always small carnivals that don’t have quite the same class of inspection. But every now and then something goes wrong at big parks like this.”

The joy in her voice was building; he wasn’t sure if it was from the teasing or the ride. Likely both.

The game was forgotten.

Cale's heart pounded.

It screamed.

It raged, holding the rest of his organs hostage, demanding to escape.

*This was most definitely not this high up when we looked at it from the ground.*

“Astrid?" he begged.

She was comforting him, right?

She hesitated, then offered, “Actually, most of the accidents are at the top of the first drop. It’s a very complex portion of the coaster.”
