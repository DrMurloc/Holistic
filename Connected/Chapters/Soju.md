**Leo**

Flashing lights strobed, techno blared, and the air thrummed with electric energy.

Well, Leo was sure it did.

He didn't notice.

All of it simply... bounced off of him.

He watched as Flask, Sheetrock, Bev, and Bee hopped across the pads one after another. After each pass, they would run back to the other side and repeat the motion.

It was the purest form of community bonding: co-ops. Charts designed for multiple people to play at once. When the line got long, they became common to keep everyone's legs moving.

The patterns shifted, and all four found themselves on the pads simultaneously, hopping alternately on their respective arrows.

They ate greats and broke combo repetitively. But it didn't matter; very few people cared about the score on such charts. The game was for fun, after all.

Right?

That was the point of games.

Fun.

Leo's eyes drifted across the large waiting crowd to May. His brow furrowed, and his shoulders stiffened.

He couldn't quite describe how he knew, but she was in a bad headspace. It ripped his heart apart when she got like this. It was like the flame that typically kept her inflated with a heated, mocking determination had simply... been turned off.

This was "fun".

They had their routine, their contract, their ritual... They were *happy*.

This was what happiness looked like, right?

Fuck.

He was doing this to her.

He agreed to these stupid rules; he should have pushed back on them or set them up differently. But he couldn't just... ask May to change them. They were there for a reason.

The song wound down, and May stepped forward to take her turn.

She paused, turning her head to meet Leo's gaze.

He averted his eyes. He shouldn't have been looking in the first place. It would be considered affectionate.

May didn't take her turn; instead, she took a seat on one of the racing games nearby, resting her head forward with fingers on her temple.

She needed support on days like this.

He knew that.

Leo wanted to run to her, to tell her how amazing she was.

But he couldn't.

"Mom and Dad still fighting?" Leo heard Sneez's voice come from behind as three other players took their place on the pads.

Leo shook his head. "It's... complicated."

"That do be how it be sometimes. Anything I can help with?"

Leo loved that so many in the community would offer help, but he himself would never accept it. It felt... wrong. He had never been able to quite place his finger on why.

He played the support role, anyway.

Sort of defeated the purpose when your healer needed healing.

"No, we'll figure it out. I appreciate you, though." Leo turned and gave what was likely a pretty lame smile.

He found a hand on his shoulder. "We're here for you two. Come on, K BBQ."

May was approached by Shino, likely receiving the same invite. After a quick headcount, Leo concluded this would likely be the largest gathering the community had seen outside of events for some time.

Yeah.

It would be nice to see so many in one place.

"I'll buy a round. I'm in." Leo nodded, a slight smile forming.

"There he fucking is!" Sneez smacked Leo on the back before strolling off and yelling to the gathering players, "We got a round on Lion!"

Leo's eyes bounced across the crowd as they emitted a collective, yet disorganized, cheer.

A smile, well out of his control, inched to the corners of his mouth as he inhaled his family's energy.

Apparently, May hadn't been the only thing he had been distancing himself from. Leo had been so hyper-focused on his relationship with May that he had forgotten his fulfillment came from multiple sources.

As he merged himself into the mob of sweaty misfits moving towards the arcade exit, he let himself crowd surf on the waves of positive vibes.

For just a moment, he wasn't Leo, he was part of something bigger. Something that he had helped create.

It was only a moment.

As the crowd began to bottleneck and file out of the front door, Leo looked back to check on May, who walked separately from the group.

He let his smile grow beyond "slight".

It caused her to pause, and for a moment, their eyes met.

Leo begged the universe to allow her a single smile; she needed it, she needed *him*.

She didn't smile.

She turned away and began to walk back to the game.

The moment filled Leo with... anger? Frustration? Pain? Motivation of some sort, in any case.

He would figure out what it was later.

If Leo was allowed to be happy, so was May.

Fuck the rules.

Just hit the arrows when they get to the top.

He stepped out of the group and grabbed May's hand.

**May**

May's heart went into... it raced with... No, it didn't.

May's heart beat. Barely.

She stared at Leo's hand, which held hers firmly.

Her mouth opened to protest.

Nothing came out.

There was nothing to come out.

She was empty.

The others had all stopped in their track at the moment, and all conversations had gone silent. The murmurings of nearby fighting games were the only thing keeping the moment anchored in real time, though the sounds slid off May like rain against a window.

Was she supposed to be mad?

Happy?

What was she supposed to feel right now?

Regardless, she just... didn't.

Leo's hand twisted slightly; he was going to pull away.

No.

She didn't know what she wanted, but she did know she needed him. The rules were dumb and made up anyway.

Her grip tightened, though she didn't meet his eyes. She couldn't look at him. Not while she was in the process of pulling him right fucking back into the cycle.

This was how it happened.

Every time.

It was her fault.

Like a fire drill, the rest of the group began to file out in double time. Conversations slowly resumed.

This was selfish.

She was selfish.

She was a fucking manipulator just like Cale. She makes up rules, then only follows them when it's convenient for her. Leo at least seemed to care about the nuance.

Leo fell in step next to her without a word as they trailed behind the rest of the group.

His grip provided a level of stability that no dance game bar could ever hope to achieve. A lone shining beacon in the center of a forest of fog and silence.

Leo didn't say anything the entire walk.

How was he so fucking good at knowing exactly what to say and what not to say? He knew that any word that escaped his mouth before May herself opened the conversation would just cause her to lose it.

He had known precisely when to start pulling away to make her latch on and help stabilize.

Why couldn't May be strong like that?

She just fucking said whatever came into her head.

Because she was pitiful, an absolute wretch.

It wasn't long before the two found themselves sitting at a large, circular table. The group needed to be split across three such tables. Black grills began heating at their centers.

The comforting smell of umami already filled the restaurant.

The first round of soju was dropped off—a battalion of green bottles circling the grills—accompanied by the steady clinks of shot glasses being placed.

May's gaze remained on the table.

"I'm sorry," she stated.

"May, you did nothing-"

"The hell I fucking didn't." May couldn't bring herself to look at him. He would be giving her an empathetic look. Because he cared. He always cared.

Leo was strong like that.

And May was weak.

"What did you do then? What are you apologizing for?" Leo asked.

May's mouth opened to answer, but once again, no words came. They were there... it was just like... their engines failed to ignite.

What the fuck was wrong with her?

Leo poured their first shots, and the table collectively toasted to The Pride.

May simply stared at her shot glass.

There was no pride here.

She threw the shot back.

Her mind barely registered conversations around the table about song difficulties and upcoming tournaments and events. Most of the conversations were repeats. What would the point be?

"May."

Leo's voice grabbed her back. What *was* she apologizing for?

"I don't know. Getting mad?" May finally turned and met Leo's gaze.

That dumb smile.

"May, I think getting mad is part of being human," Leo stated.

May shook her head, pouring the next round of shots as an excuse to avert her eyes back down.

She wasn't worth that smile.

"You don't get mad," she asserted.

Leo didn't have a response.

So they drank.

The air filled with a crisp sizzle as the first order of meat was placed on the grill.

"It's just..." The warm buzz of alcohol had already begun to hit her, thinning the fog enough to let thoughts evolve into words. "Leo, I spend... I spend all fucking day listening to relatives tell me how unsuccessful I am. How much *greater* I *could* be doing."

"Your aunt came by?"

"She's trying to get me a collegiate-level position still."

"Why does she just refer to me as 'white boy'?"

She could hear the smile at the edges of his mouth.

"She's technically not wrong." May shrugged, feeling, but not quite showing, her own smile.

Leo poured the third round.

May continued, "And then I have you. Who takes pride in... well... every little fucking thing I do. You treat me like a *queen*."

"It's because you are," Leo said.

May laughed; it felt *great*.

She *almost* believed him; he was always right.

They drank.

"But why? How can you say that?" she continued, still avoiding his eyes.

"Quite easily, with my mouth," Leo noted.

May finally let the smile show. "You know-"

"I know. I just wanted to see you smile."

She couldn't look up; looking up implied she deserved him. That she deserved the people at this table. That she wasn't simply a leech that took and never gave.

Leo broke convention and poured a second time in a row, the fourth round.

"You didn't answer," May noted.

"I didn't," Leo agreed, but immediately continued, "That doesn't mean I don't have an answer. It means there's too much of an answer."

May stared into her soju, not willing to accept the non-answer.

Leo continued, sensing her hesitation, "You protect me from me, May. You're my tank, you set the pace, which is something I always sucked at. Without you, I either pull too much and crash and burn or don't move at all."

Was that really how he saw her bursts of "passionate" assertions? The endless hypocritical insistence that he was both not doing enough and doing too much? She was guard rails?

May finally looked up.

She knew she would find no deception in Leo's face. He never lied. When he said something, he meant it. Whether or not it was true, he truly believed she was a positive contribution to his life.

They drank, and May immediately poured the fifth round as the server began cutting and distributing meat.

"I missed you," May stated.

"We saw each other yesterday," Leo noted.

"We didn't really see each other."

They drank.

"I missed you, too," Leo agreed.

The smile they exchanged would most definitely be categorized as "affectionate", and it pushed back another layer of fog.

Vague sounds of voices began to seep slowly into her alcohol-distorted awareness.

May tilted her head back and inhaled the smell of garlic and onion, reinvigorating her veins like a sponge.

She absent-mindedly began to redistribute their meat, trading more fatty pieces from Leo's plate to her own.

As they began to eat, she found her hand scratching Leo's back.

He was tense, too, and she knew it. He was always tense. He was just good at hiding it when she was like this.

The satisfaction from feeling his back muscles soften under her nails was a greater hit of ecstasy than any full combo could provide.

Another deep inhale, and her vision remembered that colors were a thing as the chatter of their friends came into focus.

May was fine.

She was good.

She could do this.

She had Leo. And Leo was strong for both of them.

"Ok, but Heart Rabbit is literally free," she heard from Lightning as her mind latched onto the conversation.

"Then why haven't you passed it?" Matt, whose game tag was in fact, just "Matt", added.

"Fucking skill issue," May interjected with an only-slightly-forced cackle. Leo squeezed her thigh as the routine round of arguments commenced.

Shots six, seven, and eight went down over the debate.

As shot nine was drunk over paid checks, someone said the word "Karaoke".

And it was all over.

**Leo**

Leo's brain...

Leo's brain... brained.

He had always been a bit of a lightweight, and by his poor soju math, his blood was likely sixteen percent alcohol at this point. Or .16 percent. There was a decimal point in there somewhere.

He and May stumbled through the streets behind the group, supporting one another with each awkward step.

Soft city lights reflected off of puddles left from a brief drizzle that had passed while they ate.

May was doing better.

And Leo loved that.

"What I'm saying is... fucking... Rivals... have a notification board where... when a rival tops you..." She paused, taking a moment to laugh at the words 'tops you'. "But, I want... to go on and see when... when... Matt takes my country bests back."

Leo contemplated, sifting through his mental backlog of ideas.

The sifting was slow, the files stuck together with the sticky residue of alcohol.

"Oh, I have like a whole thing, actually. There's a branch for that. I think... Did I delete that?" Leo squinted his eyes, attempting to remember where he had left that project.

"Clearly, you don't need me; you have everything figured out." May bumped shoulders with Leo.

He laughed. "You know, for a fact, I need you."

"Really? Even after everything? I'm so *mean* to you."

"We went over this, you're not mean. And I'm... I'm non... non-functional without you; I... spiral. Cale says-"

May spat, "Fuck Cale."

Yup.

May was back.

Leo bumped her shoulder back. "May, he's my best friend."

"You know he almost left?"

Leo nodded, "Yeah."

"He's a dick."

Leo's brain spent a full two seconds processing how much he should say.

He then proceeded to discard the results of the contemplation and continued to say whatever happened to pop into his head.

"He's going to propose."

May stopped, forcing Leo to halt as well.

"What?"

Leo tugged on her arm, getting them back into step. "He asked for my permission."

May scoffed. "He didn't ask for *my* permission. That... did I use douchenozzle yet? I think I did. That... butt... bucket." She paused. "Got 'em."

Leo laughed. "You would have blackmailed him with it."

"*Absolutely*, I would. And will. I can... probably... get that Atari off of him with this..."

Leo laughed again, knowing full well she intended to follow through on that.

He should probably feel bad about letting the surprise slip.

He would have time to feel bad in the morning.

Right now, he had May.

And that was simple.

"I love you," he stated.

His brain failed to initiate the proper bureaucracy to make such an assertion.

"Hey! No affection!" May pushed him lightly, causing them both to laugh.

"No! It's robot love. I'm not human, we established, remember? I don't get mad? No affection, I swear!"

Leo looked up in time to see her shaking her head. "You're not a robot. You... you just... get people... *I'm* the robot. I just... shit goes in my head, then it comes out."

"That's not true. And even... even if it were... The shit that you say with no thought is... like... so much smarter than shit I say with... all thought..." Leo felt that sentence had escaped him. Oh well.

May appeared to still understand. "I made us... I made us make these stupid rules."

"*We* made them," Leo corrected.

May growled, holding Leo up while he stumbled on a small pothole. "Rules are fucking dumb."

"Yeah. They made so much sense at the time."

May laughed. "No, they didn't! We just... we fucking... we listed off all the things that made us happy, then pretended like... like... not doing those things would make us... happy."

Leo stumbled to a stop, staring at a particularly deep puddle.

"What? They didn't make sense to you? Why did you agree?"

May grabbed his hand and dragged him back into motion.

"I don't... fucking know. I don't know why I... do anything. Never do," May stated.

There was something in that statement that Leo's brain attempted to brain around. It failed to get traction and just fell limply to the ground.

He would think about it when he was sober.

Minutes later, they were surrounded by strobing lights, laughter, music, and arguments. It all blended into a roaring cacophony of sensory overload.

Raiah was singing, shocking the group with the refinement and clarity of her voice. However, she was clearly struggling to stand up straight. The girl had been trying to keep up with Murk, who had a significant weight advantage over her.

More soju flowed.

Drinks ten, eleven, and twelve followed.

Leo only loosely registered his attempt to perform a Mimi Galaxy dance, his voice slurred through what little Japanese he knew for the song. May laughed in the corner, and he noted that he needed to match drinks thirteen and fourteen.

May and Bee sang The Black Parade, which the whole room joined in on.

The lights began to blur together.

Leo was laughing, though he was unsure at what.

The fifteenth shot went down.

Wait, sixteenth? Fourteenth?

Leo's brain was struggling to do Soju math, but he was pretty sure they were well past the point of "should Uber home."

He was unsure at what point May ended up in his lap.

He was unsure at what point their lips locked as they intertwined like serpents.

He was unsure who yelled, "Get a room!"

He was unsure how long they stood there waiting for their ride, pushing the boundaries of what was legally allowed in public spaces before crossing into lewd or disorderly conduct.

He was unsure how long the ride was, staring across the back seat and into those hungry brown eyes.

He was unsure what to categorize the noise that escaped his lips as May's teeth clamped down on his collarbone the moment they entered his room.

What he *was* sure of was that when he woke up the next morning, his body ached in *all* the right ways.

His head pounded, begging for long-overdue hydration. The hangover, however, barely even registered as he watched the steady rise and fall of May's chest next to him, bathed in the light of dawn.

Leo was sure she would be worse today. She always was the day after drinking.

He was sure he'd be there for her.

He was unsure what came with tomorrow.

He was unsure what came with the days after. Those sunrises only brought unfinished disputes and assertions that he still had no answers for.

But he was *most* certainly sure that right now, he felt complete again.
