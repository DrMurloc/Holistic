**Lara**

A wide, still river expanded out along the path that Lara paced.

Sections were frozen, mirroring the motionless air and mostly empty streets of the expansive city.

Breath coalesced into mist.

Her puffy, blue borrowed jacket enveloped her in a cozy warmth. Lara supposed it was her jacket now, offered to her along with several other pieces of clothing that her Mom hadn't wanted to bring back to the island.

One mitten remained off, hand stuffed in a pocket, just in case Sho messaged.

It was late for him —too late to be messaging —but the hope was enough for Lara.

The trees lining the path were bare, and very few others braved the brisk December air. It was quite the contrast to the last time she had been in DC. Sakura blossoms had dominated the landscape, attracting hordes of visitors.

Mom and Sho.

The more she detangled the tangle of Mom, the more she felt the tangle with Sho was tightening.

Why hadn't he asked to come out?

She would never expect him to... of course. But... He should have asked? Did he not care?

She shoved the thought out of her mind.

Sho wasn't a tangle.

Sho was a knot.

Was it neatly tied?

His communications had been sparse... was he losing interest?

Lara growled, picked up a rock, and threw it into the river. It skipped a few times before sinking into the ripples.

The thoughts were wrong. She logically knew it. But it didn't make the emotions any less real.

Sho did the spiraling thing too; she knew it. The thought of him alone hurt.

Long distance sucked.

She paced through the series of monuments and memorials. Honor for the lost, the events of the past cemented in marble, concrete, and stone. They were so... permanent, comforting.

When did permanent become comforting to Lara?

Did Sho instill that in her, or was it what she was out looking for in the first place?

She stopped, her eyes landing on a curled-up ball of fur perched on the long obsidian wall beside her.

A cat?

In the winter?

It was sunbathing, which made sense, but it was still an unusual sight.

Its head lifted and turned, watching Lara from above the names of the fallen.

Lara smiled.

She wasn't quite tall enough to reach up and pet it, but something about its presence helped fight off the encroaching chill.

Her phone buzzed, and her bare hand flew out of her pocket to navigate her messaging app almost instantly.

*"How are you doing?"*

Sho messaged at three AM? He wasn't really the type to stay up late, except with Lara...

Had he found someone...

No.

Lara kicked the thought overboard. She trusted him; it was one of the reasons she fell for him.

But then why text now?

Was he lying in his bed with his own anxious thoughts?

Did a concert keep him out late, and only now did he get a chance to respond?

He hadn't picked up earlier in the day, which Lara did her best not to overthink. Her best at that was not very good.

Lara cut through any further thoughts, responding, *"I would be better if you were here."*

Wait, would he think that was her pressuring him to come out?

If they were in-person, he would be able to read her and know it was merely another way for her to say "I miss you", but she couldn't convey that tone in text...

The three dots indicated that Sho was typing back.

He took his time typing, and the dots disappeared several times. Lara watched with bated breath.

After *all* that typing, the response was simply, *"I know. I'm sorry."*

Lara growled, almost throwing her phone. She wanted to scream and smash the damn device to pieces, incinerate them, and feed the ash to the worms.

This device used to be her point of connection to the boy she loved; it was their lifeline, the mechanism that enabled them to communicate.

And now all it did was let through hollow, contextless words that could mean anything that Lara's anxious mind could make up.

This wasn't communication.

Communication... was... messy.

It was... organic, unstructured...

This was a pitiful shadow of the meaningful exchange of emotions and life that came with a real-world human connection.

As if Sho read her mind all the way across the world, the phone rang.

"Sho!" Lara exclaimed, beginning a steady circular pace under the watchful gaze of her feline friend.

"Lara, sorry. I wanted to call earlier. I was busy." He responded in Japanese, meaning he was likely alone.

And he wasn't putting effort into keeping his words from slurring. It didn't bother Lara; she loved his voice. It either meant he was exhausted or that he had just stopped caring whether she heard. She hoped it was the latter.

The observation wasn't enough to keep her excitement from bubbling up and spilling out of her mouth.

"No! It's ok! It was a long day. My Mom spent all day trying to explain to me that I'm basically selling my body online. She doesn't talk about Dad, which is great, but sometimes she just doesn't get it. She doesn't understand the concept of the 'V' in V-tuber. She thinks it stands for something else. But it's... oh... wait, sorry, I missed you."

"No! Don't apologize! I like your voice. I think about it all day. I missed you, too."

The warmth that spread through Lara made her question if she had *too* many layers on.

"Are... are you ok? You were texting less," Lara asked.

There was a pause. "Yes. It's good. Everything is going to be good."

What did that mean?

Before Lara could ask, Sho continued, "Where are you in two weeks?"

Lara checked the date before responding. "Oh! There's an event. Gamfest. There are arcade games and board games... The cosplay is supposed to be very high quality, and I'm really excited to get photos. You would love it! It's like the perfect combination of modern subcultures. I can't wait to see it! I-"

She forced herself to pause, giving Sho a moment to get a word in.

"Oh."

Oh?

He sounded disappointed. Or was that just tired? Even with voice, Lara was struggling to read him.

Did he not want her to come back? Did-

"Why didn't you ask to come out? Why didn't you ask me to stay?" She let the words fly out of her mouth.

The sunbathing cat licked at its paw, though its eyes remained level on Lara.

"What?" Sho finally asked.

"I... I've been spiraling on this for the last month. I never wanted to ask because I was worried it would make you feel pressured."

"I was trying to be respectful. Was I not? You do not like it when your Mom and Dad request things of you."

He... it... was a good point.

Why did it feel different with Sho?

"And," Sho continued, "I told you. I trust you. You make good decisions."

That was why it felt different with Sho.

Lara resumed her pacing, and her mittened hand ran along a linked chain railing. She could feel the cat's gaze following.

"I think it's different for you. I *want* you to put pressure on my decisions. That's the difference between you and any other boy I've dated. And I hope you feel comfortable asking me for big decisions."

There was a long pause. He was likely processing the assertion.

"So we *are* dating?" He finally asked.

"Well, I just used the word. I don't... like labels. They're... messy... and over-prescribe behavior," Lara paused, as her mind caught up with her response. Her voice went rapid-fire to try and get it all out. "Oh, but if they matter to you, you can call it dating! Societally, it's weird: different cultures have different expectations and definitions of when the label applies. Some people like unspoken labels; some people need them. I think it's the hardest part of relationships, it's like playing a two-person game when you don't even know what type of game the other person wants to play."

Lara paused, coming to a stop in front of the cat again.

"That seems to be a theme with society," Sho laughed.

Lara paused. The way he said that... that kind of thought used to stress him out.

The thought brought a broad smile to her face, a warmth that could heat the entire city.

Her mouth worked faster than her brain. "Actually, maybe I'll come back for just a week. Mom will understand, I-"

"No!" Sho said sharply.

The smile vanished.

It felt like the cold cut through every piece of barrier that Lara wore, creeping in and grasping her by her bones. The air froze her lungs.

No?

Lara stared at her phone as if Sho could see the incredulity that dominated her face.

"No?" She finally asked.

"What I mean is, I searched for the event you mentioned. It looks fun. The tickets are non-refundable and expensive. Don't miss that."

Lara supposed that made sense.

"It has rhythm game tournaments! You would have fun there," Lara stated. "Wait, not that I'm saying you should come here! Although I would love it if you did. But..."

Sho didn't immediately respond. "I'll look into it."

"No! Sho, you would have to get a passport, which is complicated and takes longer than two weeks. I'll be fine! I swear." Lara lied.

She had never felt so lonely in her own bed in her life. It was only another month or so, though.

They were almost there.

Sho paused again, then responded, "I need to go. I just wanted to hear your voice before sleep. It was a long day. A long week. It's been a long month. I miss you."

"A long life. I miss you too," Lara sighed.

"I love you."

The word caused the battery powering Lara's heart to fill to bursting. The cold receded, and her lungs thawed.

"I love you," she returned, finally finding a smile that had been missing all day.

Sho hung up, and Lara returned her hand to the pocket, still without a mitten.

Once she returned to Sho, there would be nothing holding them back. This was merely an obligatory maintenance stop for Lara, and she knew it. They would figure things out; she had faith.

Lara continued her walk through the monuments under the faithful protection of her black cat.

**Now, some of you might have picked up on a tiny bit of deflection that Sho had masterfully executed earlier. You might be thinking such a thing is** ***slightly*** **uncharacteristic of our anime-loving and honest friend.**

**Others might be asking, "Wait, what happened to the perspective shift gimmick? What's going through Sho's mind?"**

**If my personal guess is accurate...**

**Well..**

**Sho**

Sho stood in front of his house, and he slowly lowered his hand to his side, grasping the phone tightly as if he could hug Lara through it.

A large suitcase sat beside him, but the narrow street was otherwise empty and nearly pitch-black.

He corrected himself.

Not his house.

Not anymore.

The "Property for sale" sign had been promptly taken down a week earlier. He had put a few items with sentimental value into storage to figure out later, but everything else had been sold or fit into his suitcase.

He should cry, right? Bow? Say some parting thoughts?

He grabbed the suitcase and turned down the street. The handle vibrated against his palm as the wheels rumbled against the ground.

There was still a lot to do. He had a hotel to check into tonight and a resignation request to draft. Then, there were two weeks of finalizing a few travel details.

Then he would go home.
