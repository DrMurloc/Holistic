## Tree (July)

**Naina**

Naina had begun to suspect that Zoey was *neither* a hyperfixation *nor* a special interest.

The summer blaze had decided to give everyone a breather for a day, and she found herself on a hill with her head in Zoey's lap, reading a fic that had kept her attention almost the entire month. Zoey’s white sundress pooled beneath Naina’s temple as a tiny pillow. The cloth was rough in just the right way.

A large willow tree cast its shadow down over them. Zoey’s back leaned against it while she drifted her fingers along several strands of Naina's hair–a more recent addition to Zoey's stimming repertoire. She seemed to be lost in a daydream, gazing out over the mostly-empty campus.

The realization came with inconvenience.

It was a new frontier: romantic interest. It was a feeling that Naina was cautiously studying and exploring. From what she could tell so far, on the scale of "light crush" to "ready to send armies to die in her name", Naina was located somewhere in the "would leave behind a ghost built purely of unresolved emotions" region.

Zoey sighed, and an absent-minded thumb briefly traced the edge of Naina's ear.

If only Naina could figure out if Zoey felt the same way; she was very hard to read. But Naina wasn't even sure if Zoey was into girls, or boys, for that matter.

The spectrum of sexuality and romance was comparably complex to the spectrum of neurodivergence. Naina had even thought herself to be aromantic.

Apparently, she was not.

Apparently, a single encounter in a church retreat with a quiet boy named David, who she never talked to again, had *not* been a sufficient sample size to draw such conclusions.

Which, like, in *hindsight*, felt obvious now.

Naina wished she could predict other things that would feel obvious in hindsight. She supposed then it wouldn’t be hindsight. Foresight? No, more like nowsight. Is that just… sight? What else was she overlooking? It was probably a lot.

Out of the corner of her vision, Naina caught Zoey glancing down briefly, with a smile and another sigh. Naina's heart fluttered as Zoey's stomach rose.

Maybe Zoey was flirting?

It was just so difficult, with Zoey being... well... Zoey - charm and affection incarnate.

There were just a lot of things she did that Naina didn't understand. And honestly, Naina had never been good at identifying flirting. Zoey had, at this point, tackled no fewer than four boys who, according to Zoey, had flirted with Naina.

Zoey's hand shifted, and the tips of her fingers rubbed Naina's scalp lightly. It felt very nice. Zoey was so considerate: a very good friend.

That’s why Naina was content. Even *if* Zoey wasn't interested, what they had was good and simple. And simple was good. Naina liked simple, and time with Zoey had been simple.

"I suppose your top-secret missions continue even when on summer break." Zoey looked down at the phone Naina was buried in.

Well, simple in some sense of the word.

Naina lowered the phone. "What?"

"You're always reading, and you're so secretive about it." Zoey lowered her head to see the screen, causing several strands of hair to cover Naina's face.

There was no way Zoey hadn't figured it out, right? Hadn't she read over her shoulder several times? Had she not understood what she saw?

"It's... uh..." Naina felt embarrassed even contemplating saying the word out loud. Zoey looked away from the phone, meeting Naina's eyes. *Hazel.* Zoey softly smiled.

Naina could say it, right?

There wasn't really a stigma anymore. Right?

A significant number of well-known authors admitted to writing it. It was barely even a subculture now.

Right?

Zoey wouldn't judge, regardless.

That thought gave Naina the confidence she needed. The word escaped, both virtually silent, but still too loud.

"Fanfiction."

Her body shrank slightly at the admission.

Zoey looked deep into Naina's eyes with just the most delicate, understanding and knowing smile and responded in the most non-judgmental way.

"What's that?"

Naina blinked in confusion. "What do you mean, 'what's that'?"

She sat up, placed her back against the tree, and tried hard not to think about ants.

"Are you using psychiatrist mind games on me?" Zoey's head tilted.

"Psychiatrists don't... never mind."

Naina contemplated how best to explain fan fiction to someone who had never heard of it. She wasn't quite sure how, in all of her hopeless romantic movie daydreaming and idealization, and well... *friend-shipping*... Zoey had never run into fanfic before. But Naina supposed everyone had to learn from somewhere.

Zoey leaned her head against Naina's shoulder while Naina thought, staring off into the campus.

Naina found an angle. "It's like... You know how I get fixations?"

"They're cute. You're cute. I like you," Zoey said, nodding against Naina's shoulder.

Naina contemplated further. "Well, what do you do if you finish a movie and you still have a fixation on the characters or the plot?"

"I make Cale watch it," Zoey said matter-of-factly, earning a laugh from Naina.

Naina reduced the scope. "Ok, well, what do people who don't have an incredible cousin do?"

"Make a friend in the dorm lounge and get them to watch it," Zoey said, once again, matter-of-factly.

Maybe Zoey was the wrong audience for this angle.

"I mean... Well, maybe. But. No. People write fanfic. There's only so much official content, so when I'm left fixated and unsatisfied... I read unofficial content. Alternative universes, spin-offs, crossovers, original characters, ships, self-inserts..."

The word “smut” barely caught in Naina’s open mouth before it slipped out.

This got Zoey's attention. She leaned back and turned slightly to face Naina.

"Like pirate ships?"

Naina missed the pressure and warmth on her shoulder.

"No. Well, there was that one show with the gay pirates that had all sorts of fics written. But like…"

What *would* Zoey ship?

"Oh! That movie we watched, 'Fighting Love With Love', remember how you said the ending is dumb with the main couple actually getting married?"

Zoey nodded confidently. "They didn't know how to actually fight. I know what love fighting looks like; those two had no reason not to hate each other. He should have ended up with the cute guy in the library."

Naina typed in a few words on her phone. "Eighty stories for that ship, most of which are alternate ending scripts." She paused, "That's an unfortunate ship name…"

Zoey looked over her shoulder. "Bendover? I don't get it."

She thought about it, then her eyes went wide with laughter, evoking a round of those distinctively satisfying butterflies in Naina. She looked at the website name on Naina's phone and typed it into hers.

Naina cautiously added, "It's dangerous. There is no bottom, well… actually, there's a lot of those, but the content is endless. I hope I didn't just point you to your downfall."

Naina’s heart raced. The idea of sending Zoey a list of fics that Naina found felt distinctly... intimate.

Naina had many clues that her hyperfixation on Zoey was, in fact, something that could only be described as "head over heels infatuation". But at the thought of studying Zoey's face while she read through those fics, several new clues confirmed Naina's true feelings:

The first clue: she would be locked on Zoey's eyes the entire time, not wanting to miss a single piece of over-exaggerated reaction. The impulse to look away would be left outside, banished from the room.

The second clue: Naina would be mirroring Zoey's every emotion, as if the two were connected at the heart. A deeply empathetic link that Naina had not considered possible within herself until she had met this wonderful girl.

The third clue, a subtle but important one: Naina would watch for Zoey to be at the peak of the most juicy, sensationally dripping segment. And then Naina would kiss her, riding the intoxicating high of pure, unfiltered, and unrestrained emotions.

It was a nice image. Naina liked it. She wished there was a fic for it.

So, yes, in Naina's educated but amateur self-psychoanalysis, she had to conclude that this was not, in fact, a hyperfixation.

"What is 'Sex Pollen'?" Zoey interrupted, squinting her eyes to make sure she read that right.

Naina's mind did a soft reset, and she blushed.

She was not quite sure if the blush was prompted her daydream, or by the words "Sex Pollen" coming out of Zoey's mouth. The answer was likely both.

"That's… probably avoid that tag. Likely more… advanced?... than you're ready for. Also... for you specifically, no dead doves."

Zoey looked over, head tilted. "Why would someone kill birds?"

"I mean... " Naina enjoyed those fics more than she would ever admit. "People are unique."

Zoey looked over Naina's shoulder again, and her forehead brushed against Naina's cheek. "Wait, what have you been reading? There are like trillions of stories here."

Naina was keenly aware of the word "strawberry" as the scent of Zoey's hair wafted over.

"Well… you know how WolfMan Cometh and Bride of Sharkasaurus-rex are canonically in the same world?"

Zoey pulled her head back and nodded vigorously. "Yeah! Because they were lazy and reused sets, and officially announced it was just the same city to pretend it was intentional."

"Well, what if SharkRex and WolfMan were students going to the same high school together, and the Shark Bride was a love-sick, jealous psychopath trying to keep them apart..." Naina paused briefly. "... and they could use magic."

Naina pondered what path in life had led her down the route of saying those words out loud to a girl she had a deep crush on.

"Wait, someone took the time to write that? That's amazing! May tells me that *I'm* crazy with my ideas. *That* is simply perfect."

Naina perked up, looking into Zoey's sparkling eyes.

*Hazel*.

"Seventy-eight chapters." Naina grinned.

"Send it to me. Now," she demanded.

Naina obliged, and Zoey proceeded to get lost in her phone.

She let out an occasional snort or laugh as she plowed through the content. Naina delighted in each and every one of them.

She wanted to kiss Zoey.

And as much as Naina, in her many hours of research - purely academic, of course - had recognized that "girl friends" often touched as a mechanism for bonding, kissing appeared to be for "girlfriends".

Zoey was clearly distracted, nose inches from her screen.

Naina rechecked her contacts, as if the entry marked "Cale" would have snuck out of her phone at some point. She had stolen the number off of Zoey's phone the other day. Zoey talked about her cousin all the time; surely, he could help Naina.

She needed to try. Even if it was just to find out if Zoey was interested in girls. If she wasn't, that was fine. Naina had a friend. But she at least needed to know.

A light breeze carried away the flush on her cheeks, leaving only the charged energy of norepinephrine.

Naina put her phone away, and suddenly found Zoey's head in *her* lap.

Zoey’s brows were furrowed as her eyes darted across the screen. The pattern of them didn’t look right for reasons Naina couldn’t quite name.

“What’s wrong?” Naina asked.

“Nothing!” Zoey replaced her frown with a beaming smile.

Naina ran her fingers through her friend’s hair, and the smile slowly receded.

It took a moment, but Zoey finally said, quietly. “I just suck at reading.”

The words triggered about ten threads of pattern recognition; Naina tilted her head to see the screen. Times New Roman? Did her phone settings apply that? What phone, in the ADA-compliant year of our lord 2025, applied a serif font over the website’s font?

ADHD or dyslexia?

How to put this…

“Do you only glance at the words that don’t seem interesting and have to backtrack when you get confused, or do the shape of the words just not come naturally?” Naina asked. That might be hard for her to answer. “You’re actually interested in reading it?”

“Yes!” Zoey said quickly, and her gaze darted to the screen. Her face wilted in shame. “I’m sorry. I’m just bad at–”

Naina took the phone from her hand before she could finish. With a few taps, she switched the browser font to Comic Sans. The soft and distinctly shaped letters filled the screen

Zoey’s entire body froze as she took the phone back.

“You’re a sorceress,” Zoey whispered. Her shoulders then squirmed against Naina’s knees with a squeal, “The letters are so *cute*.”

“You’re so cute, and I want to use your lips to farm the sweet tingles of dopamine and norepinephrine like a corn field of intoxicating neurotransmitters,” Naina said. Not out loud, of course. In her head. It would be crazy to say such a thing out loud.

Zoey’s smile was enough confirmation, though.

Naina would need to find a passive way to check whether she had accommodations for dyslexia.

For now, though…

Naina leaned back and let herself simply exist. No need to smile. No need to track movement. She just watched the empty campus, her hand playing lightly with a pink strand of hair.
